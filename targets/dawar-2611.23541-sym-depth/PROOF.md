# Resolution: Symmetric Depth-3 and Depth-4 Reductions (`dawar-2611.23541-sym-depth`)

## Summary of Results

We answer the open question of Dawar, Dwivedi, Pago, and Seppelt (`arXiv:2611.23541v1`, Section 1.2) on whether matrix-symmetric algebraic circuits can be reduced to depth $3$ or $4$, and at what cost in orbit size:

1. **No polynomial- or quasipolynomial-orbit reduction to depth $3$ or $4$ (Theorem 1):** Even for $p$-families in $\mathsf{symVBP} \subseteq \mathsf{symVP}$ of degree $d(n) \le n^a$ and orbit size $\le n^3$, for $\min\{d, n/2\} \ge 3^\Delta$ ($\Delta \in \{3, 4\}$), reducing $\hom_{P_{d}, n}$ to **depth $\Delta$** requires orbit size $\ge \binom{n}{\lfloor \min\{d, n/2\}^{1/\Delta}\rfloor} = 2^{\Omega(\min\{d, n\}^{1/\Delta}\log n)}$.
2. **Sharp homogeneous depth-$4$ lower bound via no-support-drop at bounded-fan-in $\Pi$-gates (Theorem 2):** Over any field $\mathbb{K}$ of characteristic zero, let $C$ be a homogeneous matrix-symmetric circuit of degree $\le d$ ($9 \le d < n/3$, $n \ge 28$) computing $\hom_{P_d, n}$ (of degree $d - 1$) with orbit size $\le f(n)$ where $3\kappa_f(n) \le n+2$ (so support size $k \le \kappa_f(n) - 1 < n/3$). After collapsing adjacent same-type gates ($\Sigma\to\Sigma$, $\Pi\to\Pi$), collapsing zero/constant-computing gates $\Gamma_n$-equivariantly, and rigidifying (`lem:reduction`), every surviving non-constant gate computes a homogeneous polynomial of degree $\ge 1$, so every $\Pi$-gate has at most $r \le d$ non-constant children. By a pure $S_n$ group-action lemma (Lemma 1), no wire entering any $\Pi$-gate is support-changing. Hence every homogeneous depth-$\le 4$ circuit of degree $\le d < n/3$ and $3\kappa_f(n) \le n+2$ has **support depth $\le 2$**, raising the lower bound for $\hom_{P_d, n}$ to $\binom{n}{\lfloor \sqrt{d}\rfloor} = 2^{\Omega(\sqrt{d}\log n)}$.
3. **Subexponential-orbit depth-$4$ reduction for $\mathsf{symVP}$ (Theorem 3):** Over any field $\mathbb{K}$ of characteristic zero (including $\mathbb{Q}$), every family $(p_n)$ of degree $\le d(n)$ computed by matrix-symmetric circuits of orbit size $s(n) \le 2^{n^{1-\varepsilon}}$ (in particular, every $p$-family in $\mathsf{symVP}$, where $\kappa = O(1)$) is computed by a **depth-$4$ ($\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$)** matrix-symmetric circuit over $\mathbb{K}$ (under `prelims.tex` L7's unweighted `+`/`\times` convention, with every gate below the top $\Sigma$-gate homogeneous, and the whole circuit homogeneous whenever $p_n$ is homogeneous) of orbit size $\le \min\{n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}, (n!)^2\}$, which moreover has support depth $\le 2$ whenever $6\kappa\sqrt{d(n)} < n/2$ (for single patterns $\hom_{F,n}$ or $|\mathcal{F}| \le n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$ pattern sums with $d \le n^{2-\delta}$, the total circuit size is also $\le n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$, matching Theorem 2 up to $O(\kappa)$ in the exponent).
4. **Subexponential-orbit depth-$3$ reduction over $\overline{\mathbb{K}}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ (Theorem 4):** Every $(p_n)$ as in Theorem 3 admits a (non-homogeneous) **depth-$3$ ($\Sigma\Pi\Sigma$)** matrix-symmetric circuit in the standard algebraic-complexity model with weighted linear-combination $\Sigma$-gates (`[KKS16, Tavenas15]`) — equivalently **depth $4$ ($\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$ with fan-in-$2$ scalar-multiplication bottom $\times$-gates)** in `prelims.tex` L7's unweighted model — of orbit size $\le \min\{n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}, (n!)^2\}$ over $\overline{\mathbb{K}}$ (total size $n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$ for $p_n = \hom_{F,n}$) and over $\mathbb{Q}$ when $p_n \in \mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{X}_n]$ (total size $n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})} \cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{d(n)}\log d(n))}$ for $p_n = \hom_{F,n}$).

## 1. Lower Bounds and the No-Support-Drop Lemma

### Theorem 1 (Unrestricted Depth-3 and Depth-4 Lower Bounds)
Let $n \ge 20$ and consider $p_n = \hom_{P_d, n} \in \mathsf{symVBP} \subseteq \mathsf{symVP}$ (which has degree $d-1$, orbit size $\le n^3$, and depth $O(\log d)$):
- If $\min\{d, n/2\} \ge 81$ (i.e., $d \ge 81$ and $n \ge 162$), every matrix-symmetric circuit of depth $\le 4$ computing $\hom_{P_d, n}$ has orbit size at least $\binom{n}{\lfloor \min\{d, n/2\}^{1/4}\rfloor} = 2^{\Omega(\min\{d, n\}^{1/4}\log n)}$.
- If $\min\{d, n/2\} \ge 27$ (i.e., $d \ge 27$ and $n \ge 54$), every matrix-symmetric circuit of depth $\le 3$ computing $\hom_{P_d, n}$ has orbit size at least $\binom{n}{\lfloor \min\{d, n/2\}^{1/3}\rfloor} = 2^{\Omega(\min\{d, n\}^{1/3}\log n)}$.

*Proof.* By Section 2.3 of `2611.23541`, every matrix-symmetric circuit $C$ has $\mathrm{supp\text{-}depth}(C) \le \mathrm{depth}(C)$. For $\Delta \in \{3, 4\}$, set $k + 1 = \lfloor \min\{d, n/2\}^{1/\Delta}\rfloor$; since $\min\{d, n/2\} \ge 3^\Delta$, we have $k + 1 \ge 3$, so $k \ge 2$. Moreover, $4(k+1) \le 4(n/2)^{1/3} \le n$ holds for all $n \ge 54$ (as $32n \le n^3$), and $(k+1)^\Delta \le \min\{d, n/2\}$. Hence all hypotheses of Lemma `lem:path-lower` (`app-vsbr.tex`) are satisfied, yielding orbit size $\ge \binom{n}{k+1}$. $\square$

### Lemma 1 (No Support-Drop at Bounded-Fan-In $\Pi$-Gates)
Let $C'$ be a reduced matrix-symmetric circuit of support size $\le k$, with $n \ge k + 5$. Let $g$ be a $\Pi$-gate of $C'$ whose non-constant children (ignoring constant input gates, which have empty support) are $h_1, \dots, h_r$. If $r + 2k < n$, then $\sup(h_t) \subseteq \sup(g)$ for every $t \in [r]$.

*Proof.* Suppose for contradiction that there exist $t \in [r]$ and $v \in \sup_L(h_t) \setminus \sup_L(g)$ (the right-side case $\sup_R$ is identical). Let $U := [n] \setminus \sup_L(h_t)$ and $W := [n] \setminus (\sup_L(h_t) \cup \sup_L(g)) \subseteq U$. Since $n \ge k + 5$ and $r + 2k < n$, we have $|U| \ge n - k \ge 5$ and $|W| \ge n - 2k > r$.
1. For every $w \in W$, the transposition $(v\ w)$ belongs to $\operatorname{Stab}(\sup(g))$, so it fixes gate $g$. Because $C'$ is reduced (`lem:reduction`), $(v\ w)$ must permute the multiset of non-constant children $\{h_1, \dots, h_r\}$ of $g$.
2. Since $|W| > r$, by the Pigeonhole Principle there exist distinct $w_1, w_2 \in W$ such that $(v\ w_1)(h_t) = (v\ w_2)(h_t) =: \tilde{h}$.
3. Setting $\tau_1 = (v\ w_1)$ and $\tau_2 = (v\ w_2)$, we have $\tau_1(\tilde{h}) = h_t$, so the $3$-cycle $\tau_1 \tau_2 = (v\ w_2\ w_1)$ satisfies $(\tau_1 \tau_2)(h_t) = \tau_1(\tilde{h}) = h_t$.
4. The subgroup $\operatorname{Stab}(\sup(h_t))$ fixes $h_t$ and contains the full symmetric group $S_U$ (acting on $U$ of size $\ge 5$), hence in particular the alternating group $A_U$. Together with the $3$-cycle $(v\ w_2\ w_1)$, $A_U$ generates the alternating group $A_{U \cup \{v\}}$ on $U \cup \{v\}$ (indeed, conjugating $(v\ w_2\ w_1)$ by elements of $A_U$ yields every $3$-cycle involving $v$, and $A_U$ already contains all $3$-cycles within $U$). Hence $A_{U \cup \{v\}}$ fixes $h_t$.
5. For any $w \in U$, pick $w' \in U \setminus \{w\}$ (since $|U| \ge 5$). Both $(w\ w') \in S_U$ and $(v\ w)(w\ w') = (v\ w\ w') \in A_{U \cup \{v\}}$ fix $h_t$, so $(v\ w)(h_t) = (v\ w)(w\ w')(h_t) = h_t$.
6. Thus $(v\ w)$ fixes $h_t$ for every $w \in U$, implying $\operatorname{Stab}(\sup(h_t) \setminus \{v\})$ fixes $h_t$ and contradicting minimality of $\sup(h_t)$. $\square$

### Theorem 2 (Sharp Homogeneous Depth-4 Lower Bound)
Let $\mathbb{K}$ have characteristic zero, $n \ge 28$, and $9 \le d < n/3$. Let $C$ be any homogeneous matrix-symmetric circuit of degree $\le d$ and depth $\le 4$ (with all gates homogeneous of degree $\le d$) computing $\hom_{P_d, n}$ (of degree $d - 1$) with orbit size $\le f(n)$ where $3\kappa_f(n) \le n + 2$.
1. **Equivariant alternation & constant/zero-gate collapse:** First apply `lem:reduction` to obtain a reduced homogeneous circuit $C'$ of depth $\le 4$, orbit size $\le f(n)$, and gate degrees $\le d$. Collapse any adjacent gates of the same type ($\Sigma \to \Sigma$ and $\Pi \to \Pi$, preserving $\Gamma_n$-equivariance, homogeneity, degree $\le d$, and orbit size $\le f(n)$ without increasing depth) so layers strictly alternate between $\Sigma$ and $\Pi$. Then repeatedly replace every gate $h$ computing a constant $c \in \mathbb{K}$ (in particular every degree-$0$ gate and every gate computing $0$, collapsing any parent $\Pi$-gate with a $0$ child to $0$ and removing $0$-children from $\Sigma$-gates) by the constant input gate $c$, remove unreachable gates, collapse any newly adjacent same-type gates, and re-apply `lem:reduction` to obtain $C''$. Because $\pi(p_h) = p_{\pi h}$ for all $\pi \in \Gamma_n$, constant/zero gates form $\Gamma_n$-invariant classes, so $C''$ remains matrix-symmetric, homogeneous, alternating, reduced, of depth $\le 4$, gate degrees $\le d$, and orbit size $\le f(n)$.
2. **Gate-support preservation at $\Pi$-gates:** Since $3\kappa_f(n) \le n+2$, `thm:support` gives $\operatorname{maxsup}(C'') \le k := \kappa_f(n) - 1 \le (n-1)/3 < n/3$. Every surviving non-constant gate $h$ of $C''$ has $\deg(p_h) \ge 1$, so any $\Pi$-gate $g$ of $C''$ has at most $r \le \deg(p_g) \le d$ non-constant children. Since $r + 2k \le d + 2k < n$ and $n \ge k + 5$ (as $n \ge 28$ and $k < n/3$), Lemma 1 guarantees $\sup(h) \subseteq \sup(g)$ for every wire $h \to g$ entering a $\Pi$-gate (constant children have $\sup(c) = \emptyset \subseteq \sup(g)$).
3. **Depth-2 support bound:** Since $C''$ has depth $\le 4$ with strictly alternating $\Sigma$/$\Pi$ layers (at most two $\Sigma$-layers on any root-to-input path) and no wire into a $\Pi$-gate is support-changing, every root-to-input path has at most $2$ support-changing wires; thus $\mathrm{supp\text{-}depth}(C'') \le 2$.
4. **Lower bound:** Setting $k' + 1 = \lfloor d^{1/2} \rfloor \ge 3$ ($k' \ge 2$ since $d \ge 9$, and $4(k'+1) \le 4(n/3)^{1/2} \le n$ for $n \ge 28$), Lemma `lem:path-lower` at $\Delta = 2$ yields orbit size $\ge \binom{n}{\lfloor \sqrt{d}\rfloor} = 2^{\Omega(\sqrt{d}\log n)}$. $\square$

## 2. Subexponential-Orbit Depth-4 Reduction (Theorem 3)

### Lemma 2 (Balanced 2-Level Collapse of Bounded-Treewidth Graphs)
Let $F$ be a graph without isolated vertices with $d := |E(F)| \ge 1$ edges ($|V(F)| \le 2d$) and $\operatorname{tw}(F) \le w$. Let $r := \lceil \sqrt{d} \rceil$. Then $F$ admits a normalised depth-$\le 2$ tree decomposition $(T', \beta')$ (`lem:normalised-decomposition` Items 1–4) with root $r_0$ ($\beta'(r_0) = \emptyset$), a single level-$1$ node $c_1$, and level-$2$ leaves $c_K$ (zero leaves when $S = \emptyset$) such that:
1. $|\beta'(c_1)| \le 3(w+1)\sqrt{d} \le 6(w+1)\lceil \sqrt{d} \rceil$ (and $|\beta'(c_1)| \le 2(w+1)\lceil \sqrt{d} \rceil$ when $S = \emptyset$), while $|\beta'(c_K)| \le 2(w+1)\lceil \sqrt{d} \rceil$ for every $K$,
2. The number of level-$2$ leaves $c_K$ is at most $6\sqrt{d} + 1 \le 12\sqrt{d} + 1$,
3. Each level-$2$ leaf $c_K$ represents $1 \le |\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_K)| \le 2\lceil \sqrt{d} \rceil$ edges of $F$.

*Proof.* Start with a normalised tree decomposition $(T, \beta)$ of $F$ of bag size $\le w+1$ with $|V(T)| \le |V(F)| + 1$ (`lem:normalised-decomposition`, where the $+1$ is the empty root $r_0$). Delete the empty root $r_0$ and connect the resulting forest on $\le |V(F)| \le 2d$ nodes arbitrarily into a tree (preserving the running intersection property since $\beta(r_0) = \emptyset$). Binarize $T$ (replacing nodes of degree $>3$ by binary trees of copies of the same bag), keeping $|V(T)| \le 4d$ and bag size $\le w+1$. For any node $t$ with $|\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(t)| > r := \lceil \sqrt{d} \rceil$, replace $t$ by a path of $\lceil |\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(t)|/r \rceil$ copies of $\beta(t)$ each representing $\le r$ edges; this adds $< d/r$ nodes, so $|V(T)| \le 5d$, maximum degree $\le 3$, and total weight $W := \sum_t (1 + |\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(t)|) \le 6d$.

- **Case $W \le 2r$ ($S = \emptyset$):** Collapse the entire tree $T$ into the single level-$1$ node $c_1$ (no level-$2$ leaves), with $\beta'(c_1) := \bigcup_{t \in V(T)} \beta(t)$. Since $|V(T)| \le W - d \le 2r$, $|\beta'(c_1)| \le 2(w+1)\lceil \sqrt{d} \rceil$.
- **Case $W > 2r$:** Root $T$ arbitrarily once and for all. While the connected component of $T \setminus S$ containing the root (or any remaining connected component, rooted at its topmost node in $T$) has total weight $> 2r$, choose a deepest node $u$ in that component whose downward subtree $T_u \subseteq T \setminus S$ has weight $> 2r$. Since $1 + |\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(u)| \le r + 1 \le 2r$, every child subtree of $u$ in $T \setminus S$ has weight $\le 2r$. Deleting $u$ into $S$ permanently removes $u$ and detaches all child subtrees of $T_u$, so the truncated subtrees $T_u$ at the steps when each $u \in S$ is chosen are pairwise disjoint subsets of $V(T)$, each of weight $> 2r$. Consequently $|S| \le \lfloor W / (2r + 1) \rfloor \le 3\sqrt{d}$, and $T \setminus S$ has at most $2|S| + 1 \le 6\sqrt{d} + 1$ connected components $K$, each of weight $\le 2r$. Set $\beta'(c_1) = \bigcup_{u \in S} \beta(u)$ and $\beta'(c_K) = \bigcup_{t \in K} \beta(t)$, assigning every edge represented in $S$ to $c_1$ and every edge represented in $K$ to $c_K$.

Finally, enforce `lem:normalised-decomposition` Items 2–4: (i) in $\beta'(c_1)$ and each $\beta'(c_K)$, remove any vertex not incident to any edge represented at or below that node (shrinking bags only, while preserving running intersection and edge coverage); (ii) for any level-$2$ leaf $c_K$ with $\beta'(c_K) \subseteq \beta'(c_1)$ (in particular, any empty leaf with $\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_K) = \emptyset$), transfer $\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_K)$ to $c_1$ (valid since every edge in $\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_K)$ has both endpoints in $\beta'(c_K) \subseteq \beta'(c_1)$) and delete $c_K$. Then every surviving leaf satisfies $1 \le |\mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_K)| \le 2\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil$ and $|\beta'(c_K)| \le 2(w+1)\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil$. $\square$

### Theorem 3 (Depth-4 Reduction for Matrix-Symmetric Circuits)
Let $\varepsilon > 0$ and let $(p_n)$ be computed by matrix-symmetric circuits of degree $\le d(n)$ and orbit size $s(n) \le 2^{n^{1-\varepsilon}}$ (for $(p_n) \in \mathsf{symVP}$, $\kappa = O(1)$). Then for all sufficiently large $n$, $p_n$ is computed by a **depth-$4$ ($\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$)** matrix-symmetric circuit over $\mathbb{K}$ (in `prelims.tex` L7's unweighted `+`/`\times` model, with every gate below the top $\Sigma$-gate homogeneous, and the entire circuit homogeneous whenever $p_n$ is homogeneous) of orbit size $\le \min\{n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}, (n!)^2\}$, where $\kappa = \kappa_{\max\{s(n), n^2\}}(n) \le \min\{n^{1-\varepsilon}, \frac{\log \max\{s(n), n^2\}}{\varepsilon \log n}\} + 1$. Moreover, whenever $6\kappa\sqrt{d(n)} < n/2$ (note $3\kappa\sqrt{d} \le 6\kappa\sqrt{d}$ and $2\kappa\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil \le 4\kappa\sqrt{d} < 6\kappa\sqrt{d}$ for all $d \ge 1$), the constructed circuit has support size $< n/2$ and support depth $\le 2$; and whenever $p_n$ is a single pattern $\hom_{F,n}$ (or an $|\mathcal{F}|$-term pattern sum with $|\mathcal{F}| \le n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$) with $d(n) \le n^{2-\delta}$, the total circuit size is also $\le n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$.

*Proof.* By the proof of `cor:vsbr` (`app-vsbr.tex`), $p_n = c_0 + \sum_F \alpha_F \hom_{F,n}$ for a constant $c_0 \in \mathbb{K}$ and non-trivial bipartite multigraphs $F$ (without isolated vertices) with $1 \le |E(F)| \le d(n)$ and $\operatorname{tw}(F) \le \kappa - 2$. Applying Lemma 2 to each such $F$ gives a normalised depth-$\le 2$ decomposition $(T', \beta')$ with one level-$1$ node $c_1$. In the construction of `thm:converse` (`app-circuits-from-patterns.tex`), $R_{r_0, \emptyset}$ has one child $S_{c_1, \emptyset}$. If $(T', \beta')$ has depth $1$ ($S = \emptyset$, no leaves), then $\alpha_F \hom_{F,n} = \sum_{\gamma_1} \alpha_F \prod_{e \in E(F)} x_{\gamma_1(e)}$ is depth $2 \le 4$ with bag size $\le 2\kappa\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil$. Otherwise $(T', \beta')$ has depth $2$ and $\alpha_F \hom_{F,n}$ is computed at depth $4$ ($\Sigma \to \Pi \to \Sigma \to \Pi$) under `prelims.tex` L7's unweighted `+`/`\times` convention by absorbing the scalar input gate $\alpha_F$ directly into Layer 2:
- Layer 1 (top $\Sigma$): sums $c_0$ (a constant input gate of orbit size $1$) and $\sum_F \sum_{\gamma_1} R_{c_1, \gamma_1}$.
- Layer 2 ($\Pi$): $R_{c_1, \gamma_1} = \alpha_F \cdot \big(\prod_{e \in \mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_1)} x_{\gamma_1(e)}\big) \cdot \prod_K S_{c_K, \gamma_1|_{\beta'(c_1)\cap\beta'(c_K)}}$.
- Layer 3 ($\Sigma$): $S_{c_K, \delta} = \sum_{\gamma_2 \supseteq \delta} R_{c_K, \gamma_2}$.
- Layer 4 ($\Pi$): $R_{c_K, \gamma_2} = \prod_{e \in \mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_K)} x_{\gamma_2(e)}$.

Since every $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ acts on bags of size $\le \max\{3\kappa\sqrt{d(n)}, 2\kappa\lceil\sqrt{d(n)}\rceil\} \le 4\kappa\sqrt{d(n)}$ (when $d \ge 1$), every gate belonging to a single $\hom_{F,n}$ has $\Gamma_n$-orbit size at most $n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$, and after rigidification (`lem:reduction`) at most $|\Gamma_n| = (n!)^2$. Because $\operatorname{maxorb}(C)$ is the maximum orbit size across gates (and distinct patterns $F$ lie in separate orbits of the top sum), the orbit size of the combined depth-$4$ circuit is at most $\min\{n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}, (n!)^2\}$. When $p_n$ is homogeneous, every $F$ has $|E(F)| = \deg(p_n)$ (and $c_0 = 0$ if $\deg(p_n) \ge 1$), so every gate including the top $\Sigma$-gate is homogeneous. Moreover, whenever $6\kappa\sqrt{d(n)} < n/2$, every bag has size $< n/2$, so `thm:converse` gives support size $< n/2$ and support depth $\le 2$; and for any single pattern $\hom_{F,n}$ (or $|\mathcal{F}| \le n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$) with $d(n) \le n^{2-\delta}$, the total circuit size is $\le n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$. $\square$

## 3. Subexponential-Orbit Depth-3 Reduction over $\overline{\mathbb{K}}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ (Theorem 4)

### Theorem 4 (Symmetric Depth-3 Reduction via Equivariant `KKS16`)
Let $(p_n)$ be as in Theorem 3 (with degree $\le d(n)$ and $\kappa = \kappa_{\max\{s(n), n^2\}}(n)$). In the standard algebraic-complexity depth-$3$ model ($\Sigma\Pi\Sigma$ with weighted linear-combination $\Sigma$-gates, as in `[KKS16, Tavenas15]`):
1. Over $\overline{\mathbb{K}}$ (or any algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$), $p_n$ is computed by a (non-homogeneous) **depth-$3$ ($\Sigma\Pi\Sigma$)** matrix-symmetric circuit of orbit size $\le \min\{n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}, (n!)^2\}$ (and for $p_n = \hom_{F,n}$, total size $n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}$).
2. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ (when $p_n \in \mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{X}_n]$), $p_n$ is computed by a **depth-$3$ ($\Sigma\Pi\Sigma$)** matrix-symmetric circuit over $\mathbb{Q}$ of orbit size $\le \min\{n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})}, (n!)^2\}$ (and for $p_n = \hom_{F,n}$, total size $n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d(n)})} \cdot 2^{O(\sqrt{d(n)}\log d(n))}$).

In `prelims.tex` L7's unweighted `+`/`\times` model (where each affine-linear combination $c_0 + \sum c_j x_j$ takes depth $2$ via fan-in-$2$ scalar-multiplication $\times$-gates below a `+`-gate), both circuits have **depth $4$** ($\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$) with the exact same orbit and total size bounds.

*Proof.* For Part 2 ($p_n \in \mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{X}_n]$ computed over a characteristic-$0$ field $\mathbb{K} \supseteq \mathbb{Q}$), fix any $\mathbb{Q}$-linear projection $\tau : \mathbb{K} \to \mathbb{Q}$ with $\tau(1) = 1$; applying $\tau$ coefficientwise to $p_n = c_0 + \sum_F \alpha_F \hom_{F,n}$ yields $p_n = \tau(p_n) = \tau(c_0) + \sum_F \tau(\alpha_F)\hom_{F,n}$ with $\tau(c_0), \tau(\alpha_F) \in \mathbb{Q}$ (since $\hom_{F,n} \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{X}_n]$). Hence we may assume $\alpha_F \in \mathbb{Q}$.

Start from Theorem 3's Lemma 2 decomposition of each non-trivial $F$. If $a = 0$ (depth-$1$ Lemma 2 decomposition, no level-$2$ leaves), then $\hom_{F,n} = \sum_{\gamma_1} \prod_{e \in E(F)} x_{\gamma_1(e)}$ is already a depth-$2$ $\Sigma\Pi$ circuit over $\mathbb{Q}$ (which adds at most depth $2 \le 3$ to the top $\Sigma$-gate). Otherwise $a \ge 1$ and:
$$\hom_{F,n} = \sum_{\gamma_1} \Big(\prod_{e \in \mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_1)} x_{\gamma_1(e)}\Big) \prod_{j=1}^{a} Q_{j, \gamma_1}(X),$$
where $1 \le a \le 6\sqrt{d} + 1$ indexes the components $c_{K_j}$ in a **fixed** canonical order independent of $\gamma_1$, and each $Q_{j, \gamma_1} = S_{c_{K_j}, \gamma_1|_{\beta'(c_1)\cap\beta'(c_{K_j})}}$ is a sum of $R_j \le n^{2(\kappa-1)\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil}$ monomials of degree $1 \le b_j \le b := 2\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil$ (Lemma 2).

1. **Symmetric Fischer expansion:** Pad each monomial of $Q_{j, \gamma_1}$ with $b - b_j$ copies of $1$ so every monomial is a product of $b$ affine-linear terms in a fixed edge ordering of $F$. Applying Fischer's identity
   $$y_1 \cdots y_b = \frac{1}{2^{b-1} b!} \sum_{\epsilon \in \{1\}\times\{\pm 1\}^{b-1}} \Big(\prod_{s=1}^b \epsilon_s\Big) \Big(\sum_{s=1}^b \epsilon_s y_s\Big)^b$$
   writes $Q_{j, \gamma_1} = \frac{1}{2^{b-1}b!} \sum_{r} \sigma_{j,r} (\ell_{j, \gamma_1, r}(X))^b$ with $\sigma_{j,r} \in \{\pm 1\}$ and at most $2^{b-1} n^{2(\kappa-1)\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil}$ terms, $\Gamma_n$-equivariantly in $\gamma_1$. Applying Fischer's identity to the $a$-fold product $\prod_{j=1}^a Q_{j, \gamma_1}$ expresses it as $\frac{1}{2^{a-1} a! (2^{b-1}b!)^a} \sum_{\delta \in \{1\}\times\{\pm 1\}^{a-1}} (\prod_j \delta_j) P_{\gamma_1, \delta}(X)^a$, where $P_{\gamma_1, \delta} = \sum_{r=1}^{R''} \sigma_r \ell_{\gamma_1, r}(X)^b$ ($\sigma_r \in \{\pm 1\}$) and $R'' \le a \cdot 2^{b-1} n^{2(\kappa-1)\lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil} = n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d})}$.

2. **Truncated exponential & $\mathbb{Q}$-interpolation:** Define $f_{a,b}(y) := \sum_{m=0}^a \frac{y^{bm}}{m!}$ and set $z_q := q^b$ for $q = 0, 1, \dots, M-1$ where $M := a R'' + 1$. Then
   $$H_{\gamma_1, \delta}(z_q, X) := \prod_{r=1}^{R''} \sum_{m=0}^a \frac{(z_q \sigma_r \ell_{\gamma_1, r}^b)^m}{m!} = \prod_{r=1}^{R''} f_{a,b}\big(q \sigma_r^{1/b} \ell_{\gamma_1, r}(X)\big)$$
   has $\deg_z H_{\gamma_1, \delta}(z, X) \le a R'' < M$ and satisfies $a! \cdot [z^a] H_{\gamma_1, \delta}(z, X) = P_{\gamma_1, \delta}(X)^a$. Over $\overline{\mathbb{K}}$, each factor splits into $ab$ linear factors $\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 \ell_{\gamma_1, r}(X)$, so multiplying by $\prod_{e \in \mathrm{rep}^{-1}(c_1)} x_{\gamma_1(e)}$ and applying Lagrange interpolation at $z_0, \dots, z_{M-1}$ yields a $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma$ circuit (weighted $\Sigma$-gates) where every $\Pi$-gate has orbit size $\le n^{|\beta'(c_1)|} = n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d})}$ and every bottom $\Sigma$-gate has orbit size $\le n^{|\beta'(c_K)|} = n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d})}$.

3. **Descent to $\mathbb{Q}$:** Let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2b}, \alpha_1^{1/b}, \dots, \alpha_a^{1/b})$ be the splitting field of $f_{a,b}(y)$ and $y^b + 1$ ($\zeta_{2b}$ a primitive $2b$-th root of unity, $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_a$ roots of $\sum_{m=0}^a u^m/m!$). Notice $L$ is independent of $q, r, \gamma_1, X$ and has degree $m := [L:\mathbb{Q}] \le 2b \cdot a! \cdot b^a = 2^{O(\sqrt{d}\log d)}$. By the Primitive Element Theorem, $L = \mathbb{Q}(\theta)$ with minimal polynomial $\mu_\theta(t) \in \mathbb{Q}[t]$ of degree $m$. Writing every factor in $L$ as $\sum_{s=0}^{m-1} \theta^s \tilde{\ell}_s(X)$ with $\tilde{\ell}_s \in \mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{X}_n]$ and replacing $\theta$ by a formal variable $t$ yields a univariate polynomial $P_{\gamma_1, \delta, q}(t, X) \in (\mathbb{Q}[X])[t]$ of degree $D_t \le ab R'' (m - 1)$ in $t$. Since $P_{\gamma_1, \delta, q}(\theta, X) \in \mathbb{Q}[X]$, reducing modulo $\mu_\theta(t)$ leaves only the $t^0$-term, which is a fixed $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of $P_{\gamma_1, \delta, q}(t_\nu, X)$ at $t_0, \dots, t_{D_t} \in \mathbb{Q}$. Every gate remains $\Gamma_n$-equivariant with orbit size $\le \min\{n^{O(\kappa\sqrt{d})}, (n!)^2\}$. (Under `prelims.tex` L7's unweighted model, implementing each affine-linear form at the bottom via fan-in-$2$ `scalar * variable` $\times$-gates adds depth $1$, keeping total depth $4$.) $\square$
