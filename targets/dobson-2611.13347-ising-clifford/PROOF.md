# Proof: Resolution of `arXiv:2611.13347v1` Section 5 Problem (3)

We resolve all parts of Problem (3) in Section 5 of Dobson and Wang (`arXiv:2611.13347v1`, `sections/5.tex` line 11):

1. **Ancilla-assisted braiding still fails to capture the Clifford group for $n \ge 3$ (Section 1–2):** Even with any finite number $a \ge 0$ of ancilla qubits initialized to $|0\rangle$, Ising anyon braiding in the dense encoding cannot simulate all $n$-qubit Clifford gates for any $n \ge 3$. This holds both in the standard register convention $|\psi\rangle_D \otimes |0^a\rangle_A$ ($D = \{0,\dots,n-1\}$, where even $H_1$ is impossible for every $a \ge 0$ and every choice of output wires $D'$) and under arbitrary input/output wire placements $D, D' \subset \{0,\dots,n+a-1\}$ (where $C_{\mathrm{GHZ}} = H_0 \mathrm{CNOT}_{2,0} \mathrm{CNOT}_{1,0}$ is impossible for every $a \ge 0$).
2. **Comparison of Ising complexity classes with $\mathbf{L}^{\texttt{STAB}}$, $\mathbf{PUSC}$, and $\mathbf{LUSC}$ (Section 3–4):**
   - **Braiding-only model ($\texttt{ISING-BRAID}$, $\mathbf{PUSB}$, $\mathbf{LUSB}$):** Strictly weaker than the Clifford classes. Specifically, $\texttt{ISING-BRAID} \in \mathbf{L}$, so $\mathbf{L}^{\texttt{ISING-BRAID}} = \mathbf{L} \subseteq \oplus\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{L}^{\texttt{STAB}}$, and unconditionally $\mathbf{LUSB} \subsetneq \mathbf{LUSC}$ and $\mathbf{PUSB} \subsetneq \mathbf{PUSC}$ (every language in $\mathbf{PUSB}$ depends on at most two adjacent input symbols for large $n$, excluding $\texttt{PARITY}$).
   - **Measurement-assisted Ising model with Pauli feed-forward ($\texttt{ISING-MEAS-PAULI}$, $\mathbf{PUSI}$, $\mathbf{LUSI}$):** Equivalent to Clifford quantum computers. Using a single ancilla initialized to $|0\rangle$, braiding plus interferometric measurements ($\mathcal{M}_1 = Z_j Z_n$, $\mathcal{M}_2 = X_n$) and conditional Pauli braids simulate every Clifford gate deterministically, while the principle of deferred measurement compiles every adaptive Pauli-feed-forward Ising circuit in log-space back to a unitary stabilizer circuit. Hence $\mathbf{L}^{\texttt{ISING-MEAS-PAULI}} = \mathbf{L}^{\texttt{STAB}} = \oplus\mathbf{L}$, $\mathbf{PUSI} = \mathbf{PUSC} = \mathbf{AFF} \cap \mathbf{P}$, and $\mathbf{LUSI} = \mathbf{LUSC}$.

## 1. Majorana Algebra of Dense-Encoded Ising Braiding

Consider $m \ge 1$ qubits in the dense encoding (`sections/2.tex`, line 420), realized in $\mathrm{Hom}(\sigma^{\otimes(2m+2)}, \mathbf{1})$ with qubits indexed $0, \dots, m-1$. By the proposition in `sections/4.tex` (lines 32–34) and the fusion rules on the left/right boundary vacuum edges (`sections/2.tex`, line 366; `sections/4.tex`, line 62), the $2m+1$ adjacent braiding generators $B_0, B_1, \dots, B_{2m}$ of the braid group $B_{2m+2}$ act on $(\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes m}$ as
$$B_0 = e^{-i\pi/8} S_0, \qquad B_{2j+1} = e^{-i\pi/8} \sqrt{X}_j \quad (0 \le j \le m-1),$$
$$B_{2j+2} = e^{-i\pi/8} (S_j \otimes S_{j+1})\mathrm{CZ}_{j,j+1} \quad (0 \le j \le m-2), \qquad B_{2m} = e^{-i\pi/8} S_{m-1},$$
where $S = \mathrm{diag}(1, i)$ and $\sqrt{X} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1+i & 1-i \\ 1-i & 1+i \end{pmatrix}$. Writing $P_1, \dots, P_{2m+1}$ for the $m$-qubit Pauli operators
$$P_1 = Z_0, \quad P_{2j+2} = X_j \ (0 \le j \le m-1), \quad P_{2j+1} = Z_{j-1} Z_j \ (1 \le j \le m-1), \quad P_{2m+1} = Z_{m-1},$$
direct matrix evaluation gives
$$B_{r-1} = e^{i\pi/8} \frac{I - i P_r}{\sqrt{2}} = e^{i\pi/8} \exp\!\left(-i \frac{\pi}{4} P_r\right) \qquad (1 \le r \le 2m+1).$$
Notice that $P_r$ and $P_s$ anticommute if $|r - s| = 1$ and commute if $|r - s| \ge 2$, and satisfy $P_1 P_3 \cdots P_{2m+1} = I$. Moreover, $B_0^2 = e^{-i\pi/4} Z_0$, $B_{2j+2}^2 = e^{-i\pi/4} Z_j Z_{j+1}$, $B_{2m}^2 = e^{-i\pi/4} Z_{m-1}$, and $B_{2j+1}^2 = e^{-i\pi/4} X_j$, so every $m$-qubit Pauli operator lies in the braiding group $\rho(B_{2m+2})$ up to global phase.

Let $\mathrm{Cl}_{2m+2}$ be the complex Clifford algebra generated by $2m+2$ Hermitian Majorana operators $\gamma_0, \dots, \gamma_{2m+1}$ satisfying $\{\gamma_a, \gamma_b\} = 2\delta_{ab} I$, acting on $(\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes m}$ via the even subalgebra identification
$$-i \gamma_{r-1} \gamma_r = P_r \qquad (1 \le r \le 2m+1),$$
under which the total parity operator satisfies $(-i)^{m+1} \gamma_0 \gamma_1 \cdots \gamma_{2m+1} = P_1 P_3 \cdots P_{2m+1} = I$.

**Lemma 1.1 (Majorana conjugation and weight invariance).**
1. For each $r \in \{1, \dots, 2m+1\}$, conjugation by $B_{r-1} = e^{i\pi/8} \exp(-\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_{r-1}\gamma_r)$ acts on the Majorana generators as the signed transposition
   $$B_{r-1} \gamma_{r-1} B_{r-1}^\dagger = \gamma_r, \qquad B_{r-1} \gamma_r B_{r-1}^\dagger = -\gamma_{r-1}, \qquad B_{r-1} \gamma_s B_{r-1}^\dagger = \gamma_s \quad (s \notin \{r-1, r\}).$$
   Consequently, every $B \in \rho(B_{2m+2})$ induces a signed permutation $\gamma_a \mapsto \epsilon_a \gamma_{\pi(a)}$ ($\epsilon_a \in \{\pm 1\}$, $\pi \in S_{2m+2}$) in the even subalgebra.
2. Every $m$-qubit Pauli operator $P = X^x Z^z$ ($x, z \in \mathbb{F}_2^m$) has a unique pair of complementary even-size masks $\{M(P), M(P)^c\} \subset \mathbb{F}_2^{2m+2}$ such that $P \propto \prod_{a \in M(P)} \gamma_a \propto \prod_{a \in M(P)^c} \gamma_a$, given explicitly by
   $$M(X^x Z^z) = \bigoplus_{j=0}^{m-1} x_j (e_{2j+1} \oplus e_{2j+2}) \;\oplus\; \bigoplus_{j=0}^{m-1} z_j \Bigl(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{2j+1} e_r\Bigr) \in \mathbb{F}_2^{2m+2},$$
   where $M(P)^c = M(P) \oplus \mathbf{1}$. Defining the Majorana weight
   $$w_\gamma(P) := \min\bigl(|M(P)|,\, 2m+2 - |M(P)|\bigr),$$
   we have $w_\gamma(B P B^\dagger) = w_\gamma(P)$ for every $B \in \rho(B_{2m+2})$ and every Pauli $P$.
3. For every $P = X^x Z^z$ and every qubit index $k \in \{0, \dots, m-1\}$,
   $$M(P)_{2k+1} \oplus M(P)_{2k+2} = z_k \pmod 2.$$

*Proof.*
(1) Since $B_{r-1} = e^{i\pi/8}\frac{I - \gamma_{r-1}\gamma_r}{\sqrt{2}} = e^{i\pi/8}\exp(-\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_{r-1}\gamma_r)$, $\gamma_{r-1}\gamma_r$ anticommutes with $\gamma_{r-1}, \gamma_r$ and commutes with $\gamma_s$ ($s \notin \{r-1,r\}$), giving $e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_{r-1}\gamma_r} \gamma_{r-1} e^{\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_{r-1}\gamma_r} = \gamma_{r-1} e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\gamma_{r-1}\gamma_r} = \gamma_r$ and similarly $\gamma_r \mapsto -\gamma_{r-1}$.
(2) By definition, $X_j = P_{2j+2} = -i\gamma_{2j+1}\gamma_{2j+2}$ has mask $e_{2j+1} \oplus e_{2j+2}$, $Z_0 = P_1 = -i\gamma_0\gamma_1$ has mask $e_0 \oplus e_1$, and $Z_j = P_1 P_3 \cdots P_{2j+1}$ has mask $\bigoplus_{r=0}^{2j+1} e_r$. Because $(-i)^{m+1}\gamma_0 \cdots \gamma_{2m+1} = I$, complementing $M(P)$ yields the same Pauli operator up to scalar in $\{\pm 1, \pm i\}$. Since every $B \in \rho(B_{2m+2})$ permutes $\{\gamma_0, \dots, \gamma_{2m+1}\}$ up to signs, $|M(B P B^\dagger)|$ equals either $|M(P)|$ or $2m+2 - |M(P)|$, preserving $w_\gamma$.
(3) For any $j \in \{0,\dots,m-1\}$, the generator mask $M(X_j) = e_{2j+1} \oplus e_{2j+2}$ affects bits $2k+1$ and $2k+2$ equally (contributing $0$ to $M_{2k+1} \oplus M_{2k+2}$). Meanwhile, $M(Z_j) = \bigoplus_{r=0}^{2j+1} e_r$ contains index $2k+1$ iff $k \le j$ and contains index $2k+2$ iff $2k+2 \le 2j+1 \iff k < j$, so $M(Z_j)_{2k+1} \oplus M(Z_j)_{2k+2} = \mathbf{1}_{j=k}$. By $\mathbb{F}_2$-linearity of $M$, $M(X^x Z^z)_{2k+1} \oplus M(X^x Z^z)_{2k+2} = z_k$. $\square$

## 2. Impossibility of Simulating All $n$-Qubit Clifford Gates with $|0\rangle$ Ancillas

Fix $n \ge 3$ data qubits and $a \ge 0$ ancilla qubits initialized to $|0\rangle^{\otimes a}$, so $m = n + a$. Let $D = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1})$ be the distinct data wire indices in $\{0, \dots, m-1\}$ and $A = \{0,\dots,m-1\} \setminus D$ the $a$ ancilla wires. Suppose a braid $B \in \rho(B_{2m+2})$ simulates an $n$-qubit Clifford unitary $C$ on output wires $D' = (d'_0, \dots, d'_{n-1})$ (allowing arbitrary relocation of the data qubits and any ancilla output state):
$$B\bigl(|\psi\rangle_D \otimes |0^a\rangle_A\bigr) = (C|\psi\rangle)_{D'} \otimes |\phi(\psi)\rangle_{A'} \qquad \forall |\psi\rangle \in (\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes n},$$
where $A' = \{0,\dots,m-1\} \setminus D'$.

**Lemma 2.1.**
1. By linearity of $B$, the output ancilla state $|\phi(\psi)\rangle_{A'} = |\phi_{\mathrm{anc}}\rangle_{A'}$ is independent of $|\psi\rangle$ (up to global phase, which can be absorbed into $C$).
2. For every $n$-qubit Pauli operator $Q$ on $D$, there exists $\mathbf{v}(Q) \in \mathbb{F}_2^A$ such that
   $$B^\dagger \bigl((C Q C^\dagger)_{D'} \otimes I_{A'}\bigr) B = \pm Q_D \otimes Z_A^{\mathbf{v}(Q)}.$$

*Proof.*
(1) If $|\psi_1\rangle, |\psi_2\rangle$ are orthogonal unit vectors, applying $B$ to $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\psi_1\rangle+|\psi_2\rangle)$ forces $(C|\psi_1\rangle)|\phi(\psi_1)\rangle + (C|\psi_2\rangle)|\phi(\psi_2)\rangle = (C|\psi_1\rangle+C|\psi_2\rangle)|\phi\rangle$ for a unit vector $|\phi\rangle$; by linear independence of $C|\psi_1\rangle$ and $C|\psi_2\rangle$, $|\phi(\psi_1)\rangle = |\phi(\psi_2)\rangle = |\phi\rangle$ up to phase.
(2) Set $P' := B^\dagger \bigl((C Q C^\dagger)_{D'} \otimes I_{A'}\bigr) B$. Since $B$ is Clifford, $P'$ is an $m$-qubit Pauli operator. For every $|\psi\rangle \in (\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes n}$,
$$P' (|\psi\rangle_D \otimes |0^a\rangle_A) = B^\dagger \bigl((C Q |\psi\rangle)_{D'} \otimes |\phi_{\mathrm{anc}}\rangle_{A'}\bigr) = (Q|\psi\rangle)_D \otimes |0^a\rangle_A.$$
Hence $P' (Q_D^\dagger \otimes I_A) (|\psi\rangle_D \otimes |0^a\rangle_A) = |\psi\rangle_D \otimes |0^a\rangle_A$ for all $|\psi\rangle$, so the Pauli operator $P' (Q_D^\dagger \otimes I_A)$ acts as $+I$ on the full $D$-register and fixes $|0^a\rangle_A$, forcing it to equal $\pm I_D \otimes Z_A^{\mathbf{v}(Q)}$ for some $\mathbf{v}(Q) \in \mathbb{F}_2^A$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.2 (Standard-register no-go with $|0\rangle$ ancillas).**
Let $n \ge 3$ and $D = (0, 1, \dots, n-1)$ with $A = \{n, \dots, n+a-1\}$ initialized to $|0^a\rangle_A$. For every $a \ge 0$ and every choice of output wires $D' = (d'_0, \dots, d'_{n-1})$, no braid $B \in \rho(B_{2(n+a)+2})$ can simulate $H_1$ (Hadamard on qubit $1$).

*Proof.*
Take $Q = Z_1$ on $D$, so $H_1 Z_1 H_1^\dagger = X_1$. By Lemma 2.1(2), $B^\dagger (X_{d'_1} \otimes I_{A'}) B = \pm Z_1 \otimes Z_A^{\mathbf{v}}$ for some $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{F}_2^A$. On the left-hand side, $w_\gamma(X_{d'_1} \otimes I_{A'}) = |\{2d'_1+1, 2d'_1+2\}| = 2$. On the right-hand side, $P = Z_1 \otimes Z_A^{\mathbf{v}}$ has $x = 0$ and $z_0 = 0, z_1 = 1, z_2 = 0$ (using $n \ge 3$). In $M(Z^z)$, each consecutive pair $\{2k, 2k+1\}$ ($0 \le k \le m$) has constant bit value $p_k := \bigoplus_{j=k}^{m-1} z_j \pmod 2$ with $p_m = 0$. Since $z_0 = 0, z_1 = 1, z_2 = 0$, we have $p_0 = p_1 \neq p_2 = p_3$. Thus at least two indices among $\{0,1,2,3\}$ have $p_k = 1$ and at least two have $p_k = 0$, giving $|M(P)| \ge 4$ and $2m+2 - |M(P)| \ge 4$. Hence $w_\gamma(Z_1 \otimes Z_A^{\mathbf{v}}) \ge 4 > 2 = w_\gamma(X_{d'_1} \otimes I_{A'})$, contradicting Lemma 1.1(2). $\square$

**Theorem 2.3 (Arbitrary-placement no-go with $|0\rangle$ ancillas).**
Let $n \ge 3$. For every $a \ge 0$, every choice of input data wires $D = (d_0, \dots, d_{n-1}) \subset \{0, \dots, n+a-1\}$ (with $A = \{0,\dots,n+a-1\} \setminus D$ initialized to $|0^a\rangle_A$), and every choice of output wires $D' = (d'_0, \dots, d'_{n-1})$, no braid $B \in \rho(B_{2(n+a)+2})$ can simulate the $n$-qubit Clifford gate $C_{\mathrm{GHZ}} := H_0 \mathrm{CNOT}_{2,0} \mathrm{CNOT}_{1,0}$ (acting on qubits $0,1,2$ and as $I$ on $3,\dots,n-1$).

*Proof.*
Since $\mathrm{CNOT}_{c,t}^\dagger Z_t \mathrm{CNOT}_{c,t} = Z_c Z_t$ and $H_0^\dagger X_0 H_0 = Z_0$, we have $C_{\mathrm{GHZ}}^\dagger X_0 C_{\mathrm{GHZ}} = Z_0 Z_1 Z_2$, i.e., $C_{\mathrm{GHZ}} (Z_0 Z_1 Z_2) C_{\mathrm{GHZ}}^\dagger = X_0$. Applying Lemma 2.1(2) with $Q = Z_0 Z_1 Z_2$ gives
$$B^\dagger (X_{d'_0} \otimes I_{A'}) B = \pm Z_{d_0} Z_{d_1} Z_{d_2} \otimes Z_A^{\mathbf{v}}$$
for some $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{F}_2^A$. By Lemma 1.1(2), $w_\gamma(X_{d'_0} \otimes I_{A'}) = 2$, so we must have $w_\gamma(P) = 2$ for $P = Z_{d_0} Z_{d_1} Z_{d_2} \otimes Z_A^{\mathbf{v}}$. However, $P$ has $z_{d_0} = z_{d_1} = z_{d_2} = 1$, so by Lemma 1.1(3) the three pairwise-disjoint pairs $\{2d_r+1, 2d_r+2\}$ ($r = 0,1,2$) each satisfy $M(P)_{2d_r+1} \neq M(P)_{2d_r+2}$. Therefore $M(P)$ contains at least one $1$ and at least one $0$ in each of these three disjoint pairs, yielding $|M(P)| \ge 3$ and $2m+2 - |M(P)| \ge 3$. Since $|M(P)|$ and $2m+2$ are both even, $|M(P)| \ge 4$ and $2m+2 - |M(P)| \ge 4$, so $w_\gamma(P) \ge 4$, a contradiction. $\square$

*(Remark: Because Lemma 1.1(2) only uses invariance of the quadratic Majorana subspace $\Lambda^2 \mathbb{C}^{2m+2}$, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3 also hold for arbitrary fermionic Gaussian unitaries in $\mathrm{Pin}(2m+2)$, not only Ising braids.)*

## 3. Braiding-Only Complexity Classes vs. $\mathbf{L}^{\texttt{STAB}}$, $\mathbf{PUSC}$, and $\mathbf{LUSC}$

Following Section 3 of `arXiv:2611.13347v1` (`sections/3.tex`, Definitions 3.1, 3.4, 3.8), if we restrict quantum circuits to unitary Ising braiding gates $\{B_0, \dots, B_{2m}\}$ (and their inverses) in the dense encoding, define:
- $\texttt{ISING-BRAID}$: descriptions of dense-encoding Ising braiding circuits that, on input $|0^m\rangle$, output $1$ on qubit $0$ with certainty.
- $\mathbf{PUSB}$ (resp. $\mathbf{LUSB}$): languages $A$ having a binary encoding $\epsilon: \Sigma \to \mathbb{F}_2^k$ and a poly-time (resp. log-space) uniform family $\{B_n\}$ of Ising braiding circuits that decide $A$ with respect to $\epsilon$ on $|\epsilon(\sigma)0^a\rangle$ (outputting $1$ on qubit $0$ with probability $\ge 2/3$ if $\sigma \in A$ and $\le 1/3$ if $\sigma \notin A$).

**Theorem 3.1 ($\texttt{ISING-BRAID} \in \mathbf{L}$).**
$\texttt{ISING-BRAID} \in \mathbf{L}$, and consequently $\mathbf{L}^{\texttt{ISING-BRAID}} = \mathbf{L} \subseteq \oplus\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{L}^{\texttt{STAB}}$.

*Proof.*
Given an $m$-qubit braiding circuit $B = g_T \cdots g_1$ (each $g_t \in \{B_{r-1}^{\pm 1} : 1 \le r \le 2m+1\}$), we must decide whether $B |0^m\rangle$ is a $(-1)$-eigenstate of $Z_0 = -i \gamma_0 \gamma_1$, i.e., whether $B^\dagger Z_0 B |0^m\rangle = -|0^m\rangle$. By Lemma 1.1(1), for each $t = T, T-1, \dots, 1$, conjugating $(-1)^s (-i \gamma_u \gamma_v)$ by $g_t^\dagger$ updates the two distinct Majorana indices $(u, v) \in \{0, \dots, 2m+1\}^2$ and sign bit $s \in \mathbb{F}_2$ via a local rule on $(u, v, s)$:
- For $g_t = B_{r-1}$ ($B_{r-1}^\dagger \gamma_{r-1} B_{r-1} = -\gamma_r$, $B_{r-1}^\dagger \gamma_r B_{r-1} = \gamma_{r-1}$): any occurrence of $r-1$ maps to $r$ (flipping $s$), and any occurrence of $r$ maps to $r-1$ (no sign flip); for $g_t = B_{r-1}^{-1}$ ($B_{r-1} \gamma_{r-1} B_{r-1}^\dagger = \gamma_r$, $B_{r-1} \gamma_r B_{r-1}^\dagger = -\gamma_{r-1}$): $r-1$ maps to $r$ (no sign flip) and $r$ maps to $r-1$ (flipping $s$).
Normalizing $u < v$ (swapping $u, v$ and flipping $s$ if $u > v$), a deterministic log-space transducer stores only $(u, v, s)$ and loop counters using $O(\log m + \log T)$ workspace, producing $B^\dagger Z_0 B = (-1)^s (-i \gamma_u \gamma_v)$ with $u < v$.

Now $-i\gamma_u\gamma_v$ ($u < v$) is a $Z$-only operator iff its mask $\{u, v\}$ or $\{u, v\}^c$ equals $M(Z^w)$ for some $w \in \mathbb{F}_2^m$. Since $M(Z^w)$ and $M(Z^w)^c$ are unions of pairs $\{2k, 2k+1\}$ ($0 \le k \le m$), a $2$-element set $\{u, v\}$ ($u < v$) corresponds to a $Z$-only operator iff $u = 2k$ and $v = 2k+1$ for some $k \in \{0, \dots, m\}$. In that case $-i \gamma_{2k}\gamma_{2k+1} = P_{2k+1} \in \{Z_0, Z_{k-1}Z_k, Z_{m-1}\}$, which acts as $+1$ on $|0^m\rangle$. Hence $B^\dagger Z_0 B |0^m\rangle = -|0^m\rangle$ iff $u$ is even, $v = u+1$, and $s = 1$, which is checkable in $\mathbf{L}$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.2 (Unconditional strict separation of $\mathbf{PUSB}, \mathbf{LUSB}$ from $\mathbf{PUSC}, \mathbf{LUSC}$).**
$\mathbf{LUSB} \subsetneq \mathbf{LUSC}$ and $\mathbf{PUSB} \subsetneq \mathbf{PUSC}$.

*Proof.*
Every braiding circuit is a Clifford circuit, so $\mathbf{LUSB} \subseteq \mathbf{LUSC}$ and $\mathbf{PUSB} \subseteq \mathbf{PUSC}$. Conversely, suppose $A \in \mathbf{PUSB}$ over alphabet $\Sigma$ is decided by $\{B_n\}$ with respect to $\epsilon: \Sigma \to \mathbb{F}_2^k$. Since $B_n$ is Clifford, by Proposition 3.7 of `sections/3.tex` (`prop:5050`), measuring $Z_0$ on $B_n |\epsilon(\sigma)0^a\rangle$ yields $1$ with probability in $\{0, 1/2, 1\}$, so bounded-error acceptance forces deterministic $0/1$ outcomes for all $\sigma \in \Sigma^n$ ($n \ge N$). Thus $B_n^\dagger Z_0 B_n = (-1)^{s_n} Z^{\mathbf{a}_n} \otimes Z_A^{\mathbf{v}_n}$ for some $\mathbf{a}_n \in \mathbb{F}_2^{kn}$ and $\mathbf{v}_n \in \mathbb{F}_2^a$. By Lemma 1.1(2), $w_\gamma(B_n^\dagger Z_0 B_n) = w_\gamma(Z_0) = 2$. As shown in Theorem 3.1, the only $Z$-only operators of Majorana weight $2$ on $m = kn+a$ wires are $P_{2r+1}$ ($0 \le r \le m$), namely $Z_0$, $Z_{r-1} Z_r$ ($1 \le r \le m-1$), and $Z_{m-1}$. Restricting to $|\epsilon(\sigma)0^a\rangle$ (where $Z_j = +1$ for $j \ge kn$), the accepted strings satisfy $\mathbf{1}_A(\sigma) = s_n \oplus \mathbf{a}_n^T \epsilon(\sigma)$, where $\mathbf{a}_n$ has Hamming weight at most $2$ and any two nonzero coordinates are adjacent indices $(r-1, r)$. Since each symbol $\sigma_\ell$ maps to a contiguous $k$-bit block of $\epsilon(\sigma)$, $\mathbf{1}_A(\sigma)$ depends on at most two adjacent symbols $(\sigma_\ell, \sigma_{\ell+1})$ for all $n \ge N$. In contrast, $\texttt{PARITY} = \{x \in \{0,1\}^* : \bigoplus_i x_i = 1\}$ is affine and in $\mathbf{L}$, so $\texttt{PARITY} \in \mathbf{AFF} \cap \mathbf{L} \subseteq \mathbf{LUSC} \subseteq \mathbf{PUSC}$ (`thm:usc_classification`), while for $n \ge 3$ it depends on all $n$ input bits and hence $\texttt{PARITY} \notin \mathbf{PUSB}$. $\square$

## 4. Measurement-Assisted Ising Quantum Computers Match Clifford Complexity Classes

In Section 4 of `arXiv:2611.13347v1`, an Ising quantum computer possesses both braiding and interferometric charge measurements (`thm:measurements_pauli`, `sections/4.tex` lines 113, 148, 234):
- **Case 1 ($\mathcal{M}_1$):** Measuring $2(k' - j)$ adjacent $\sigma$ anyons between qubit edges $j < k'$ projects onto $\mathcal{M}_1 = Z_j Z_{k'}$.
- **Case 2 ($\mathcal{M}_2$ for $k=1$):** Measuring the two $\sigma$ anyons above qubit $j$ projects onto $\mathcal{M}_2 = X_j$.

Consider the measurement-assisted Ising circuit model with classical Pauli feed-forward (`ISING-MEAS-PAULI`, $\mathbf{PUSI}$, $\mathbf{LUSI}$): circuits act on dense-encoded qubits by Ising braids $B_r^{\pm 1}$, interferometric measurements $\mathcal{M}_1 = Z_j Z_{k'}$ and $\mathcal{M}_2 = X_j$ (recording outcome bits $m_t \in \mathbb{F}_2$), and Pauli braids $X_j, Z_j$ (which are $B_{2j+1}^2$ and $B_0^2 B_2^2 \cdots B_{2j}^2$ up to global phases) conditioned on $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear combinations of prior outcome bits, with final readout of qubit $0$.

**Theorem 4.1 (Exact 1-ancilla Clifford universality via interferometric measurements).**
With a single ancilla qubit $n$ initialized to $|0\rangle_n$ (so $m = n + 1$), Ising braids, $\mathcal{M}_1 = Z_j Z_n$, $\mathcal{M}_2 = X_n$, and conditional Pauli braids simulate every elementary local Clifford generator ($S_j, H_j, \mathrm{CZ}_{j,j+1}, \mathrm{CNOT}_{j,j+1}$) deterministically in $O(n)$ operations and every arbitrary-range $\mathrm{CNOT}_{c,t}$ in $O(n^2)$ operations, returning ancilla $n$ to $|0\rangle_n$.

*Proof.*
Using the indexing of Section 1 for $m = n + 1$ qubits ($0, \dots, n$, with $2m+1 = 2n+3$ braiding generators $B_0, \dots, B_{2n+2}$), qubit $n$ is the rightmost qubit, so
- $B_{2n+2} = e^{-i\pi/8} S_n$, $B_{2n+1} = e^{-i\pi/8} \sqrt{X}_n$, so $H_n = e^{-i\pi/4} S_n \sqrt{X}_n S_n$, $Z_n = S_n^2$, $X_n = (\sqrt{X}_n)^2$ are all Ising braids.
- For any data qubit $j \in \{0, \dots, n-1\}$, $S_j$ is implemented on $|\psi\rangle \otimes |0\rangle_n$ by:
  1. Prepare $|+i\rangle_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle_n + i|1\rangle_n)$ from $|0\rangle_n$ via the braid $Z_n \sqrt{X}_n |0\rangle_n = e^{i\pi/4}|+i\rangle_n$.
  2. Measure $\mathcal{M}_1 = Z_j Z_n$ with outcome $m_1 \in \{0,1\}$ (eigenvalue $(-1)^{m_1}$). For $|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$ on qubit $j$, the unnormalized post-measurement state is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\alpha|00\rangle + i\beta|11\rangle)$ if $m_1=0$, and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(i\alpha|01\rangle + \beta|10\rangle)$ if $m_1=1$.
  3. Measure $\mathcal{M}_2 = X_n$ with outcome $m_2 \in \{0,1\}$ (eigenvalue $(-1)^{m_2}$, projecting ancilla $n$ onto $|(m_2)_X\rangle_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle_n + (-1)^{m_2}|1\rangle_n)$). The post-measurement state on qubit $j$ is $\frac{1}{2}(\alpha|0\rangle + (-1)^{m_2}i\beta|1\rangle) = \frac{1}{2} Z_j^{m_2} S_j |\psi\rangle$ if $m_1=0$, and $\frac{1}{2}((-1)^{m_2}i\alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle) = \frac{1}{2}(-1)^{m_2}i Z_j^{1 \oplus m_2} S_j |\psi\rangle$ if $m_1=1$.
  4. Apply the conditional Pauli braid $Z_j^{m_1 \oplus m_2}$ (realized up to global phase by $(B_0^2 B_2^2 \cdots B_{2j}^2)^{m_1 \oplus m_2}$). This produces $(S_j |\psi\rangle) \otimes |(m_2)_X\rangle_n$ up to global phase in all four outcome branches.
  5. Reset ancilla $n$ to $|0\rangle_n$ via the braid $X_n^{m_2} H_n$ (since $H_n |(m_2)_X\rangle_n = |m_2\rangle_n$).
Having synthesized $S_j$ on every qubit $0 \le j \le n-1$, we immediately obtain:
- $H_j = e^{-i\pi/4} S_j \sqrt{X}_j S_j$ on every qubit $j$ (since $\sqrt{X}_j = e^{i\pi/8} B_{2j+1}$ is a braid);
- nearest-neighbor $\mathrm{CZ}_{j,j+1} = e^{i\pi/8} (Z_j S_j \otimes Z_{j+1} S_{j+1}) B_{2j+2}$ (using $S^\dagger = Z S$);
- nearest-neighbor $\mathrm{CNOT}_{j,j+1} = H_{j+1} \mathrm{CZ}_{j,j+1} H_{j+1}$, hence nearest-neighbor $\mathrm{SWAP}_{j,j+1}$, and thus arbitrary-range $\mathrm{CNOT}_{c,t}$, generating all of $\mathrm{Cliff}_n$. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (Equivalence of measurement-assisted Ising and Clifford complexity classes).**
$$\mathbf{L}^{\texttt{ISING-MEAS-PAULI}} = \mathbf{L}^{\texttt{STAB}} = \oplus\mathbf{L}, \qquad \mathbf{PUSI} = \mathbf{PUSC} = \mathbf{AFF} \cap \mathbf{P}, \qquad \mathbf{LUSI} = \mathbf{LUSC}.$$

*Proof.*
($\supseteq$): Given any unitary stabilizer circuit $C$ on $n$ qubits with $g$ gates, replacing each elementary Clifford gate ($S_j, H_j, \mathrm{CZ}_{j,j+1}, \mathrm{CNOT}_{c,t}$) by its $O(n^2)$-size deterministic 1-ancilla gadget from Theorem 4.1 (adding $1$ ancilla qubit at wire $n$ initialized to $|0\rangle$) is computable in deterministic log-space and preserves the exact output distribution of qubit $0$. Thus $\texttt{STAB} \le_L \texttt{ISING-MEAS-PAULI}$, $\mathbf{PUSC} \subseteq \mathbf{PUSI}$, and $\mathbf{LUSC} \subseteq \mathbf{LUSI}$.

($\subseteq$): Conversely, by the principle of deferred measurement (together with `thm:measurements_pauli`), any measurement-assisted Ising circuit $C_{\text{Ising}}$ on $m$ wires with $r$ interferometric measurements can be compiled in deterministic log-space into a unitary stabilizer circuit $C'$ on $m + r$ wires (adding one fresh $|0\rangle$ ancilla $a_t$ per measurement $t \in \{1,\dots,r\}$):
- each braid $B_{r'}^{\pm 1}$ is replaced by its explicit Clifford gate decomposition;
- each interferometric measurement $\mathcal{M}_1 = Z_j Z_{k'}$ or $\mathcal{M}_2 = X_j$ is replaced by a coherent unitary parity-copy into $a_t$ ($\mathrm{CNOT}_{j, a_t}\mathrm{CNOT}_{k', a_t}$, or $H_j \mathrm{CNOT}_{j, a_t} H_j$);
- each Pauli braid conditioned on $\bigoplus_{t \in T} m_t$ is replaced by the product over $t \in T$ of controlled-Pauli Clifford gates ($\mathrm{CNOT}_{a_t, j}$ or $\mathrm{CZ}_{a_t, j}$).
Tracing out the $r$ deferred-measurement ancillas yields the exact same output distribution on qubit $0$ as $C_{\text{Ising}}$ on every input state $|0^m\rangle$ or $|\epsilon(\sigma)0^a\rangle$. Thus $\texttt{ISING-MEAS-PAULI} \le_L \texttt{STAB}$ (so $\mathbf{L}^{\texttt{ISING-MEAS-PAULI}} \subseteq \mathbf{L}^{\texttt{STAB}} = \oplus\mathbf{L}$), $\mathbf{PUSI} \subseteq \mathbf{PUSC}$, and $\mathbf{LUSI} \subseteq \mathbf{LUSC}$. $\square$
