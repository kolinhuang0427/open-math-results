# Proof of Chang–Yang–Yin–Yao Conjecture 2 (`arXiv:2201.02781v1`)

## Main Result

**Theorem.** *For every finite simple connected graph $G = (V, E)$ with $|V| \ge 3$, $\mathrm{fgndi}_{\sum}(G) \le 3$. More strongly, the constant vertex coloring $f(v) = 2$ for all $v \in V$ extends to an NFSD-total $3$-coloring $f: V \cup E \to \{1, 2, 3\}$.*

With $f(v) = 2$ for every $v \in V$ and $\omega(e) := f(e) \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ for $e \in E$, Definition 1 of Chang–Yang–Yin–Yao (`arXiv:2201.02781v1`) gives
$$\phi(x) = f(x) + \sum_{e \ni x} f(e) + \sum_{y \in N(x)} f(y) = c(x) + s_\omega(x),$$
where $c(x) := 2 + 2\deg_G(x) \in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ and $s_\omega(x) := \sum_{e \ni x} \omega(e)$. Thus the Theorem follows immediately by applying the following generalization of Keusch's 1-2-3 Theorem (*J. Combin. Theory Ser. B* 166, 2024, `arXiv:2303.02611v4`) to $c(x) = 2 + 2\deg_G(x)$.

**Theorem 1 (Even-Offset 1-2-3 Theorem).** *Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite simple connected graph with $|V| \ge 3$, and let $c: V \to 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ satisfy $c(x) \le c(y)$ whenever $\deg_G(x) = 1$ with unique neighbor $y$. Then there exists an edge-weighting $\omega: E \to \{1, 2, 3\}$ such that $\phi(u) := c(u) + s_\omega(u) \ne c(v) + s_\omega(v) =: \phi(v)$ for every edge $\{u, v\} \in E$.*

---

## Proof of Theorem 1

We adapt Keusch's proof of the 1-2-3 Conjecture (`arXiv:2303.02611v4`) by tracking the even vertex offsets $c(v)$ explicitly through the auxiliary lemmas and every exceptional graph-modification step.

### Notation

For $W \subseteq V$, write $E(W)$ for the edge set of $G[W]$, $\deg_W(v) := |N(v) \cap W|$, and $s_\omega(v) := \sum_{u \in N(v)} \omega(\{u, v\})$. For disjoint $S, T \subseteq V$, let $G(S, T)$ be the bipartite graph $(S \cup T, E(S, T))$ where $E(S, T)$ is the set of edges with one endpoint in $S$ and one in $T$.

---

### Preliminary Lemmas

**Lemma 1** (Keusch, `arXiv:2303.02611v4`, Lemma 1). *Let $G = (V, E)$ be connected, $v, w \in V$, and $p$ a shortest $v$-$w$-path. Then there exists an independent set $R \subseteq V$ such that $G(R, V \setminus R)$ is connected and $p$ alternates between $R$ and $V \setminus R$, where we may choose $v \in R$ or $v \in V \setminus R$.*

*Proof.* Put alternating vertices of $p$ into $R$ starting from $v$ (or its successor on $p$), then order the remaining vertices $v_{k+1}, \dots, v_n$ so each $v_i$ has a neighbor $v_j$ with $j < i$, adding $v_i$ to $R$ iff $v_i$ has no neighbor already in $R$. Both properties hold by induction. $\square$

**Lemma 2** (Keusch, *Combinatorica* 2023, `arXiv:2202.02788v3`, Lemma 2). *Let $H = (V, E)$ have a maximum cut $C = (S, T)$, let $F \subseteq E(S) \cup E(T)$, and let $\sigma$ be an orientation of $F$. Define the directed network $H_{C,F,\sigma}$ on $V \cup \{s, t\}$ with capacity-$1$ arcs $(u, v)$ and $(v, u)$ for each $\{u, v\} \in E(S, T)$, and capacity-$1$ arcs $(s, u)$ and $(v, t)$ for each $(u, v) \in \sigma$. Then $H_{C,F,\sigma}$ admits an $s$-$t$-flow of value $|F|$.*

*Proof.* Suppose the minimum $s$-$t$-cut $(A_H, B_H)$ has capacity $\ell < |F|$. Let $A = A_H \setminus \{s\}$, $B = B_H \setminus \{t\}$, $F' = \{\{u, v\} \in F : u \in A, v \in B\}$, and $E_1 = E(S \cap A, T \cap B) \cup E(S \cap B, T \cap A)$. Each edge of $F \setminus F'$ contributes at least one arc $(s, u)$ or $(v, t)$ to the cut, and each edge of $E_1$ contributes $(u, v)$ or $(v, u)$, so $|E_1| \le \ell - (|F| - |F'|) < |F'|$. Letting $E_2 = E(S \cap A, S \cap B) \cup E(T \cap A, T \cap B) \supseteq F'$, the cut $C'' = ((S \cap A) \cup (T \cap B), (S \cap B) \cup (T \cap A))$ of $H$ has size $|C| - |E_1| + |E_2| > |C| - |F'| + |F'| = |C|$, contradicting maximality of $C$. $\square$

**Lemma 3** (Even-offset version of `arXiv:2303.02611v4`, Lemma 3). *Let $H = (V, E)$ be a graph (possibly disconnected or empty-edged) and $h: V \to 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$. Then there exist $\omega: E \to \{1, 2, 3\}$ and $f: V \to \{1, 3, 5, \dots\}$ such that (i) $f(u) \ne f(v)$ for all $\{u, v\} \in E$, and (ii) $|s_\omega(v) + h(v) - f(v)| = 1$ for all $v \in V$.*

*Proof.* Order $V = \{v_1, \dots, v_n\}$ and fix a maximum cut $C = (S, T)$ of $H$. Set $s(v_i) := h(v_i) + 2\deg_H(v_i)$ (even). Processing $v_1, \dots, v_n$ sequentially, let $k_i$ be the number of neighbors $v_j$ ($j < i$) on the same side of $C$ as $v_i$. In the set of $2k_i + 2$ consecutive odd integers
$$S(v_i) := \{s(v_i) - 2k_i - 1, \dots, s(v_i) - 1, s(v_i) + 1, \dots, s(v_i) + 2k_i + 1\},$$
every element is positive because $h(v_i) \ge 2$ and $k_i \le \deg_H(v_i)$ imply $s(v_i) - 2k_i - 1 \ge 2 + 2(\deg_H(v_i) - k_i) - 1 \ge 1$. Moreover, $S(v_i)$ contains $k_i + 1$ elements satisfying $f(v_i) \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ (if $v_i \in S$) or $3 \pmod 4$ (if $v_i \in T$), of which at most $k_i$ are already used by earlier same-side neighbors, so we can pick a distinct value $f(v_i) \in S(v_i)$ in that residue class.
For each $v_i$, let $g(v_i) := \frac{1}{2}(f(v_i) - s(v_i) - 1)$ if $f(v_i) > s(v_i)$ and $\frac{1}{2}(f(v_i) - s(v_i) + 1)$ if $f(v_i) < s(v_i)$, so $|g(v_i)| \le k_i$. Initialize $\omega \equiv 2$. For each $v_i \in S$ (resp. $T$), pick $|g(v_i)|$ edges $\{v_i, v_j\}$ ($j < i$, $v_j$ on same side) into $F$, setting $\omega = 3$ and orienting $(v_i, v_j) \in \sigma$ (resp. $(v_j, v_i)$) if $f(v_i) > s(v_i)$, or $\omega = 1$ and orienting $(v_j, v_i)$ (resp. $(v_i, v_j)$) if $f(v_i) < s(v_i)$. Applying Lemma 2 gives $|F|$ edge-disjoint $s$-$t$-paths in $H_{C, F, \sigma}$. Along each nontrivial subpath $p' = (u_1, \dots, u_m)$ in $H(S, T)$, increase $\omega(\{u_r, u_{r+1}\})$ from $2$ to $3$ if $u_r \in S$, and decrease from $2$ to $1$ if $u_r \in T$ (alternating). Internal vertices keep unchanged weighted degrees, and summing the endpoint $+1/-1$ changes gives $s_\omega(v_i) = 2\deg_H(v_i) + 2g(v_i)$ for all $v_i \in V$, hence $|s_\omega(v_i) + h(v_i) - f(v_i)| = |2g(v_i) + s(v_i) - f(v_i)| = 1$. Finally, since $f(u) \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ on $S$ and $3 \pmod 4$ on $T$, and distinct for adjacent same-side vertices, (i) holds. $\square$

**Lemma 4** (Keusch, `arXiv:2303.02611v4`, Lemma 4). *Let $H = (R \cup B, E)$ be a connected bipartite graph, $\alpha: B \to \mathbb{N}$ satisfy $\alpha(v) \in \{2\deg(v)-1, 2\deg(v), 2\deg(v)+1\}$, and $R' \subseteq R$ satisfy $|R'| + \sum_{v \in B} \alpha(v) \equiv 0 \pmod 2$. Then there exists $\omega: E \to \{1, 2, 3\}$ such that $s_\omega(r)$ is odd iff $r \in R'$ ($r \in R$) and $s_\omega(v) = \alpha(v)$ ($v \in B$). Moreover, for any fixed path $p = (v_1, \dots, v_k)$ ($k \ge 3$) with $v_1, v_k \in B$, $\omega$ can be chosen so that (iv) $\omega(\{v_1, v_2\}) \ne 1$ if $\alpha(v_1) = 2\deg(v_1)+1$ and $\ne 3$ otherwise, (v) $\omega(\{v_{i-1}, v_i\}) + \omega(\{v_i, v_{i+1}\}) \in \{3, 4, 5\}$ for internal $v_i \in B$, and (vi) $\omega(\{v_{k-1}, v_k\}) \ne 1$ if $\alpha(v_k) = 2\deg(v_k)+1$ and $\ne 3$ otherwise.*

*Proof.* Set $\omega \equiv 2$ initially, take a spanning tree $T$ of $H$ containing $p$ rooted at any $r_0$, and let $V_o = R' \cup \{v \in B : \alpha(v) \ne 2\deg(v)\}$ (even cardinality). Processing $T$ bottom-up, include edge $\{v, \mathrm{par}(v)\}$ in $E_o$ iff the parity of $|E_o(v)|$ would otherwise disagree with $v \in V_o$; since $\sum_v |E_o(v)| = 2|E_o|$ is even, the root automatically matches. For each $v \in B$, $|E_o(v)|$ has the parity of $\alpha(v)$, and we reweight $\frac{1}{2}(|E_o(v)| - (\alpha(v) - 2\deg(v)))$ edges of $E_o(v)$ to $1$ and the rest to $3$, giving $s_\omega(v) = \alpha(v)$ and $s_\omega(r) \equiv |E_o(r)| \pmod 2$ for $r \in R$. When $p$ is given, at $v_1$ (resp. $v_k$) if the incident $p$-edge is in $E_o(v_1)$, assign it $3$ when $\alpha(v_1) = 2\deg(v_1)+1$ and $1$ otherwise (possible since at least one edge of $E_o(v_1)$ gets that weight); at internal $v_i \in B$, if both incident $p$-edges lie in $E_o(v_i)$, assign one $1$ and one $3$ (also always possible). $\square$

### Three Base Configurations (Even-Offset Adaptation)

We say $(R, B)$ is a *good partition* of a connected graph $H$ if $R$ is an independent set, $H(R, B)$ is connected, and $|B|$ is even. Given $c: V(G) \to 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ and an edge-weighting $\omega$, write $\phi_\omega(v) := c(v) + s_\omega(v)$. Note that $\phi_\omega(v) \equiv s_\omega(v) \pmod 2$ because every $c(v)$ is even.

**Lemma 5 (Basic Configuration).** *If $G$ has a good partition $(R, B)$, there exists $\omega: E \to \{1, 2, 3\}$ such that $\phi_\omega$ is a proper coloring of $G$ with $\phi_\omega(v)$ even iff $v \in R$.*

*Proof.* Set $h(v) := c(v) + 2\deg_R(v) \in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ on $B$. Apply Lemma 3 to $(G[B], h)$ to get $\omega_1: E(B) \to \{1, 2, 3\}$ and odd proper $f$ on $G[B]$. Let $\alpha(v) := f(v) - s_{\omega_1}(v) - c(v) \in \{2\deg_R(v)-1, 2\deg_R(v)+1\}$; since $|B|$ is even and each $\alpha(v)$ is odd, $\sum_{v \in B}\alpha(v)$ is even. Apply Lemma 4 to $G(R, B)$ with $R' = \emptyset$ to get $\omega_2$ on $E(R, B)$. Then $\omega = \omega_1 \cup \omega_2$ gives $\phi_\omega(v) = f(v)$ (odd, proper on $E(B)$) for $v \in B$ and $\phi_\omega(r) = c(r) + s_{\omega_2}(r)$ (even) for $r \in R$. Since $R$ is independent, $\phi_\omega$ is proper. $\square$

**Lemma 6 (Remaining Vertex with $\deg_R(v_0) \ge 2$).** *Let $v_0 \in V$ be such that $G - v_0$ is connected and admits a good partition $(R, B)$ with $\deg_R(v_0) \ge 2$, and either $\deg_R(v_0)$ is even or $\deg_B(v_0) \ge 1$. Then there exists $\omega: E \to \{1, 2, 3\}$ such that $\phi_\omega$ is proper.*

*Proof.* Set $h(v) := c(v) + 2|N(v) \setminus B| \in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ on $B$, and apply Lemma 3 to $(G[B], h)$ to obtain $\omega_1$ and $f$. Let $R' := N(v_0) \cap R$. If $|R'|$ is even, set $\alpha(v) := f(v) - s_{\omega_1}(v) - c(v) - 2\mathbf{1}_{v \in N(v_0)}$ for $v \in B$. If $|R'|$ is odd, fix $u_0 \in N(v_0) \cap B$, set $\alpha(u_0) := 2\deg_R(u_0)$, and set $\alpha(v) := f(v) - s_{\omega_1}(v) - c(v) - 2\mathbf{1}_{v \in N(v_0)}$ for $v \in B \setminus \{u_0\}$. In both cases $|R'| + \sum_{v \in B} \alpha(v)$ is even and $\alpha(v) \in \{2\deg_R(v)-1, 2\deg_R(v), 2\deg_R(v)+1\}$, so Lemma 4 yields $\omega_2$ on $G(R, B)$. Define $\omega_3$ on edges at $v_0$ by $\omega_3(\{v_0, r\}) = 1$ for $r \in R'$, $\omega_3(\{v_0, u_0\}) = f(u_0) - s_{\omega_1}(u_0) - s_{\omega_2}(u_0) - c(u_0) \in \{1, 3\}$ (if $|R'|$ is odd), and $\omega_3(e) = 2$ on all remaining edges between $v_0$ and $B$. Combining $\omega = \omega_1 \cup \omega_2 \cup \omega_3$, every $v \in B$ satisfies $\phi_\omega(v) = f(v)$ (odd, proper on $E(B)$), every $r \in R$ has $\phi_\omega(r)$ even, and $\phi_\omega(v_0)$ is even. Order $R' = \{v_1, \dots, v_k\}$ ($k \ge 2$) so that $\phi_\omega(v_1) \le \dots \le \phi_\omega(v_k)$. If $\phi_\omega(v_0) = \phi_\omega(v_i)$ for some $i$, let $x \ge 1$ be minimal with $\phi_\omega(v_0) + 2x \notin \{\phi_\omega(v_1), \dots, \phi_\omega(v_k)\}$ and $i' \le k$ maximal with $\phi_\omega(v_{i'}) < \phi_\omega(v_0) + 2x$. Raising $\omega(\{v_0, v_i\})$ from $1$ to $3$ for $i > k-x$ (if $i' \le k-x$), for $i \ge k-x$ (if $i' > k-x$ and $x < k$), or for $i = 2$ (if $x = k$, where $\phi_\omega(v_i) = \phi_\omega(v_0) + 2i - 2$) eliminates all conflicts between $v_0$ and $R'$ while keeping $\phi_\omega(r)$ even for all $r \in R$ and $\phi_\omega(v)$ unchanged on $B$. $\square$

**Lemma 7 (Remaining Vertex with $\deg_R(v_0) = 1$).** *Let $v_0 \in V$ be such that $G - v_0$ is connected and admits a good partition $(R, B)$ with $N(v_0) \cap R = \{u_0\}$, and suppose $G(R, B)$ contains a path $p = (v_1, \dots, v_k)$ ($k \ge 3$) with $v_1, v_k \in N(v_0) \cap B$ and $u_0 \notin p$. Then there exists $\omega: E \to \{1, 2, 3\}$ such that $\phi_\omega$ is proper.*

*Proof.* Set $h(v) := c(v) + 2|N(v) \setminus B| \in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ on $B$ and apply Lemma 3 to $(G[B], h)$ for $\omega_1, f$. For $v \in N(v_0) \cap B$, let $\beta(v) := f(v) - s_{\omega_1}(v) - c(v) - 2\deg_R(v) \in \{1, 3\}$. Set $\omega_2(e) = 2$ for all edges incident to $v_0$ if $\beta(v_1) = \beta(v_k)$, taking $R' = \emptyset$; if $\beta(v_1) \ne \beta(v_k)$, assume w.l.o.g. $\beta(v_1) = 3, \beta(v_k) = 1$, set $\omega_2(\{v_0, u_0\}) = \omega_2(\{v_0, v_1\}) = 3$ and $\omega_2(e) = 2$ on other edges at $v_0$, taking $R' = \{u_0\}$. Defining $\alpha(v) := f(v) - s_{\omega_1}(v) - c(v) - \mathbf{1}_{v \in N(v_0)}\omega_2(\{v_0, v\})$ for $v \in B$, Lemma 4 gives $\omega_3$ on $G(R, B)$ satisfying (iv)–(vi) on $p$. Under $\omega = \omega_1 \cup \omega_2 \cup \omega_3$, $\phi_\omega = f$ on $B$ (odd, proper on $E(B)$), $\phi_\omega(r)$ is even on $R$, and $\phi_\omega(v_0)$ is even. Thus the only possible conflict is $\phi_\omega(v_0) = \phi_\omega(u_0)$. If this occurs, we adjust weights along the cycle $p \cup \{v_0\}$:
- if $\beta(v_1) = \beta(v_k) = 1$, set $\omega(\{v_0, v_1\}) = \omega(\{v_0, v_k\}) = 1$ (decreasing $\phi_\omega(v_0)$ by $2$) and increase $\omega(\{v_1, v_2\}), \omega(\{v_{k-1}, v_k\})$ by $1$ (valid by (iv), (vi));
- if $\beta(v_1) = \beta(v_k) = 3$, set $\omega(\{v_0, v_1\}) = \omega(\{v_0, v_k\}) = 3$ (increasing $\phi_\omega(v_0)$ by $2$) and decrease $\omega(\{v_1, v_2\}), \omega(\{v_{k-1}, v_k\})$ by $1$;
- if $\beta(v_1) = 3, \beta(v_k) = 1$, set $\omega(\{v_0, v_1\}) = 2, \omega(\{v_0, v_k\}) = 1$ (decreasing $\phi_\omega(v_0)$ by $2$) and increase $\omega(\{v_1, v_2\}), \omega(\{v_{k-1}, v_k\})$ by $1$.
By (v), for each internal $v_i \in B$ on $p$, we can adjust its two incident $p$-edges by $+1$ and $-1$ (or $-1$ and $+1$) to leave $s_\omega(v_i)$ unchanged. Internal vertices $v_j \in R$ on $p$ change by an even amount ($0$ or $\pm 2$), staying even, while $\phi_\omega(u_0)$ is untouched since $u_0 \notin p$. Hence $\phi_\omega(v_0) \ne \phi_\omega(u_0)$ and $\phi_\omega$ is proper. $\square$

### Exhaustive Reduction of Any Connected $G$ ($|V| \ge 3$)

**Lemma 8** (Keusch, `arXiv:2303.02611v4`, Lemma 6). *If $G$ is connected with $\delta(G) \ge 2$, there exists $\{x, y\} \in E$ such that $G - \{x, y\}$ is connected.*

*Proof.* Take a deepest leaf $x$ of a DFS tree $T$ of $G$ and its parent $y$; every other child of $y$ is also a leaf of $T$ and (having degree $\ge 2$) has a back-edge to a proper ancestor of $y$, so $G - \{x, y\}$ remains connected. $\square$

We now prove Theorem 1 for any connected $G = (V, E)$ with $|V| \ge 3$ and $c: V \to 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$.

**Case 1: $\delta(G) = 1$.** Let $\deg_G(x) = 1$ with neighbor $y$. Apply Lemma 1 to $G' = G - x$ with $p = (y)$ and $y \in R$, setting $B = (V \setminus \{x\}) \setminus R$.
- If $|B|$ is odd, $(R, B \cup \{x\})$ is a good partition of $G$, and Lemma 5 finishes.
- If $|B|$ is even, then $|B| \ge 2$ (as $G'$ is connected on $\ge 2$ vertices and $R$ is independent), so $(R, B)$ is a good partition of $G'$. Apply Lemma 5 to $G'$ with even offset $c'(v) := c(v) + 2\mathbf{1}_{v=y}$ ($v \in V \setminus \{x\}$) to obtain $\omega$ on $E(G')$, and set $\omega(\{x, y\}) = 2$. Then $\phi_\omega$ on $G$ equals $\phi_{\omega'}$ on $G'$ for all $v \in V \setminus \{x\}$ (so all edges of $G'$ are conflict-free), while $\phi_\omega(x) = c(x) + 2$ and $\phi_\omega(y) = c(y) + 2 + s_{\omega'}(y) > c(y) + 2 \ge c(x) + 2 = \phi_\omega(x)$ (as $s_{\omega'}(y) \ge \deg_{G'}(y) \ge 1$ and $c(x) \le c(y)$), so $\{x, y\}$ is also conflict-free.

- **Case 2: $\delta(G) \ge 2$ and Lemma 8 gives $\{x, y\} \in E$ with $\deg_G(x) = \deg_G(y) = 2$.** Let $G'' = G - \{x, y\}$, $z_x \in N(x) \setminus \{y\}$, $z_y \in N(y) \setminus \{x\}$ (possibly $z_x = z_y$). Apply Lemma 1 to $G''$ with $p = (z_x)$ and $z_x \in R$, setting $B = V(G'') \setminus R$.
  - $|B|$ even, $z_y \in R$: $(R, B \cup \{x, y\})$ is a good partition of $G$; apply Lemma 5.
  - $|B|$ even, $z_y \in B$: $(R \cup \{y\}, B)$ is a good partition of $G - x$; apply Lemma 6 with $v_0 = x$.
  - $|B|$ odd, $z_y \in B$: $(R \cup \{y\}, B \cup \{x\})$ is a good partition of $G$; apply Lemma 5.
  - $|B|$ odd, $z_y \in R$: form $\tilde{G} = (V(G'') \cup \{w\}, E(G'') \cup \{\{w, z_x\}\})$ with good partition $(R, B \cup \{w\})$. Apply Lemma 5 to $\tilde{G}$ with $c'(w) = 2$ and $c'(v) = c(v) + 2\mathbf{1}_{v = z_y}$ ($v \in V(G'')$) to get $\tilde{\omega}$. Since $w \in B \cup \{w\}$ and $z_x \in R$, $\mu := \tilde{\omega}(\{w, z_x\}) \in \{1, 3\}$ is odd. Transfer $\tilde{\omega}$ to $E(G'')$, set $\omega(\{y, z_y\}) = 2$, $\omega(\{x, z_x\}) = \mu$, and choose $\omega(\{x, y\}) \in \{1, 3\}$. Then $\phi_\omega(v) = \phi_{\tilde{\omega}}(v)$ for all $v \in V(G'')$ (even on $R$, odd proper on $B$), $\phi_\omega(y) = c(y) + 2 + \omega(\{x, y\})$ is odd (distinct from $\phi_\omega(z_y)$ even), and $\phi_\omega(x) = c(x) + \mu + \omega(\{x, y\})$ is even (distinct from $\phi_\omega(y)$ odd). The two choices $\omega(\{x, y\}) \in \{1, 3\}$ give distinct values for $\phi_\omega(x)$, so we pick one $\ne \phi_\omega(z_x)$.

- **Case 3 & Case 4: $\delta(G) \ge 2$ and $\deg_G(x) + \deg_G(y) > 4$.** W.l.o.g. $\deg_G(x) \ge 3$. If $(N(x) \setminus \{y\}) \cap (N(y) \setminus \{x\}) \ne \emptyset$, pick $z_x = z_y$ there and $p = (z_x)$; otherwise take a shortest path $p$ in $G''$ from $N(x) \setminus \{y\}$ to $N(y) \setminus \{x\}$, whose internal vertices avoid $N(x) \cup N(y)$, with endpoints $z_x \in N(x)$ and $z_y \in N(y)$. Apply Lemma 1 to $G''$ and $p$ with $z_x \in B := V(G'') \setminus R$.
  - **If $|B|$ is odd:**
    - If $\deg_R(x) = 0$ (resp. $\deg_R(y) = 0$ and $\deg_R(x) \ge 1$): add $x$ to $R$ and $y$ to $B$ (resp. $y$ to $R, x$ to $B$) and apply Lemma 5.
    - If $\deg_R(x) \ge 2$ (resp. $\deg_R(y) \ge 2$): add $y$ to $B$ and apply Lemma 6 with $v_0 = x$ (resp. $x$ to $B, v_0 = y$).
    - Otherwise $\deg_R(x) = \deg_R(y) = 1$, say $N(x) \cap R = \{r_x\}$ and $N(y) \cap R = \{r_y\}$.
      - If $r_x = r_y =: q$: since $\deg_G(x) \ge 3$, $\deg_B(x) \ge 1$. Form $\tilde{G} = (V \cup \{w\}, (E \setminus \{\{x, y\}, \{x, q\}\}) \cup \{\{w, y\}\})$ with good partition $(R \cup \{x, w\}, B \cup \{y\})$ and $c'(w) = 2$, $c'(q) = c(q) + 2$, $c'(v) = c(v)$ elsewhere. Apply Lemma 5 to $\tilde{G}$ to get $\tilde{\omega}$; since $w \in R \cup \{x, w\}$ has degree $1$, $\tilde{\omega}(\{w, y\}) = 2$. Transfer $\tilde{\omega}$ to $E \setminus \{\{x, y\}, \{x, q\}\}$ and set $\omega(\{x, y\}) = \omega(\{x, q\}) = 2$. Then $\phi_\omega(v) = \phi_{\tilde{\omega}}(v)$ for $v \ne x$ (even on $R$, odd proper on $B \cup \{y\}$), and $\phi_\omega(x) = \phi_{\tilde{\omega}}(x) + 4$ is even, so only $\{x, q\}$ can conflict. If $\phi_\omega(x) = \phi_\omega(q)$, letting $\mu = \omega(\{y, q\})$, set $(\omega(\{y, q\}), \omega(\{x, y\}), \omega(\{x, q\}))$ to $(3, 1, 1)$ if $\mu = 2$ and to $(2, \mu, \mu)$ if $\mu \in \{1, 3\}$; $\phi_\omega(y)$ and $\phi_\omega(q)$ are unchanged while $\phi_\omega(x)$ shifts by $\pm 2$.
      - If $r_x \ne r_y$: if $z_y \in R$, note $z_y \ne r_x$ (else $z_x = z_y = r_x \in B$), so $p' = p \cup \{y\}$ in $G(R, B \cup \{y\})$ avoids $r_x$; apply Lemma 7 with $v_0 = x, u_0 = r_x, p'$. If $z_y \in B$, neither $r_x$ nor $r_y$ lies on $p$, so in any spanning tree $T$ of $G''(R, B)$ containing $p$, either the $z_y$-$r_x$-path avoids $r_y$ (extend by $x$, apply Lemma 7 with $v_0 = y, u_0 = r_y$) or the $z_x$-$r_y$-path avoids $r_x$ (extend by $y$, apply Lemma 7 with $v_0 = x, u_0 = r_x$).
  - **If $|B|$ is even:**
    - If $\deg_R(x) \ge 1$ and $\deg_R(y) \ge 1$: add $x, y$ to $B$ and apply Lemma 5.
    - If $\deg_R(x) \ge 1$ and $\deg_R(y) = 0$: add $y$ to $R$ and apply Lemma 6 with $v_0 = x$ (since $z_x \in N(x) \cap B$).
    - If $\deg_R(x) = 0$ (so $\deg_B(x) \ge 2$):
      - If $\deg_R(y) = 0$: add $y$ to $R$, pick $z_x' \in (N(x) \cap B) \setminus \{z_x\}$ and a $z_x$-$z_x'$-path $p'$ in $G''(R, B)$, and apply Lemma 7 with $v_0 = x, u_0 = y, p'$.
      - If $\deg_R(y) \ge 1$ and ($\deg_B(y) \ge 1$ or $\deg_G(y)$ is even): add $x$ to $R$ and apply Lemma 6 with $v_0 = y$.
      - If $\deg_R(y) \ge 2$ is even (so $\deg_G(y) \ge 3$ is odd) and $\deg_B(y) = 0$: pick $z_y \in N(y) \cap R$ and form $\tilde{G} = (V, (E \setminus \{\{x, y\}, \{y, z_y\}\}) \cup \{\{x, z_y\}\})$ with good partition $(R, B \cup \{x, y\})$. Apply Lemma 5 to $\tilde{G}$ with $c' = c$ to get $\tilde{\omega}$. Since $\phi_{\tilde{\omega}}(y) = c(y) + s_{\tilde{\omega}}(y)$ is odd and $c(y)$ is even, some edge $\tilde{e}$ incident to $y$ in $\tilde{G}$ has $\tilde{\omega}(\tilde{e}) \in \{1, 3\}$ (its other endpoint lies in $R$). Put $\mu = \tilde{\omega}(\{x, z_y\})$, $\omega(\{x, y\}) = \omega(\{y, z_y\}) = \mu$, and $\omega = \tilde{\omega}$ elsewhere. Then $\phi_\omega = \phi_{\tilde{\omega}}$ on $V \setminus \{y\}$ and $\phi_\omega(y) = \phi_{\tilde{\omega}}(y) + 2\mu$ is odd. The only possible conflict is $\{x, y\}$, resolved by flipping $\omega(\tilde{e}) \mapsto 4 - \omega(\tilde{e}) \in \{1, 3\}$ (keeping the $R$-endpoint of $\tilde{e}$ even and $\phi_\omega(y)$ odd).

This exhausts all cases and proves Theorem 1. Taking $c(v) = 2 + 2\deg_G(v) \in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}$ (which satisfies $c(x) = 4 < 6 \le c(y)$ whenever $\deg_G(x) = 1$ with neighbor $y$, as $|V| \ge 3$ implies $\deg_G(y) \ge 2$) proves Conjecture 2 of Chang–Yang–Yin–Yao (`arXiv:2201.02781v1`). $\square$
