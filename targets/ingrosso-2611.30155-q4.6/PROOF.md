# Solution to Question 4.6 (`arXiv:2611.30155v1`, Section 4)

**Answer:** The smallest possible defect of subnormality in Theorem 4.5 (`\label{sip}`) is **$2$**.

More precisely:
1. **Sharp Upper Bound ($\le 2$):** In *any* group $G$ (not even requiring $G$ to be locally graded) whose subgroups are pronormal or contained in the pro-norm $N_{\mathrm{pro}}(G)$, every subnormal subgroup $H \le G$ satisfies $H \trianglelefteq H^G \trianglelefteq G$ and therefore has subnormal defect at most $2$.
2. **Tightness ($\ge 2$):** The finite $2$-group $M_{16} = \langle a, x \mid a^8 = x^2 = 1,\, x^{-1}ax = a^5 \rangle$ of order $16$ (which is locally graded and of type (2) in Theorem 3.1 of the paper) has every subgroup either normal or contained in its norm (hence pronormal or contained in $N_{\mathrm{pro}}(M_{16})$), and its subgroup $H = \langle x \rangle$ has exact subnormal defect $2$ in $M_{16}$.

---

## 1. Conventions and Definitions

Throughout, conjugation is written on the right: $x^g := g^{-1} x g$ and $K^g := g^{-1} K g$ for $x, g \in G$ and $K \le G$. (The same proof holds verbatim under left conjugation $g x g^{-1}$ upon swapping $g$ and $g^{-1}$.)

- A subgroup $H \le G$ is *pronormal* in $G$ if every $g \in G$ *pronormalizes* $H$ in $G$, i.e., $g \in P_G(H) := \{y \in G : \exists u \in \langle H, H^y \rangle \text{ such that } H^y = H^u\}$.
- The *pro-norm* of $G$ is $N_{\mathrm{pro}}(G) := \bigcap_{K \le G} P_G(K)$, the set of elements of $G$ pronormalizing every subgroup of $G$.
- The *normal closure* of $H$ in $G$ is $H^G := \langle H^g : g \in G \rangle = \langle h^g : h \in H,\, g \in G \rangle \trianglelefteq G$.

## 2. Proof of the Upper Bound ($\text{defect} \le 2$)

**Lemma 1 (Pronormalizer of a subnormal subgroup).** *Let $G$ be any group and $H \le G$ be a subnormal subgroup of $G$. Then $P_G(H) = N_G(H)$.*

*Proof.* Clearly $N_G(H) \subseteq P_G(H)$ (take $u = 1$). Conversely, let $y \in P_G(H)$ and put $J := \langle H, H^y \rangle$, so there exists $u \in J$ with $H^y = H^u$. Let
$$H = H_0 \trianglelefteq H_1 \trianglelefteq \dots \trianglelefteq H_d = G$$
be a finite subnormal series for $H$ in $G$, and set $J_i := J \cap H_i$ for $0 \le i \le d$. Then
$$H = J_0 \trianglelefteq J_1 \trianglelefteq \dots \trianglelefteq J_d = J.$$
Suppose for contradiction that $H^y \neq H$, so $J_0 = H < J = J_d$. Let $r \in \{1, \dots, d\}$ be minimal such that $J_r = J$. Then $H = J_0 \le J_{r-1} \trianglelefteq J_r = J$ and $J_{r-1} < J$. Since $u \in J$ and $J_{r-1} \trianglelefteq J$, we have
$$H^y = H^u \le (J_{r-1})^u = J_{r-1}.$$
Hence both $H$ and $H^y$ lie in $J_{r-1}$, so $J = \langle H, H^y \rangle \le J_{r-1} < J$, a contradiction. Therefore $H^y = H$, i.e., $y \in N_G(H)$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (Conjugation invariance of the pro-norm).** *For any group $G$, $N_{\mathrm{pro}}(G)$ is closed under conjugation by $G$.*

*Proof.* Let $y \in N_{\mathrm{pro}}(G)$ and $g \in G$, and let $K \le G$ be any subgroup. Since $y \in P_G(K^{g^{-1}})$, there exists $u \in \langle K^{g^{-1}}, K^{g^{-1} y} \rangle$ such that $K^{g^{-1} y} = K^{g^{-1} u}$. Conjugating both sides by $g$ (and noting $g^{-1} y g = y^g$ and $g^{-1} u g = u^g$) gives
$$K^{y^g} = K^{u^g}, \qquad u^g \in \langle K, K^{y^g} \rangle.$$
Thus $y^g \in P_G(K)$ for every $K \le G$, so $y^g \in N_{\mathrm{pro}}(G)$. $\square$

**Theorem (Sharp defect bound $\le 2$).** *Let $G$ be any group whose subgroups are pronormal or contained in $N_{\mathrm{pro}}(G)$. If $H \le G$ is subnormal in $G$, then $H \trianglelefteq H^G \trianglelefteq G$, so $H$ has subnormal defect at most $2$ in $G$.*

*Proof.*
- **Case 1:** $H$ is pronormal in $G$. Then $G = P_G(H) = N_G(H)$ by Lemma 1, so $H \trianglelefteq G$ (defect $\le 1$).
- **Case 2:** $H$ is not pronormal in $G$. By hypothesis, $H \subseteq N_{\mathrm{pro}}(G)$. By Lemma 2, for every $h \in H$ and $g \in G$, the conjugate $h^g$ belongs to $N_{\mathrm{pro}}(G) \subseteq P_G(H)$. Applying Lemma 1 gives $h^g \in N_G(H)$ for all $h \in H$ and $g \in G$. Because the normalizer $N_G(H)$ is a subgroup of $G$ and $H^G$ is generated by $\{h^g : h \in H,\, g \in G\}$, it follows that $H^G \le N_G(H)$. Since $H \le H^G \trianglelefteq G$, we obtain $H \trianglelefteq H^G \trianglelefteq G$, so $H$ has subnormal defect at most $2$ in $G$. $\square$

## 3. Tightness ($\text{defect} = 2$ achieved)

Consider the modular $2$-group of order $16$:
$$M_{16} := \langle a, x \mid a^8 = x^2 = 1,\, x^{-1} a x = a^5 \rangle.$$
Since $M_{16}$ is finite, it is locally graded. We verify directly that $M_{16}$ satisfies all hypotheses of Theorem 4.5 and has a subnormal subgroup of defect $2$:

1. **Subgroups of $M_{16}$:** Since $[a, x] = a^{-1} x^{-1} a x = a^4 \in Z(M_{16})$, we have $M_{16}' = \langle a^4 \rangle \le Z(M_{16})$ of order $2$, and $M_{16}/M_{16}' \cong C_4 \times C_2$ is abelian. Therefore every subgroup of $M_{16}$ containing $a^4$ is normal in $M_{16}$ (hence pronormal in $M_{16}$).
2. **Subgroups not containing $a^4$:** Every element of $M_{16}$ has the form $a^j$ or $x a^j$ ($0 \le j \le 7$). Note $(x a^j)^2 = a^{6j}$, which has order $4$ when $j$ is odd (hence contains $a^4$), order $2$ with $(x a^j)^2 = a^4$ when $j \in \{2, 6\}$, and equals $1$ only for $j \in \{0, 4\}$. Since every non-trivial subgroup of $\langle a \rangle$ contains $a^4$, the only non-trivial subgroups of $M_{16}$ not containing $a^4$ are $H = \langle x \rangle$ and $\langle x a^4 \rangle$.
3. **Membership in the norm / pro-norm:** For every $0 \le j \le 7$, $x^{-1} a^j x = a^{5j} \in \langle a^j \rangle$, and $[x a^j, x] = a^{-j} x^{-1} a^j x = a^{4j} = (a^4)^j$: when $j$ is even, $a^{4j} = 1$, so $x$ commutes with $x a^j$; when $j$ is odd, $a^{4j} = a^4 = (x a^j)^4$ (since $(x a^j)^2 = a^{6j}$ has order $4$, so $(x a^j)^4 = a^{12j} = a^4$), hence $x^{-1}(x a^j)x = (x a^j) a^4 = (x a^j)^5 \in \langle x a^j \rangle$. Thus $x$ normalizes every cyclic subgroup, hence every subgroup of $M_{16}$, so $x \in N(M_{16}) \subseteq N_{\mathrm{pro}}(M_{16})$ (and likewise $x a^4 \in N(M_{16}) \subseteq N_{\mathrm{pro}}(M_{16})$ since $a^4 \in Z(M_{16})$). Therefore every subgroup of $M_{16}$ is either normal (hence pronormal) or contained in $N_{\mathrm{pro}}(M_{16})$.
4. **Exact defect $2$:** For $H = \langle x \rangle$, $a^{-1} x a = x a^4 \notin H$, so $H$ is not normal in $M_{16}$ (defect $> 1$), whereas $H^G = \langle x, a^4 \rangle$ satisfies $H \trianglelefteq \langle x, a^4 \rangle \trianglelefteq M_{16}$ (since $a^4 \in Z(M_{16})$ and $\langle x, a^4 \rangle \ge M_{16}'$), giving subnormal defect exactly $2$.
