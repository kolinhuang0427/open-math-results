# Answer to Question 3.9 (`arXiv:2611.34308v1`, Section 3)

**Answer:** **YES.** For every locally compact non-Archimedean valued field $\mathbb{L}$ (of arbitrary characteristic $0$ or $p$) with residue field $\mathbb{F}_p$ ($p$ prime), the action $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright (\mathbb{L}, m_{\mathbb{L}})$ is **expanding**.

*(Remark: Combined with the paradoxicality of $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright \mathbb{L}$ cited in the paper at L625 from `[KO-CMB, Theorem 11]`, Theorem 2.17 (`thm:consequences_of_parad+expanding`, `\cite[Corollary 1.10, Lemma 8.1]{GrMaPikhurko}`) immediately extends the measurable equidecomposability and Banach–Ruziewicz conclusions (assertions (i) and (ii) of Corollary 3.4, with $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$ in place of $\Gamma$) to prime residue fields $\mathbb{F}_p$ as well.)*

---

## Proof

Let $(\mathbb{L}, |\cdot|)$ be a locally compact non-Archimedean valued field with valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}} = \{x \in \mathbb{L} : |x| \le 1\}$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{L}} = \{x \in \mathbb{L} : |x| < 1\}$, residue field $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}} / \mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{L}} \cong \mathbb{F}_p$, and Haar measure $m_{\mathbb{L}}$ on $(\mathbb{L}, +)$. By `[UC, Corollary 12.2]` (`Measurable-equidecompositions-in-Q_p_n.tex` L171), $\mathbb{L}$ is complete and its value group $|\mathbb{L}^\times|$ is infinite cyclic, generated by some real number $c > 1$. Choose a uniformizer $\pi \in \mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{L}}$ with $|\pi| = c^{-1}$.

### Step 1: Prerequisites of Definition 2.10 (`def:expanding_action`) and measure preservation

1. **Polish space and Radon measure:** For each $n \ge 0$, $\pi^{-n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}} / \pi^m \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ is finite of cardinality $p^{n+m}$ (since $\pi^k \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}} / \pi^{k+1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{L}} \cong \mathbb{F}_p$). Hence each ball $\pi^{-n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ is compact and metrizable, and $\mathbb{L} = \bigcup_{n \ge 0} \pi^{-n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ is locally compact, $\sigma$-compact, complete, and separable—therefore Polish. The Haar measure $m_{\mathbb{L}}$ is a nonzero Radon measure, so $m_{\mathbb{L}}$ is $\sigma$-finite, finite on compact sets (in particular $0 < m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}) < \infty$), and nonzero on every nonempty open set (since countably many translates of any nonempty open ball cover $\mathbb{L}$).
2. **Measure-preserving action:** Every $g \in \mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$ is a homeomorphism of $\mathbb{L}$ (hence Borel measurable). As noted in `Measurable-equidecompositions-in-Q_p_n.tex` L386 (`[Christensen, Theorem 1]`), because $d_{\mathbb{L}}(x, y) = |x - y|$ is translation-invariant and $g$ is a surjective isometry, $g_* m_{\mathbb{L}}$ is a Haar measure on $(\mathbb{L}, +)$, so $g_* m_{\mathbb{L}} = \alpha m_{\mathbb{L}}$ for some constant $\alpha > 0$. Since $|g(x) - g(0)| = |x| \le 1 \iff g(x) \in g(0) + \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$, we have $g(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}) = g(0) + \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$, whence
   \[
   \alpha\, m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}) = (g_* m_{\mathbb{L}})(g(0) + \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}) = m_{\mathbb{L}}(g^{-1}(g(0) + \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})) = m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}) = m_{\mathbb{L}}(g(0) + \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})
   \]
   by translation invariance of $m_{\mathbb{L}}$. Thus $\alpha = 1$, and $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright (\mathbb{L}, m_{\mathbb{L}})$ is measure-preserving.

### Step 2: Ultrametric identification of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ with a pro-$p$ congruence subgroup $U_0 \le \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$

Define
\[
e := \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } p \ge 3, \\ 2 & \text{if } p = 2, \end{cases}
\qquad q := p^e, \qquad
\Gamma := \ker\big(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\big),
\]
and for each $m \ge e$, let
\[
K_m := \ker\big(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p) \to \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/p^m\mathbb{Z})\big), \qquad U_0 := K_e.
\]

1. **Pro-$p$ filtration with index-$p$ steps:** For every $m \ge e$, any $M \in K_m$ writes uniquely as $M = I + p^m A$ with $A \in M_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. Because $2m \ge m + 1$,
   \[
   1 = \det(I + p^m A) = 1 + p^m \mathrm{tr}(A) + p^{2m}\det(A) \implies \mathrm{tr}(A) \equiv 0 \pmod p,
   \]
   and $(I + p^m A)(I + p^m B) \equiv I + p^m(A + B) \pmod{p^{m+1}}$. Thus $\theta_m(I + p^m A) := A \bmod p$ is a group homomorphism $\theta_m : K_m \to \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{F}_p) = \{X \in M_2(\mathbb{F}_p) : \mathrm{tr}(X) = 0\}$ with $\ker(\theta_m) = K_{m+1}$. It is surjective because the lifts
   \[
   I + p^m E_{12} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & p^m \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
   I + p^m E_{21} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ p^m & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
   \begin{pmatrix} 1 + p^m & 0 \\ 0 & (1 + p^m)^{-1} \end{pmatrix}
   \]
   all lie in $K_m$ (as $p \nmid (1 + p^m)$ and $(1 + p^m)^{-1} \equiv 1 - p^m \pmod{p^{2m}}$, with $2m \ge m+1$) and map to an $\mathbb{F}_p$-basis of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{F}_p) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^3$.

   Choosing a flag $\{0\} = V_3 \subset V_2 \subset V_1 \subset V_0 = \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ of $\mathbb{F}_p$-subspaces of dimensions $0, 1, 2, 3$ and setting $U_{3(m-e)+r} := \theta_m^{-1}(V_r)$ for $m \ge e$ and $r \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ produces a descending chain of open (hence closed) normal subgroups of $U_0$: indeed, for any $g = I + p^e B \in U_0 = K_e$ ($e \ge 1$) and $h = I + p^m A \in K_m$ ($m \ge e$), $g \equiv I \pmod{p}$ implies $g A g^{-1} \equiv A \pmod{p}$ and hence $\theta_m(g h g^{-1}) = \theta_m(h)$, so $U_0$ acts trivially by conjugation on $K_m / K_{m+1}$. Thus:
   \[
   U_0 > U_1 > U_2 > \cdots, \qquad [U_k : U_{k+1}] = p \quad (\forall k \ge 0), \qquad [U_0 : U_k] = p^k, \qquad \bigcap_{k=0}^\infty U_k = \bigcap_{m \ge e} K_m = \{I\}.
   \]

2. **Metric and measure isomorphism $\Phi : (U_0, d_U, m_{U_0}) \to (\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}, d_{\mathbb{L}}, m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})^{-1} m_{\mathbb{L}}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}})$:**
   Equip the compact group $U_0$ with its normalized Haar probability measure $m_{U_0}$ and the ultrametric
   \[
   d_U(u, v) := c^{-v(u, v)} \qquad \text{where} \qquad v(u, v) := \sup\{k \ge 0 : u U_k = v U_k\} \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\} \quad (c^{-\infty} := 0).
   \]
   Left multiplication $L_g(u) := gu$ for $g \in U_0$ satisfies $g u U_k = g v U_k \iff u U_k = v U_k$, so every $L_g$ is a surjective isometry of $(U_0, d_U)$.

   To construct $\Phi$, fix a set of coset representatives $R = \{r_0, \dots, r_{p-1}\} \subset \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{L}} \cong \mathbb{F}_p$ (so $|r_a - r_b| = 1$ for $a \ne b$). Every $x \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ admits a unique convergent expansion $x = \sum_{k=0}^\infty r_{a_k(x)} \pi^k$ ($a_k(x) \in \{0, \dots, p-1\}$), giving a homeomorphism $\psi : \{0, \dots, p-1\}^{\mathbb{N}} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ such that for $a \ne b$ with $k_0 = \min\{k : a_k \ne b_k\}$,
   \[
   |\psi(a) - \psi(b)| = \Big| (r_{a_{k_0}} - r_{b_{k_0}})\pi^{k_0} + \sum_{k > k_0} (r_{a_k} - r_{b_k})\pi^k \Big| = |\pi|^{k_0} = c^{-k_0},
   \]
   because $|(r_{a_{k_0}} - r_{b_{k_0}})\pi^{k_0}| = c^{-k_0}$ while every remaining term has norm $\le c^{-(k_0+1)}$. Similarly, each coset $u U_k$ partitions into exactly $p$ cosets in $U_0 / U_{k+1}$; choosing any bijection from the $p$ children of each $u U_k$ to $\{0, \dots, p-1\}$ yields a bijection $\tau : U_0 \to \{0, \dots, p-1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ (since $U_0$ is compact and $\bigcap_k U_k = \{I\}$) where $\tau(u)$ and $\tau(v)$ agree on indices $0, \dots, k-1$ iff $u U_k = v U_k$. Setting $\Phi := \psi \circ \tau : U_0 \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ gives a bijection satisfying
   \[
   |\Phi(u) - \Phi(v)| = c^{-v(u, v)} = d_U(u, v) \qquad (\forall u, v \in U_0),
   \]
   i.e., $\Phi$ is a **surjective isometry** $(U_0, d_U) \to (\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}, d_{\mathbb{L}})$. Moreover, for every level-$k$ coset $u U_k$, $\Phi(u U_k)$ is a translate of $\pi^k \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$, which has measure $p^{-k} m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}) = m_{U_0}(u U_k)\, m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})$ because $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ is the disjoint union of $p^k$ translates of $\pi^k \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$. Since the cosets $u U_k$ generate the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of $U_0$ (as a countable basis of clopen sets closed under finite intersections), we have
   \[
   \Phi_* m_{U_0} = \frac{1}{m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})}\, m_{\mathbb{L}}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}}.
   \]

3. **Extension of $U_0$-isometries to $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$:** For each $g \in U_0$, define $\tilde{\alpha}_g : \mathbb{L} \to \mathbb{L}$ by
   \[
   \tilde{\alpha}_g(x) := \begin{cases}
   (\Phi \circ L_g \circ \Phi^{-1})(x) & \text{if } x \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}, \\
   x & \text{if } x \in \mathbb{L} \setminus \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}.
   \end{cases}
   \]
   Clearly $\tilde{\alpha}_g$ is a bijection with $\tilde{\alpha}_g(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ and $\tilde{\alpha}_g^{-1} = \tilde{\alpha}_{g^{-1}}$. For $x, y \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$, $|\tilde{\alpha}_g(x) - \tilde{\alpha}_g(y)| = |x - y|$ because $\Phi$ and $L_g$ are isometries; for $x, y \notin \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$, equality is trivial; and if $x \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ while $y \in \mathbb{L} \setminus \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$, then $|x| \le 1 < |y|$ and $|\tilde{\alpha}_g(x)| \le 1 < |y|$, so by the ultrametric property,
   \[
   |\tilde{\alpha}_g(x) - \tilde{\alpha}_g(y)| = |\tilde{\alpha}_g(x) - y| = |y| = |x - y|.
   \]
   Hence $\tilde{\alpha}_g \in \mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$ for every $g \in U_0$.

### Step 3: Local spectral gap and domain of expansion for $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$

We apply Theorem 2.15 (`thm:Golsefidy's_theorem`, `\cite[Theorem 1]{Golsefidy_p-adic}`) to $K = \mathbb{Q}$, $n = 2$, and $\Gamma = \ker(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})) \le \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$:

1. **Finite generation:** Since $|\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) : \Gamma| \le |\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})| < \infty$ and $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is finitely generated, $\Gamma$ is finitely generated (by Schreier's lemma); fix a finite symmetric generating set $S \subseteq \Gamma$ containing the identity $I \in S$.
2. **Closure in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ equals $U_0$:** Because $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \to \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})$ is surjective for every $k \ge e$ (every matrix in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})$ is a product of elementary transvections, as $\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z}$ is local so some entry of its first column is a unit), any $A \in U_0$ admits $B \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ with $B \equiv A \pmod{p^k}$. As $q \mid p^k$ and $A \equiv I \pmod{q}$, we have $B \in \Gamma$. Thus $\Gamma_p := \overline{\Gamma} = U_0$, which is compact, so $p \in \Sigma$.
3. **Semisimple Zariski closure:** Every element of $\Gamma$ has $\det = 1$, and $\Gamma$ contains $\begin{pmatrix}1 & qn \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\ qn & 1\end{pmatrix}$ for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Any polynomial vanishing on $\Gamma$ vanishes identically on both $1$-parameter unipotent subgroups, whose algebraic span is all of $\mathrm{SL}_2$, a semisimple algebraic group.

Therefore, by Theorem 2.15 (`\cite[Theorem 1]{Golsefidy_p-adic}`), $\|T_S|_{L^2_0(U_0, m_{U_0})}\| < 1$, where $T_S(\varphi) := \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{g \in S} g \cdot \varphi$ (note that including $I \in S$ rules out $-1 \in \mathrm{Spec}(T_S|_{L^2_0})$ even at $p = 2$, since $\langle T_S \varphi, \varphi \rangle = \frac{1}{|S|} \|\varphi\|_2^2 + \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{g \in S \setminus \{I\}} \langle g \cdot \varphi, \varphi \rangle \ge \big(-1 + \frac{2}{|S|}\big) \|\varphi\|_2^2$ for all $\varphi$, while still satisfying Theorem 2.15 and adding $0$ to $\sum_{g \in S} \|g \cdot \varphi - \varphi\|_2$). Put $\delta := 1 - \|T_S|_{L^2_0(U_0, m_{U_0})}\| > 0$. For every $\varphi \in L^2_0(U_0, m_{U_0})$,
\[
\delta \|\varphi\|_2^2 \le \langle (I - T_S)\varphi, \varphi \rangle \le \|(I - T_S)\varphi\|_2 \|\varphi\|_2 \le \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{g \in S} \|g \cdot \varphi - \varphi\|_2 \|\varphi\|_2,
\]
so $\|\varphi\|_2 \le \kappa \sum_{g \in S} \|g \cdot \varphi - \varphi\|_2$ with $\kappa := \frac{1}{\delta |S|} > 0$.

Now let $\tilde{S} := \{\tilde{\alpha}_g : g \in S\} \subseteq \mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$, and let $f \in L^2(\mathbb{L}, m_{\mathbb{L}})$ satisfy $\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}} f \, dm_{\mathbb{L}} = 0$. Setting $\varphi := (f|_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}}) \circ \Phi \in L^2(U_0, m_{U_0})$, the relation $\Phi_* m_{U_0} = m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})^{-1} m_{\mathbb{L}}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}}$ gives $\int_{U_0} \varphi \, dm_{U_0} = 0$ and $\|\varphi\|_2 = m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})^{-1/2} \|f\|_{2, \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}}$. Moreover, for every $g \in S$ and $u \in U_0$, since $\tilde{\alpha}_g^{-1}(\Phi(u)) = \Phi(g^{-1}u) \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$, we have $((\tilde{\alpha}_g \cdot f)|_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}} \circ \Phi)(u) = (g \cdot \varphi)(u)$, so $\|\tilde{\alpha}_g \cdot f - f\|_{2, \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}} = m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})^{1/2} \|g \cdot \varphi - \varphi\|_2$. Multiplying $\|\varphi\|_2 \le \kappa \sum_{g \in S} \|g \cdot \varphi - \varphi\|_2$ by $m_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}})^{1/2}$ yields
\[
\|f\|_{2, \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}} \le \kappa \sum_{s \in \tilde{S}} \|s \cdot f - f\|_{2, \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}}.
\]
Thus $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright (\mathbb{L}, m_{\mathbb{L}})$ has local spectral gap with respect to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ (Definition 2.13). By Lemma 2.16 (`lem:LSG=domain`, `\cite[Lemma 5.2]{GrMaPikhurko}`), $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ is a **domain of expansion** for $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright (\mathbb{L}, m_{\mathbb{L}})$.

### Step 4: Every $C \in \mathcal{B}_{m_{\mathbb{L}}} \cap \mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright \mathbb{L})$ is a domain of expansion

First note that $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}} \in \mathcal{B}_{m_{\mathbb{L}}} \cap \mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright \mathbb{L})$ (it is compact, open, and $\mathrm{id}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$). Moreover, $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$ contains all translations $\{x \mapsto x + a : a \in \mathbb{L}\}$, which act transitively on $\mathbb{L}$. Therefore, any two sets $A, B \in \mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright \mathbb{L})$ $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$-cover each other: if $B$ $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$-covers a nonempty open set $U$, then $\{a + U : a \in \mathbb{L}\}$ is an open cover of the compact set $\overline{A}$, so finitely many translates of $U$ (and hence finitely many $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$-translates of $B$) cover $A$.

Hence Remark 2.11 (`rem:expanding_one_set`, `\cite[Lemma 2.5]{GrMaPikhurko}`) already implies that $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright (\mathbb{L}, m_{\mathbb{L}})$ is expanding. For complete self-containedness directly from Definition 2.8 (`def:domain_of_exp`(i)), let $C \in \mathcal{B}_{m_{\mathbb{L}}} \cap \mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright \mathbb{L})$ be arbitrary. Since $\overline{C}$ is compact, $m_{\mathbb{L}}(C) < \infty$; since $C$ covers a nonempty open set $U$ via finitely many isometries and $m_{\mathbb{L}}(U) > 0$, $m_{\mathbb{L}}(C) > 0$. Because $C$ and $C_0 := \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{L}}$ $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$-cover each other, there exist finite families $g_1, \dots, g_k \in \mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$ and $h_1, \dots, h_m \in \mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$ such that
\[
C \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^k g_i \cdot C_0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad C_0 \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^m h_j \cdot C.
\]
Given any $\eta \in (0, 1)$, set $\eta' := \min\big(\frac{\eta\, m_{\mathbb{L}}(C)}{k\, m_{\mathbb{L}}(C_0)}, \frac{\eta}{k m}\big) > 0$. Since $C_0$ is a domain of expansion (Definition 2.8(i)), there is a finite subset $R_0 \subseteq \mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L})$ witnessing $\eta'$ on $C_0$. Define the finite set
\[
R := \{g_i r g_{i_0}^{-1} : 1 \le i, i_0 \le k,\, r \in R_0\} \cup \{h_j^{-1} r g_{i_0}^{-1} : 1 \le j \le m,\, 1 \le i_0 \le k,\, r \in R_0\} \subseteq \mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}).
\]
Let $Y \subseteq C$ be $m_{\mathbb{L}}$-measurable. Choose $i_0 \in \{1, \dots, k\}$ maximizing $m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y \cap g_{i_0} \cdot C_0)$, so $Y' := g_{i_0}^{-1}(Y \cap g_{i_0} \cdot C_0) \subseteq C_0$ satisfies $m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y') \ge m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y)/k$. Put $E := (R_0 \cdot Y') \cap C_0$. Then:
- Either $m_{\mathbb{L}}(E) \ge (1 - \eta') m_{\mathbb{L}}(C_0)$, so $m_{\mathbb{L}}(C_0 \setminus E) \le \eta' m_{\mathbb{L}}(C_0)$. Since $\bigcup_{i=1}^k g_i \cdot E \subseteq R \cdot Y$ and $C \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^k g_i \cdot E \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^k g_i \cdot (C_0 \setminus E)$, measure preservation gives
  \[
  m_{\mathbb{L}}(C \setminus (R \cdot Y)) \le k\, m_{\mathbb{L}}(C_0 \setminus E) \le k \eta' m_{\mathbb{L}}(C_0) \le \eta\, m_{\mathbb{L}}(C),
  \]
  whence $m_{\mathbb{L}}((R \cdot Y) \cap C) \ge (1 - \eta) m_{\mathbb{L}}(C)$.
- Or $m_{\mathbb{L}}(E) \ge m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y')/\eta' \ge m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y)/(k\eta')$. Since $C_0 \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^m h_j \cdot C$, there exists $j \in \{1, \dots, m\}$ with $m_{\mathbb{L}}(E \cap h_j \cdot C) \ge \frac{1}{m} m_{\mathbb{L}}(E)$. Then $h_j^{-1}(E \cap h_j \cdot C) \subseteq (R \cdot Y) \cap C$, yielding
  \[
  m_{\mathbb{L}}((R \cdot Y) \cap C) \ge \frac{m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y)}{k m \eta'} \ge \frac{m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y)}{\eta}.
  \]

Thus $m_{\mathbb{L}}((R \cdot Y) \cap C) \ge \min\{(1-\eta)m_{\mathbb{L}}(C), m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y)/\eta\}$ for all $Y \subseteq C$ in $\mathcal{B}_{m_{\mathbb{L}}}$ (and for $\eta \ge 1$, taking $R = \{\mathrm{id}\}$ already gives $m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y) \ge m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y)/\eta \ge \min\{(1-\eta)m_{\mathbb{L}}(C), m_{\mathbb{L}}(Y)/\eta\}$). Therefore every $C \in \mathcal{B}_{m_{\mathbb{L}}} \cap \mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright \mathbb{L})$ is a domain of expansion, so $\mathrm{Iso}(\mathbb{L}) \curvearrowright (\mathbb{L}, m_{\mathbb{L}})$ is expanding. $\blacksquare$
