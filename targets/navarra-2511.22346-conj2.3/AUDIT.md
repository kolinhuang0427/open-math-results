## 2. Mathematical Verification of `COUNTEREXAMPLE.md`
Checked every claim independently by hand and via exact symbolic computation:
1. **Validity of $\mathcal{P}$:** $\mathcal{P}$ consists of $10$ unit cells $[(x,y),(x+1,y+1)]$ in $\{1,2,3,4\}^2 \subset \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2 \subset \mathbb{N}^2$, forming a single weakly connected component (two edge-connected $5$-cell components sharing vertices $(2,2)$, $(3,3)$, and $(4,4)$). Its vertex set $V(\mathcal{P}) = \{1,\dots,5\}^2 \setminus \{(1,1),(1,5),(5,1),(5,5)\}$ has $|V(\mathcal{P})| = 21$.
2. **Complete enumeration of proper inner intervals:** Exhaustive inspection of $\{1,2,3,4\}^2$ confirms $\mathcal{P}$ contains no $2\times 2$ square and no $1\times 3$ or $3\times 1$ rectangle (every row/column of length $\ge 3$ has a gap at $(2,2)$ or $(3,3)$). Thus the proper inner intervals are precisely the $10$ unit cells, $4$ horizontal $2\times 1$ intervals, and $4$ vertical $1\times 2$ intervals ($18$ in total).
3. **Elementary proof valid over every field $K$:** Let $Z = \{2,3,4\}^2 \subset V(\mathcal{P})$ ($|Z| = 9$) and $\mathfrak{p} = (x_v : v \in Z) \subset S_{\mathcal{P}}$. Every one of the $18$ inner intervals has at least one diagonal corner and at least one anti-diagonal corner in $Z$ (geometrically, any proper interval containing two vertices in $V(\mathcal{P}) \setminus Z$ would either span width/height $4$ or contain one of the four missing corner cells $(1,1),(1,4),(4,1),(4,4)$). Hence $I_{\mathcal{P}} \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$, so $d = \dim K[\mathcal{P}] \ge \dim(S_{\mathcal{P}}/\mathfrak{p}) = 21 - 9 = 12$. Since $I_{\mathcal{P}}$ is homogeneous and generated in degree $2$, $\dim_K K[\mathcal{P}]_0 = 1$ and $\dim_K K[\mathcal{P}]_1 = 21$. Expanding $\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t) = h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t)/(1-t)^d$ gives $h_0 = 1$ and $h_1 + d = 21$, so $h_1 = 21 - d \le 9$. Meanwhile $\tilde{r}_1(\mathcal{P}) = |\mathcal{P}| = 10$ (every cell is a distinct $1$-rook configuration with no switches on $1$ rook). Therefore $h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t) \neq \tilde{r}_{\mathcal{P}}(t)$ over every field $K$.
4. **Exact invariants & field independence:** Since $I_{\mathcal{P}}$ is generated by pure-difference binomials $m_1 - m_2$, Buchberger's algorithm produces only pure-difference binomials (coefficients $\pm 1$), making the reduced Gröbner basis, $\mathrm{in}_<(I_{\mathcal{P}})$, $\dim K[\mathcal{P}]$, and $h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t)$ independent of $\mathrm{char}(K)$.
