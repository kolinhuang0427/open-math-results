# Counterexample to Navarra, Qureshi & Rinaldo (2025), Conjecture 2.3 (`conj`)

**Target:** `arXiv:2511.22346v1`, Section 2, Conjecture 2.3 (`\label{conj}`, `HILBERT_Series_NEW.tex` lines 645–647).

## Counterexample

Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the weakly connected collection of $10$ unit cells $C = [(x,y),(x+1,y+1)]$ inside $\{1,2,3,4\}^2 \subset \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}^2$ given by the $10$ lower-left corners:
\[
\mathcal{P} = \bigl\{(1,2), (1,3), (2,1), (2,3), (2,4), (3,1), (3,2), (3,4), (4,2), (4,3)\bigr\}.
\]
Visually ($y=4$ at top, $y=1$ at bottom, $x=1,\dots,4$ left to right):
```
y=4: . # # .
y=3: # # . #
y=2: # . # #
y=1: . # # .
     1 2 3 4
```
(This is a $4\times 4$ grid with the four corner cells $(1,1),(1,4),(4,1),(4,4)$ and the two central cells $(2,2),(3,3)$ removed.)

Then $|\mathcal{P}| = 10$ and $|V(\mathcal{P})| = 21$ (the $5\times 5$ grid $\{1,\dots,5\}^2$ minus the four outer corners $(1,1),(1,5),(5,1),(5,5)$).

## Elementary Proof that $h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t) \neq \tilde{r}_{\mathcal{P}}(t)$ over Every Field $K$

1. **All $18$ inner intervals of $\mathcal{P}$:**
   $\mathcal{P}$ contains no $2\times 2$ block, so its proper inner intervals $[a,b]$ are precisely the $10$ unit cells ($1\times 1$) together with the $8$ pairs of edge-adjacent cells ($1\times 2$ or $2\times 1$):
   - $1\times 1$: $[(1,2),(2,3)]$, $[(1,3),(2,4)]$, $[(2,1),(3,2)]$, $[(2,3),(3,4)]$, $[(2,4),(3,5)]$, $[(3,1),(4,2)]$, $[(3,2),(4,3)]$, $[(3,4),(4,5)]$, $[(4,2),(5,3)]$, $[(4,3),(5,4)]$.
   - $1\times 2$: $[(1,2),(2,4)]$, $[(2,3),(3,5)]$, $[(3,1),(4,3)]$, $[(4,2),(5,4)]$.
   - $2\times 1$: $[(1,3),(3,4)]$, $[(2,4),(4,5)]$, $[(2,1),(4,2)]$, $[(3,2),(5,3)]$.

2. **A $12$-dimensional prime ideal containing $I_{\mathcal{P}}$:**
   Let $Z = \{2,3,4\} \times \{2,3,4\} \subset V(\mathcal{P})$ (note $|Z| = 9$), and consider the prime monomial ideal $\mathfrak{p} = (x_v : v \in Z) \subset S_{\mathcal{P}}$.
   By direct inspection of all $18$ inner intervals $[a,b]$ with diagonal corners $a,b$ and anti-diagonal corners $c,d$:
   - $[(1,2),(2,3)]$: $(2,3),(2,2) \in Z$
   - $[(1,3),(2,4)]$: $(2,4),(2,3) \in Z$
   - $[(2,1),(3,2)]$: $(3,2),(2,2) \in Z$
   - $[(2,3),(3,4)]$: all four corners in $Z$
   - $[(2,4),(3,5)]$: $(2,4),(3,4) \in Z$
   - $[(3,1),(4,2)]$: $(4,2),(3,2) \in Z$
   - $[(3,2),(4,3)]$: all four corners in $Z$
   - $[(3,4),(4,5)]$: $(3,4),(4,4) \in Z$
   - $[(4,2),(5,3)]$: $(4,2),(4,3) \in Z$
   - $[(4,3),(5,4)]$: $(4,3),(4,4) \in Z$
   - $[(1,2),(2,4)]$: $(2,4),(2,2) \in Z$
   - $[(2,3),(3,5)]$: $(2,3),(3,3) \in Z$
   - $[(3,1),(4,3)]$: $(4,3),(3,3) \in Z$
   - $[(4,2),(5,4)]$: $(4,2),(4,4) \in Z$
   - $[(1,3),(3,4)]$: $(3,4),(3,3) \in Z$
   - $[(2,4),(4,5)]$: $(2,4),(4,4) \in Z$
   - $[(2,1),(4,2)]$: $(4,2),(2,2) \in Z$
   - $[(3,2),(5,3)]$: $(3,2),(3,3) \in Z$

   Every generator $x_a x_b - x_c x_d$ of $I_{\mathcal{P}}$ has both $\{a,b\} \cap Z \neq \emptyset$ and $\{c,d\} \cap Z \neq \emptyset$, so $I_{\mathcal{P}} \subseteq \mathfrak{p}$.
   Since $S_{\mathcal{P}}/\mathfrak{p} \cong K[x_v : v \in V(\mathcal{P}) \setminus Z]$ is a polynomial ring in $21 - 9 = 12$ variables, we have
   \[
   d := \dim K[\mathcal{P}] \ge \dim(S_{\mathcal{P}}/\mathfrak{p}) = 12.
   \]

3. **Contradiction on the linear coefficient of the $h$-polynomial:**
   Because $I_{\mathcal{P}}$ is homogeneous and generated in degree $2$, there are no degree-$1$ relations, so $\dim_K K[\mathcal{P}]_1 = |V(\mathcal{P})| = 21$. Writing $\mathrm{HP}_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t) = \frac{h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t)}{(1-t)^d}$ with $h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(0) = 1$, comparing the coefficient of $t^1$ gives
   \[
   21 = \dim_K K[\mathcal{P}]_1 = h_1 + d \implies h_1 = 21 - d \le 21 - 12 = 9.
   \]
   On the other hand, the linear coefficient of the switching rook polynomial $\tilde{r}_{\mathcal{P}}(t)$ is $\tilde{r}_1(\mathcal{P}) = |\mathcal{P}| = 10$ (every cell gives a distinct $1$-rook configuration, with no switches on $1$ rook). Since $h_1 \le 9 < 10 = \tilde{r}_1(\mathcal{P})$, we have $h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t) \neq \tilde{r}_{\mathcal{P}}(t)$ over every field $K$.

## Exact Invariants (Computed by `verify.py`)

Because $I_{\mathcal{P}}$ is generated by pure-difference binomials $m_1 - m_2$, Buchberger's algorithm only forms pure-difference binomials (coefficients $\pm 1$), so the reduced Gröbner basis and initial monomial ideal $\mathrm{in}_{<_{\mathrm{grevlex}}}(I_{\mathcal{P}})$ are independent of the field $K$. Exact computation gives:
- $\dim K[\mathcal{P}] = 12$.
- $h_{K[\mathcal{P}]}(t) = 1 + 9t + 27t^2 + 25t^3 - 16t^4 - 33t^5 - 11t^6 - t^7$ (which even has negative coefficients, proving $K[\mathcal{P}]$ is not Cohen–Macaulay).
- $\tilde{r}_{\mathcal{P}}(t) = 1 + 10t + 37t^2 + 62t^3 + 46t^4 + 12t^5 + t^6$ (with rook number $r(\mathcal{P}) = 6$).
