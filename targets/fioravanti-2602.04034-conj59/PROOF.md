# Proof: Uniform Generation of Clonoids over All Finite Abelian $p$-Groups and the Coprime-Order Characterization for Finite Modules

**Primary Source:** Stefano Fioravanti, Michael Kompatscher, and Bernardo Rossi, *Clonoids over vector spaces*, `arXiv:2602.04034v1` (3 February 2026):
- Section 7 (*Conclusion*), **Conjecture 59** (`\label{conjecture:strong}`, `main.tex` lines 1511–1515, p. 29);
- Section 1 (*Introduction*), **Conjecture 1** (`\label{conjecture:main}`, `main.tex` lines 221–223, p. 2).

## Exact Conjectures Settled

> **Conjecture 59 (`conjecture:strong`).** *Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a finite abelian $p$-group, and let $\mathbf{R}$ be a ring (considered as regular $\mathbf{R}$-module), such that $p$ is invertible in $\mathbf{R}$. Then, there is a $k \in \mathbb{N}$, such that $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{R}}$ is uniformly generated by $k$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{R})$-minors.*

> **Conjecture 1 (`conjecture:main`).** *Let $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ be finite modules. Then, there are only finitely many clonoids from $\mathbf{A}$ to $\mathbf{B}$, if and only if $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are of coprime order.*

## Main Results

We prove **both** conjectures in full generality, with an explicit linear upper bound on the generating arity $k$:

> **Theorem 1 (Proof of Conjecture 59 / `conjecture:strong`).**
> *Let $p$ be a prime, let $\mathbf{A}$ be a finite abelian $p$-group, and let $\mathbf{R}$ be any unital ring (considered as a regular left $\mathbf{R}$-module) in which $p \cdot 1_R$ is invertible. Set*
> $$k(\mathbf{A}) := \max\big(1,\; \log_p |\mathbf{A}|\big) \in \mathbb{N}.$$
> *Then $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{R}}$ is uniformly generated by $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{R})$-minors.*

> **Theorem 2 (General Coprime Modules and Proof of Conjecture 1 / `conjecture:main`).**
> 1. *Let $\mathbf{A}$ be any finite module (over any unital ring), with primary decomposition $\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \prod_{j = 1}^r \mathbf{A}_{p_j}$ of its underlying abelian group into Sylow $p_j$-subgroups ($r \ge 0$), and put*
>    $$k(\mathbf{A}) := \max\big(\{1\} \cup \{\log_{p_j} |\mathbf{A}_{p_j}| \mid 1 \le j \le r\}\big) \in \mathbb{N}.$$
>    *Let $\mathbf{R}$ be any unital ring in which $|A| \cdot 1_R$ is invertible, and let $\mathbf{B}$ be any left $\mathbf{R}$-module. Then $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}}$ is uniformly generated by $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-minors, and every $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-clonoid is generated by its $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary part.*
> 2. **(Conjecture 1 / `conjecture:main`.)** *Let $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ be finite modules. Then there are only finitely many clonoids from $\mathbf{A}$ to $\mathbf{B}$ if and only if $|\mathbf{A}|$ and $|\mathbf{B}|$ are coprime. Moreover, when $\gcd(|\mathbf{A}|, |\mathbf{B}|) = 1$, every $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-clonoid is generated by its $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary part and is the polymorphism minion of relational structures of maximal arity at most $|A|^{k(\mathbf{A})}$.*

*(Note: By Lemma 27 (`lem:unifgenforpolyequiv`) of `arXiv:2602.04034v1`, Theorem 2(1)–(2) also extends immediately to finite algebras polynomially equivalent to finite modules of coprime order, with generating arity $k(\mathbf{A}) + 1$.)*

## 1. Preliminaries and Notation

We adhere strictly to the definitions and conventions of `arXiv:2602.04034v1`, Sections 2–3:

- **Rings and modules (Section 2.2):** Every ring $\mathbf{R} = (R, +, -, 0, \cdot, 1)$ is associative and unital (not necessarily commutative, and possibly trivial with $1_R = 0_R$), and modules are one-sorted left $\mathbf{R}$-modules $(B, +, 0, -, (r)_{r \in R})$ with $r(b) = r b$. The regular left $\mathbf{R}$-module has universe $R$ and left multiplication by elements of $R$. Every left $\mathbf{R}$-module $\mathbf{B}$ with annihilator $I_B = \{r \in R \mid \forall b \in B,\; r b = 0\}$ is term-equivalent to the faithful left $(\mathbf{R}/I_B)$-module $(B, +, 0, -, (r + I_B)_{r \in R})$.
- **Central homomorphism from $S = \mathbb{Z}[1/p]$:** Fix a prime $p$ and set $S := \mathbb{Z}[1/p] \subset \mathbb{Q}$. For any unital ring $\mathbf{R}$ in which $p \cdot 1_R$ is invertible, $p \cdot 1_R$ lies in the center $Z(\mathbf{R})$ (as $(p \cdot 1_R) r = r + \dots + r = r (p \cdot 1_R)$ for all $r \in R$). Multiplying on both sides by $(p \cdot 1_R)^{-1}$ shows $(p \cdot 1_R)^{-1} \in Z(\mathbf{R})$ as well. Hence
  $$\iota \colon S \longrightarrow Z(\mathbf{R}) \subseteq \mathbf{R}, \qquad \iota\!\left(\frac{a}{p^t}\right) := (a \cdot 1_R)\big((p \cdot 1_R)^{-1}\big)^t \quad (a \in \mathbb{Z},\; t \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}),$$
  is a well-defined unital ring homomorphism into $Z(\mathbf{R})$.
- **Abelian $p$-groups as modules and matrix actions:** Any finite abelian $p$-group $\mathbf{A} = (A, +, 0, -)$ of exponent $p^e$ ($e \ge 1$) is term-equivalent to a faithful $\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z}$-module. For any $m, l \in \mathbb{N}$, the term clone $\mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A})$ yields $(\mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A})^{(m)})^l = \{X \mapsto M X \mid M \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{l \times m}\}$, where $X = (a_1, \dots, a_m)^T \in A^m$ is viewed as a column of $m$ elements of $A$. The *inner rank* $\mathrm{rk}(M)$ of $M \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{l \times m}$ is the least $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ such that $M = U V$ for some $U \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{l \times n}$ and $V \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{n \times m}$, and
  $$R_n^{m, l}(\mathbf{A}) = \big\{ X \mapsto M X \;\big|\; M \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{l \times m},\; \mathrm{rk}(M) \le n \big\}.$$
  When $\mathbf{A}$ is decomposed as $\mathbf{A} = (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^s \times \mathbf{A}'$ with $p^{e-1}\mathbf{A}' = \{0\}$ ($e \ge 2$, $s \ge 1$), we write a tuple $X \in A^m$ as a pair $(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{m \times s} \times (\mathbf{A}')^m$ whose $i$-th row is $(x_i, y_i) \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^s \times \mathbf{A}'$. For any matrix $M \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{l \times m}$, its action is
  $$M X = \big(M \mathbf{X},\; \widetilde{M} \mathbf{Y}\big) \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{l \times s} \times (\mathbf{A}')^l,$$
  where throughout we use **tilde** $\widetilde{\cdot}$ for reduction modulo $p^{e-1}$ (into $\mathbb{Z}/p^{e-1}\mathbb{Z}$) and **bar** $\bar{\cdot}$ for reduction modulo $p$ (into $\mathbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$). Note that because $e \ge 2$, reduction modulo $p^{e-1}$ refines reduction modulo $p$, and $p^{e-1} a$ for $a \in \mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z}$ depends only on $\bar{a} \in \mathbb{F}_p$, giving a canonical isomorphism $\mathbb{F}_p \xrightarrow{\sim} p^{e-1}(\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})$, $\bar{a} \mapsto p^{e-1} a$.
- **Uniform generation and representation (Section 3 of `arXiv:2602.04034v1`):**
  For a finite abelian $p$-group $\mathbf{A}$ and $S = \mathbb{Z}[1/p]$, an operator $I \colon \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ is *$(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-uniformly representable* ($(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR; Definition 15 `def:unifrep`) if there exist finitely many $\alpha_i \in S$ and $M_i \in R_n^{m, m}(\mathbf{A})$ such that $I(f)(X) = \sum_i \alpha_i f(M_i X)$ for all $f \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ and $X \in A^m$. A set $U \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ is *$(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-uniformly generated* ($(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UG; Definition 14 `def:unifgen`) iff $\mathrm{id}|_U$ is $(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR, and $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}$ is $(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UG iff $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ is $(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UG for every $m \in \mathbb{N}$. We use the following structural facts from `arXiv:2602.04034v1`:
  - **Lemma 12 (`lemma:rkprod`):** $r \in R_n^{j, k}(\mathbf{A})$, $t \in R_q^{k, l}(\mathbf{A}) \implies t \circ r \in R_{\min(n, q)}^{j, l}(\mathbf{A})$.
  - **Lemma 17 (`lemma:urobs`):** $(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR operators are monotone in $n$ (item 2), closed under $S$-linear combinations (item 3), and closed under composition with $(n_2, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR operators at rank $\min(n, n_2)$ (item 4).
  - **Theorem 18 (`theorem:unigen`):** For $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}$ (trivially closed under constant operations on $A$) and $n \ge 1$, if $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(n+1)}$ is $(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UG then $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}$ is $(n, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UG.
  - **Theorem 49 (`theorem:main`):** For a finite field $\mathbf{F}$, $s \in \mathbb{N}$, and an $\mathbf{R}$-module $\mathbf{B}$ with $|F| \cdot 1_R \in R^\times$, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}^s, \mathbf{B}}$ is uniformly generated by $s$-ary $(\mathbf{F}^s, \mathbf{B})$-minors.

## 2. Auxiliary Lemmas

### Lemma 1 (Higher-Codimension Subspace Radon Inversion on $\mathbb{F}_p^m$)
Let $p$ be a prime, $S = \mathbb{Z}[1/p]$, and let $m > s \ge 1$ be integers. For any subspace $U \le \mathbb{F}_p^m$, set
$$\mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(U) := \big\{ W \in \mathbb{F}_p^{m \times s} \;\big|\; \mathrm{colspan}(W) \subseteq U \big\},$$
so $|\mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(U)| = |U|^s = p^{s \dim U}$. For integers $n \ge 0$ and $c \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $\binom{n}{c}_p$ denote the Gaussian binomial coefficient (the number of $c$-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_p^n$, with $\binom{n}{c}_p = 0$ if $c < 0$ or $c > n$, and $\binom{n}{0}_p = 1$). Then:
1. For every $r \in \{0, 1, \dots, s\}$,
   $$\sum_{c = 0}^s (-1)^{s - c} p^{\binom{s - c + 1}{2} - (m - c) s} \binom{m - 1 - c}{s - c}_p \binom{m - r}{c}_p = \delta_{r, 0}.$$
2. For every function $\phi \colon \mathbb{F}_p^{m \times s} \to S$,
   $$\phi(0) = \sum_{c = 0}^s (-1)^{s - c} p^{\binom{s - c + 1}{2}} \binom{m - 1 - c}{s - c}_p \sum_{\substack{U \le \mathbb{F}_p^m \\ \mathrm{codim}(U) = c}} \left( \frac{1}{|U|^s} \sum_{W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(U)} \phi(W) \right).$$
   Every outer coefficient $(-1)^{s - c} p^{\binom{s - c + 1}{2}} \binom{m - 1 - c}{s - c}_p$ is an integer, and every inner denominator $|U|^s = p^{(m - c) s}$ is a power of $p$.
3. For any two subspaces $V_1, V_2 \le \mathbb{F}_p^m$ and any function $\phi \colon \mathbb{F}_p^{m \times s} \to S$,
   $$\frac{1}{|V_1|^s} \sum_{W_1 \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(V_1)} \left( \frac{1}{|V_2|^s} \sum_{W_2 \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(V_2)} \phi(W_1 + W_2) \right) = \frac{1}{|V_1 + V_2|^s} \sum_{W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(V_1 + V_2)} \phi(W).$$

**Proof.**
1. Recall the $q$-Pascal recurrence for all $n \ge 1$ and $c \in \mathbb{Z}$:
   $$\binom{n}{c}_p = \binom{n - 1}{c}_p + p^{n - c} \binom{n - 1}{c - 1}_p. \tag{P1}$$
   *(Verification of (P1): for $c < 0$ or $c > n$ both sides are $0$; for $c = 0$ both are $1$; for $c = n$ both are $1$; for $1 \le c \le n - 1$, from $\binom{n}{c}_p = \prod_{i=0}^{c-1} \frac{p^{n-i}-1}{p^{i+1}-1}$ we have $\binom{n-1}{c}_p + p^{n-c}\binom{n-1}{c-1}_p = \binom{n-1}{c-1}_p \big(\frac{p^{n-c}-1}{p^c-1} + p^{n-c}\big) = \binom{n-1}{c-1}_p \frac{p^n - 1}{p^c - 1} = \binom{n}{c}_p$.)*

   We establish two formal power series identities in $\mathbb{Z}[[t]]$ by induction using (P1):
   - *Finite Cauchy product ($n \ge 0$):*
     $$\prod_{j = 0}^{n - 1} (1 + p^j t) = \sum_{c = 0}^n p^{\binom{c}{2}} \binom{n}{c}_p t^c. \tag{C1}$$
     The base case $n = 0$ is $1 = 1$. Multiplying (C1) at $n - 1$ by $(1 + p^{n-1} t)$, the coefficient of $t^c$ is $p^{\binom{c}{2}} \binom{n-1}{c}_p + p^{n - 1 + \binom{c-1}{2}} \binom{n-1}{c-1}_p = p^{\binom{c}{2}} \big(\binom{n-1}{c}_p + p^{n-c}\binom{n-1}{c-1}_p\big) = p^{\binom{c}{2}}\binom{n}{c}_p$ by (P1) (using $n - 1 + \binom{c-1}{2} - \binom{c}{2} = n - c$ for $c \ge 1$, and noting the second term vanishes at $c = 0$).
   - *Reciprocal Cauchy series ($M \ge 1$):*
     $$\frac{1}{\prod_{j = 0}^{M - 1} (1 + p^j t)} = \sum_{d = 0}^\infty (-1)^d \binom{M + d - 1}{d}_p t^d. \tag{C2}$$
     At $M = 1$, $(1 + t)^{-1} = \sum_{d = 0}^\infty (-1)^d t^d = \sum_{d = 0}^\infty (-1)^d \binom{d}{d}_p t^d$. For $M \ge 2$, multiplying $\sum_{d = 0}^\infty (-1)^d \binom{M + d - 1}{d}_p t^d$ by $(1 + p^{M - 1} t)$ gives constant term $1 = \binom{M - 2}{0}_p$ and, for $d \ge 1$, coefficient of $t^d$ equal to
     $$(-1)^d \left(\binom{M + d - 1}{d}_p - p^{M - 1} \binom{M + d - 2}{d - 1}_p\right) = (-1)^d \binom{M + d - 2}{d}_p$$
     by (P1) with $(n, c) = (M + d - 1, d)$ (since $n - c = M - 1$). By induction on $M$, this proves (C2).

   Now put $M := m - s \ge 1$ and fix $r \in \{0, 1, \dots, s\}$, so $M \le m - r \le M + s$. Multiplying (C1) at $n = m - r$ by (C2) at $M = m - s$ in $\mathbb{Z}[[t]]$ cancels the first $M$ factors and yields the polynomial identity
   $$\left(\sum_{c = 0}^{m - r} p^{\binom{c}{2}} \binom{m - r}{c}_p t^c\right)\left(\sum_{d = 0}^\infty (-1)^d \binom{m - s + d - 1}{d}_p t^d\right) = \frac{\prod_{j = 0}^{m - r - 1} (1 + p^j t)}{\prod_{j = 0}^{m - s - 1} (1 + p^j t)} = \prod_{j = m - s}^{m - r - 1} (1 + p^j t).$$
   Extract the coefficient of $t^s$ on both sides:
   - On the left-hand side, substituting $d = s - c$ (so $m - s + d - 1 = m - 1 - c \ge m - 1 - s = M - 1 \ge 0$, and $\binom{m - r}{c}_p = 0$ whenever $c > m - r$) gives
     $$[t^s]\,\mathrm{LHS} = \sum_{c = 0}^s (-1)^{s - c} p^{\binom{c}{2}} \binom{m - 1 - c}{s - c}_p \binom{m - r}{c}_p.$$
   - On the right-hand side, $\prod_{j = m - s}^{m - r - 1} (1 + p^j t)$ is a polynomial of degree $(m - r) - (m - s) = s - r$:
     - if $1 \le r \le s$, its degree is $s - r < s$, so $[t^s]\,\mathrm{RHS} = 0$;
     - if $r = 0$, its degree is $s$, and its leading coefficient is $\prod_{j = m - s}^{m - 1} p^j = p^{s(m - s) + \binom{s}{2}}$.

   Dividing both sides by $p^{s(m - s) + \binom{s}{2}}$ and using the exact exponent identity
   $$\binom{c}{2} - \binom{s}{2} - s(m - s) = \frac{c^2 - c - s^2 + s}{2} - ms + s^2 = \frac{(s - c)(s - c + 1)}{2} - (m - c)s = \binom{s - c + 1}{2} - (m - c)s$$
   proves Part (1).

2. Expand the right-hand side of Part (2) by collecting the coefficient of $\phi(W)$ for each $W \in \mathbb{F}_p^{m \times s}$. Let $V_W := \mathrm{colspan}(W) \le \mathbb{F}_p^m$ and $r := \dim(V_W) = \rk_{\mathbb{F}_p}(W) \in \{0, 1, \dots, s\}$. For any $c \in \{0, 1, \dots, s\}$, the subspaces $U \le \mathbb{F}_p^m$ of codimension $c$ satisfying $W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(U)$ (equivalently $V_W \subseteq U$) correspond bijectively via $U \mapsto U / V_W$ to codimension-$c$ subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_p^m / V_W \cong \mathbb{F}_p^{m - r}$, and hence by orthogonal complementation to $c$-dimensional subspaces of $(\mathbb{F}_p^{m - r})^* \cong \mathbb{F}_p^{m - r}$. There are precisely $\binom{m - r}{c}_p$ such subspaces (including $0$ when $c > m - r$), and each has $|U|^s = p^{(m - c) s}$. Hence the coefficient of $\phi(W)$ on the right-hand side equals
   $$\sum_{c = 0}^s (-1)^{s - c} p^{\binom{s - c + 1}{2} - (m - c) s} \binom{m - 1 - c}{s - c}_p \binom{m - r}{c}_p = \delta_{r, 0}$$
   by Part (1). Since $\rk_{\mathbb{F}_p}(W) = 0 \iff W = 0$, the entire expression reduces to $\phi(0)$.

3. The addition map $\alpha \colon \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(V_1) \times \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(V_2) \to \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(V_1 + V_2)$, $(W_1, W_2) \mapsto W_1 + W_2$, is $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear and surjective (columnwise by definition of $V_1 + V_2$), with kernel $\{(W, -W) \mid W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(V_1 \cap V_2)\}$ of cardinality $|V_1 \cap V_2|^s$. Thus every $W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(V_1 + V_2)$ has exactly $|V_1 \cap V_2|^s = \frac{|V_1|^s |V_2|^s}{|V_1 + V_2|^s}$ preimages under $\alpha$. Dividing by $|V_1|^s |V_2|^s$ yields Part (3). $\square$

### Lemma 2 (Subgroup-Peeling Lemma)
Let $p$ be a prime, $S = \mathbb{Z}[1/p]$, and $\mathbf{A}$ be a finite abelian $p$-group of exponent $p^e$ ($e \ge 1$). Let $C_1, \dots, C_L < \mathbf{A}$ ($L \ge 1$) be subgroups of $\mathbf{A}$, and suppose $k \in \mathbb{N}$ is such that $\mathcal{O}_{C_i, S}$ is uniformly generated by $k$-ary $(C_i, S)$-minors for every $i \in \{1, \dots, L\}$. Then for every $m \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists a $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR operator $J_{\mathrm{prop}} \colon \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ such that
$$J_{\mathrm{prop}}(f)(X) = f(X) \qquad \text{for all } f \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)} \text{ and all } X \in \bigcup_{i = 1}^L C_i^m.$$

*(Remark: This is the module specialization of Lemma 30 (`lemma:ugsubalgebras`) of `arXiv:2602.04034v1` / [MW24, Lemma 2.14(3)], stated here at arbitrary arity $m \in \mathbb{N}$.)*

**Proof.**
Fix $m \in \mathbb{N}$. For each $i \in \{1, \dots, L\}$, since $\mathcal{O}_{C_i, S}^{(m)}$ is $(k, C_i, S)$-UG, there exist finitely many coefficients $\alpha_{i, \nu} \in S$ and operations $r_{i, \nu} \in R_k^{m, m}(C_i)$ such that $g(X) = \sum_\nu \alpha_{i, \nu} g(r_{i, \nu}(X))$ for all $g \colon C_i^m \to S$ and all $X \in C_i^m$. Since $C_i$ is an abelian group of exponent dividing $p^e$, every $r_{i, \nu} \in R_k^{m, m}(C_i)$ factors as $u_{i, \nu} \circ v_{i, \nu}$ with $u_{i, \nu} \in (\mathrm{Clo}(C_i)^{(k)})^m$ and $v_{i, \nu} \in (\mathrm{Clo}(C_i)^{(m)})^k$; lifting their integer-linear coefficients to matrices $U_{i, \nu} \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{m \times k}$ and $V_{i, \nu} \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{k \times m}$ produces $M_{i, \nu} := U_{i, \nu} V_{i, \nu} \in R_k^{m, m}(\mathbf{A})$ satisfying $M_{i, \nu} X = r_{i, \nu}(X)$ for all $X \in C_i^m$. Hence
$$I_i(f)(X) := \sum_\nu \alpha_{i, \nu} f(M_{i, \nu} X)$$
is a $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR operator on $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ such that $I_i(f)(X) = f(X)$ for all $f \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ and all $X \in C_i^m$.

Define recursively $J_1 := I_1$ and, for $i = 2, \dots, L$,
$$J_i := J_{i - 1} + I_i - I_i \circ J_{i - 1}.$$
By Lemma 17 (`lemma:urobs`)(3)–(4), every $J_i$ is $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR. We prove by induction on $i \in \{1, \dots, L\}$ that $J_i(f)(X) = f(X)$ for all $f \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ and all $X \in \bigcup_{j = 1}^i C_j^m$:
- For $i = 1$, $J_1 = I_1$ satisfies this by construction.
- For $i - 1 \to i$:
  - If $X \in C_i^m$, then $I_i(g)(X) = g(X)$ holds for both $g = f$ and $g = J_{i - 1}(f)$, so
    $$J_i(f)(X) = J_{i - 1}(f)(X) + f(X) - J_{i - 1}(f)(X) = f(X).$$
  - If $X \in C_j^m$ for some $j < i$, then because $C_j$ is a subgroup (hence a $\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z}$-submodule) of $\mathbf{A}$, every matrix $M_{i, \nu}$ satisfies $M_{i, \nu} X \in C_j^m$. By the induction hypothesis, $J_{i - 1}(f)(M_{i, \nu} X) = f(M_{i, \nu} X)$ for every $\nu$, whence $I_i(J_{i - 1}(f))(X) = I_i(f)(X)$. Therefore
    $$J_i(f)(X) = J_{i - 1}(f)(X) + I_i(f)(X) - I_i(f)(X) = J_{i - 1}(f)(X) = f(X).$$
Setting $J_{\mathrm{prop}} := J_L$ proves the lemma. $\square$

## 3. Proof of Theorem 1 (Conjecture 59 / `conjecture:strong`)

Fix a prime $p$ and set $S := \mathbb{Z}[1/p]$. We first prove by strong induction on $N := \log_p |\mathbf{A}| \ge 0$ that for every finite abelian $p$-group $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}$ is uniformly generated by $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, S)$-minors, where $k(\mathbf{A}) := \max(1, N)$.

- **Trivial group ($N = 0$, $\mathbf{A} = \{0\}$):** Here $k(\mathbf{A}) = 1$. For every $m \in \mathbb{N}$, $f(X) = 1_S \cdot f(0_{m \times m} X)$ with $0_{m \times m} = 0_{m \times 1} 0_{1 \times m} \in R_1^{m, m}(\mathbf{A})$, so $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}$ is $(1, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UG.
- **Base case of exponent $p$ ($e = 1$, $N = s \ge 1$):** Here $\mathbf{A} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^s$. Because every scalar multiplication in $\mathbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is repeated addition, the abelian group $\mathbb{Z}_p^s$ is term-equivalent to the $s$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space $\mathbb{F}_p^s$. Since $|\mathbb{F}_p| = p \in S^\times$, **Theorem 49 (`theorem:main`)** of `arXiv:2602.04034v1` (with $\mathbf{F} = \mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{R} = S$) immediately implies that $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}$ is uniformly generated by $s$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, S)$-minors, and $s = N = k(\mathbf{A})$.

- **Inductive step ($e \ge 2$, $N \ge 2$):** Assume the claim holds for every finite abelian $p$-group of order strictly less than $|\mathbf{A}| = p^N$. By the fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups, write
  $$\mathbf{A} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^s \times \mathbf{A}',$$
  where $p^e = \exp(\mathbf{A})$ ($e \ge 2$), $s \ge 1$ is the multiplicity of the largest invariant factor $p^e$, and $\mathbf{A}'$ is a (possibly trivial) finite abelian $p$-group satisfying $p^{e - 1} \mathbf{A}' = \{0\}$. Observe that:
  - $N = \log_p |\mathbf{A}| = e s + \log_p |\mathbf{A}'| \ge 2 s \ge 2$.
  - Set $k := N$ and $m := k + 1 = N + 1$, so **$m \ge 2 s + 1 > 2 s$**.
  - The subgroup $p^{e - 1}\mathbf{A} = p^{e - 1}(\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^s \times \{0\} \cong \mathbb{F}_p^s$ has order $p^s$, and the quotient
    $$\overline{\mathbf{A}} := \mathbf{A} / p^{e - 1}\mathbf{A} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p^{e - 1}\mathbb{Z})^s \times \mathbf{A}'$$
    has exponent $p^{e - 1}$ and order $|\overline{\mathbf{A}}| = p^{N - s} < p^N$ with $k' := N - s = (e - 1) s + \log_p |\mathbf{A}'| \ge s \ge 1$.
  - By the induction hypothesis applied to $\overline{\mathbf{A}}$, $\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathbf{A}}, S}$ is uniformly generated by $k'$-ary $(\overline{\mathbf{A}}, S)$-minors. Notice that **$s + k' = k$** and **$m - s = k' + 1 \ge 2$**.

We construct a $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR formula for the identity on $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ in four steps.

### Step 1: Peeling All Proper Subgroups
Let $C_1, \dots, C_L$ be all maximal proper subgroups of $\mathbf{A}$ (since $\mathbf{A} \neq \{0\}$ is a finite $p$-group, $L = \frac{p^d - 1}{p - 1} \ge 1$ where $p^d = |\mathbf{A}/p\mathbf{A}|$). Each $C_i$ is a finite abelian $p$-group of order $p^{N - 1} < p^N$ (with $N - 1 \ge 1$), so by the induction hypothesis $\mathcal{O}_{C_i, S}$ is uniformly generated by $(N - 1)$-ary $(C_i, S)$-minors, hence also by $k$-ary $(C_i, S)$-minors (Lemma 17(2), as $N - 1 < k$).

By **Lemma 2**, there exists a $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR operator $J_{\mathrm{prop}} \colon \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ such that $J_{\mathrm{prop}}(f)(X) = f(X)$ for all $f \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ and all $X \in \bigcup_{i = 1}^L C_i^m$. Define the complement
$$O := A^m \setminus \bigcup_{i = 1}^L C_i^m = \big\{ (a_1, \dots, a_m)^T \in A^m \;\big|\; \langle a_1, \dots, a_m \rangle = \mathbf{A} \big\}.$$
Then for every $f \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$, the remainder $h := f - J_{\mathrm{prop}}(f)$ satisfies $\mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq O$. We record three elementary properties of $O$:
1. *($\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})$-invariance.)* For every $T \in \mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})$ and $X \in A^m$, the coordinates of $T X$ are linear combinations of those of $X$ and vice versa via $T^{-1}$, so $\langle T X \rangle = \langle X \rangle$; thus $T(O) = O$.
2. *(Invariance under $p^{e - 1}\mathbf{A}$-shifts.)* For any subgroup $C \le \mathbf{A}$, if $C + p\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}$ then iterating gives $C + p^t\mathbf{A} = C + p(C + p^{t-1}\mathbf{A}) = \mathbf{A}$ for all $t \ge 1$, whence $C = C + p^e\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}$. Thus membership of $X \in A^m$ in $O$ depends only on the image of $X$ in $(\mathbf{A}/p\mathbf{A})^m$. Since $e \ge 2$ implies $p^{e - 1}\mathbf{A} \subseteq p\mathbf{A}$, we have $(\mathbf{X} + p^{e - 1} W, \mathbf{Y}) \in O \iff (\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \in O$ for every $X = (\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \in A^m$ and every $W \in \mathbb{F}_p^{m \times s}$.
3. *(Full mod-$p$ column rank on the top-exponent block.)* If $X = (\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \in A^m$ has $\rk_{\mathbb{F}_p}(\bar{\mathbf{X}}) < s$ (where $\bar{\mathbf{X}} := (\mathbf{X} \bmod p) \in \mathbb{F}_p^{m \times s}$), choose $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{F}_p^s \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}$ with $\bar{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}$. The homomorphism $\varphi_{\mathbf{c}} \colon \mathbf{A} \to \mathbb{F}_p$, $(x, y) \mapsto (x \bmod p) \cdot \mathbf{c}$, is surjective, so $C_{\mathbf{c}} := \ker(\varphi_{\mathbf{c}})$ is a proper (index-$p$) subgroup of $\mathbf{A}$ containing every coordinate of $X$; hence $X \in C_{\mathbf{c}}^m$, so $X \notin O$.

### Step 2: Codimension-$s$ Subspace Averaging Operator $A_{H_0}$ on $\{h \mid \mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq O\}$
Let $h \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ satisfy $\mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq O$. Set $H_0 := \{0\}^s \times \mathbb{F}_p^{m - s} \le \mathbb{F}_p^m$ (of codimension $s$ and dimension $m - s$). Split any tuple $X = (\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{m \times s} \times (\mathbf{A}')^m$ into its top $s$ rows and bottom $m - s$ rows:
$$\mathbf{X} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{X}_1 \\ \mathbf{X}' \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{Y} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{Y}_1 \\ \mathbf{Y}' \end{pmatrix},$$
with $\mathbf{X}_1 \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{s \times s}$, $\mathbf{X}' \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{(m - s) \times s}$, $\mathbf{Y}_1 \in (\mathbf{A}')^s$, $\mathbf{Y}' \in (\mathbf{A}')^{m - s}$. Define the $H_0$-coset average
$$F_{H_0}(h)(X) := \frac{1}{p^{s(m - s)}} \sum_{W \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times s}} h\!\left( \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{X}_1 \\ \mathbf{X}' + p^{e - 1} W \end{pmatrix},\; \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{Y}_1 \\ \mathbf{Y}' \end{pmatrix} \right) = \frac{1}{|H_0|^s} \sum_{W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(H_0)} h\big(\mathbf{X} + p^{e - 1} W,\; \mathbf{Y}\big).$$
Because $W$ ranges uniformly over $\mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times s}$, $\mathbf{X}' + p^{e - 1} W$ ranges uniformly over the entire fiber of $\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}' := (\mathbf{X}' \bmod p^{e - 1}) \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^{e - 1}\mathbb{Z})^{(m - s) \times s}$. Thus $F_{H_0}(h)(X)$ depends only on $(\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1) \in A^s$ and the $(m - s)$-tuple $(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}', \mathbf{Y}') \in \overline{\mathbf{A}}^{m - s}$, so we may write
$$F_{H_0}(h)(X) = \Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1}\big(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}',\; \mathbf{Y}'\big)$$
for a family of functions $\Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1} \colon \overline{\mathbf{A}}^{m - s} \to S$.

By the induction hypothesis on $\overline{\mathbf{A}}$ (which is a faithful $\mathbb{Z}/p^{e - 1}\mathbb{Z}$-module) at arity $m - s = k' + 1$, there exist finitely many coefficients $\beta_{\widetilde{B}} \in S$ and matrices $\widetilde{B} \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^{e - 1}\mathbb{Z})^{(m - s) \times (m - s)}$ of inner rank $\mathrm{rk}(\widetilde{B}) \le k'$ over $\mathbb{Z}/p^{e - 1}\mathbb{Z}$ such that
$$\Psi(\widetilde{Z}') = \sum_{\widetilde{B}} \beta_{\widetilde{B}} \, \Psi(\widetilde{B} \widetilde{Z}') \qquad \text{for all } \Psi \colon \overline{\mathbf{A}}^{m - s} \to S \text{ and } \widetilde{Z}' \in \overline{\mathbf{A}}^{m - s}.$$
For each $\widetilde{B}$ in the sum, fix a factorization $\widetilde{B} = \widetilde{U} \widetilde{V}$ with $\widetilde{U} \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^{e - 1}\mathbb{Z})^{(m - s) \times k'}$ and $\widetilde{V} \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^{e - 1}\mathbb{Z})^{k' \times (m - s)}$, and lift $\widetilde{U}, \widetilde{V}$ entrywise to $U \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{(m - s) \times k'}$ and $V \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{k' \times (m - s)}$. Set $\bar{U} := (U \bmod p)$, $\bar{V} := (V \bmod p)$, and $\bar{B} := \bar{U}\bar{V} = (\widetilde{B} \bmod p)$.

For $W' \in \{0, \dots, p - 1\}^{(m - s) \times s}$ and $U' \in \{0, \dots, p - 1\}^{(m - s) \times k'}$ (identified with $\mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times s}$ and $\mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times k'}$), define the $m \times m$ matrix over $\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z}$:
$$M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') := \begin{pmatrix} I_s & 0 \\ p^{e - 1} W' & U + p^{e - 1} U' \end{pmatrix}_{m \times (s + k')} \begin{pmatrix} I_s & 0 \\ 0 & V \end{pmatrix}_{(s + k') \times m} = \begin{pmatrix} I_s & 0 \\ p^{e - 1} W' & (U + p^{e - 1} U') V \end{pmatrix}.$$
As a product of an $m \times (s + k')$ matrix and an $(s + k') \times m$ matrix over $\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z}$, we have $\mathrm{rk}(M(\widetilde{B}, U', W')) \le s + k' = k$, i.e., $M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') \in R_k^{m, m}(\mathbf{A})$. Define the $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR operator
$$A_{H_0}(h)(X) := \sum_{\widetilde{B}} \beta_{\widetilde{B}} \cdot \frac{1}{p^{(m - s)(s + k')}} \sum_{W' \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times s}} \sum_{U' \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times k'}} h\big(M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') X\big).$$

**Claim.** *For every $h \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ with $\mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq O$ and every $X = (\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \in A^m$,*
$$A_{H_0}(h)(X) = F_{H_0}(h)(X) = \frac{1}{|H_0|^s} \sum_{W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(H_0)} h\big(\mathbf{X} + p^{e - 1} W,\; \mathbf{Y}\big).$$

**Proof of Claim.** Fix $\widetilde{B} = \widetilde{U}\widetilde{V}$ and $X = (\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \in A^m$. Modulo $p^{e - 1}$, $M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') \equiv \mathrm{diag}(I_s, \widetilde{B}) \pmod{p^{e - 1}}$, independent of $(W', U')$. Since $p^{e - 1}\mathbf{A}' = \{0\}$, the action on the $\mathbf{Y}$-component is
$$\widetilde{M(\widetilde{B}, U', W')} \, \mathbf{Y} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{Y}_1 \\ \widetilde{B}\mathbf{Y}' \end{pmatrix}.$$
On the $\mathbf{X}$-component, because $p^{e - 1} a = p^{e - 1} \bar{a}$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z}$, we have
$$M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') \mathbf{X} = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{X}_1 \\ U V \mathbf{X}' + p^{e - 1} [W',\, U'] \, \mathbf{T} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{T} := \begin{pmatrix} \bar{\mathbf{X}}_1 \\ \bar{V} \bar{\mathbf{X}}' \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(s + k') \times s},$$
where $[W', U'] \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times (s + k')}$ and $U V \mathbf{X}' \equiv \widetilde{U}\widetilde{V}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}' = \widetilde{B}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}' \pmod{p^{e - 1}}$. Consider the two exhaustive cases for $\rk_{\mathbb{F}_p}(\mathbf{T}) \le s$:
- **Case 1 ($\rk_{\mathbb{F}_p}(\mathbf{T}) = s$):** Since $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(s + k') \times s}$ has full column rank $s$, it admits a left inverse $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{F}_p^{s \times (s + k')}$ with $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{T} = I_s$. Hence the $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear map $\Phi_{\mathbf{T}} \colon \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times (s + k')} \to \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times s}$, $\Omega = [W', U'] \mapsto \Omega \mathbf{T}$, is surjective (since $\Phi_{\mathbf{T}}(W \mathbf{L}) = W$ for every $W \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times s}$). Therefore every $W \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times s}$ is attained with uniform multiplicity $p^{(m - s) k'}$, and the inner average equals
  $$\frac{1}{p^{(m - s) s}} \sum_{W \in \mathbb{F}_p^{(m - s) \times s}} h\!\left( \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{X}_1 \\ U V \mathbf{X}' + p^{e - 1} W \end{pmatrix},\; \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{Y}_1 \\ \widetilde{B}\mathbf{Y}' \end{pmatrix} \right) = \Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1}\big(\widetilde{B}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}',\; \widetilde{B}\mathbf{Y}'\big).$$
- **Case 2 ($\rk_{\mathbb{F}_p}(\mathbf{T}) < s$):** Choose $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{F}_p^s \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}$ with $\mathbf{T}\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}$, so $\bar{\mathbf{X}}_1 \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\bar{V}\bar{\mathbf{X}}'\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}$, whence $\bar{B}\bar{\mathbf{X}}'\mathbf{c} = \bar{U}(\bar{V}\bar{\mathbf{X}}'\mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{0}$. Because $e \ge 2$ gives $p \mid p^{e - 1}$, every tuple $M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') X$ has mod-$p$ $\mathbf{X}$-component equal to $\binom{\bar{\mathbf{X}}_1}{\bar{B}\bar{\mathbf{X}}'}$, which annihilates $\mathbf{c}$; by Property 3 of $O$, $M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') X \in C_{\mathbf{c}}^m \subseteq A^m \setminus O$, so $h(M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') X) = 0$. Likewise, every summand in the average defining $\Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1}(\widetilde{B}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}', \widetilde{B}\mathbf{Y}')$ evaluates $h$ at a tuple whose $\mathbf{X}$-component is congruent to $\binom{\mathbf{X}_1}{\widetilde{B}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}'}$ modulo $p^{e - 1}$, hence congruent to $\binom{\bar{\mathbf{X}}_1}{\bar{B}\bar{\mathbf{X}}'}$ modulo $p$, which also annihilates $\mathbf{c}$ and lies in $C_{\mathbf{c}}^m$; thus $\Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1}(\widetilde{B}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}', \widetilde{B}\mathbf{Y}') = 0$. Both sides equal $0$.

In both cases the inner average equals $\Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1}(\widetilde{B}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}', \widetilde{B}\mathbf{Y}')$. Summing against $\beta_{\widetilde{B}}$ and applying the induction hypothesis on $\overline{\mathbf{A}}$ to $\Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1}$ yields
$$A_{H_0}(h)(X) = \sum_{\widetilde{B}} \beta_{\widetilde{B}} \, \Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1}\big(\widetilde{B}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}', \mathbf{Y}')\big) = \Psi_{\mathbf{X}_1, \mathbf{Y}_1}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}', \mathbf{Y}') = F_{H_0}(h)(X),$$
proving the Claim. $\square$

### Step 3: Conjugation to All Codimension-$\le s$ Subspaces and Radon Inversion
1. *All codimension-$s$ subspaces:* For every subspace $H \le \mathbb{F}_p^m$ of codimension $s$, choose a basis $\bar{\mathbf{n}}_1, \dots, \bar{\mathbf{n}}_s$ of $H^\perp \le (\mathbb{F}_p^m)^*$, extend it to a basis of $(\mathbb{F}_p^m)^*$, and let $\bar{T}_H \in \mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{F}_p)$ be the invertible matrix whose top $s$ rows are $\bar{\mathbf{n}}_1^T, \dots, \bar{\mathbf{n}}_s^T$, so $\bar{T}_H(H) = H_0$. Lift $\bar{T}_H$ entrywise to $T_H \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^{m \times m}$; since $\det(T_H) \equiv \det(\bar{T}_H) \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, we have $\det(T_H) \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^\times$ and hence $T_H \in \mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})$. Define
   $$A_H(h)(X) := A_{H_0}(h \circ T_H^{-1})(T_H X),$$
   whose underlying matrices are $T_H^{-1} M(\widetilde{B}, U', W') T_H \in R_k^{m, m}(\mathbf{A})$ (Lemma 12), so $A_H$ is $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR. By Property 1 of $O$, $\mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq O \implies \mathrm{supp}(h \circ T_H^{-1}) \subseteq O$. Applying the Claim of Step 2 to $h \circ T_H^{-1}$ at $T_H X = (T_H \mathbf{X}, \widetilde{T}_H \mathbf{Y})$ gives
   $$A_H(h)(X) = \frac{1}{|H_0|^s} \sum_{\widetilde{W} \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(H_0)} h\big(T_H^{-1}(T_H \mathbf{X} + p^{e - 1} \widetilde{W}),\; \widetilde{T}_H^{-1} \widetilde{T}_H \mathbf{Y}\big) = \frac{1}{|H|^s} \sum_{W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(H)} h\big(\mathbf{X} + p^{e - 1} W,\; \mathbf{Y}\big),$$
   since $p^{e - 1} T_H^{-1} \widetilde{W} = p^{e - 1} \bar{T}_H^{-1} \widetilde{W}$ and $\widetilde{W} \mapsto \bar{T}_H^{-1} \widetilde{W}$ maps $\mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(H_0)$ bijectively onto $\mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(H)$. Moreover, by Property 2 of $O$, $\mathrm{supp}(A_H(h)) \subseteq O$ whenever $\mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq O$.

2. *All subspaces of codimension $c \in \{0, 1, \dots, s - 1\}$:* Let $U \le \mathbb{F}_p^m$ have codimension $c \in \{0, 1, \dots, s - 1\}$ (dimension $m - c$). Choose a basis $u_1, \dots, u_{m - c}$ of $U$ and define
   $$H_1 := \mathrm{span}(u_1, \dots, u_{m - s}), \qquad H_2 := \mathrm{span}(u_{s - c + 1}, \dots, u_{m - c}).$$
   Since $m \ge 2 s + 1 > 2 s - c$, we have $1 \le s - c + 1 \le m - s < m - c$, so both $H_1, H_2 \le U$ have dimension $m - s$ (codimension $s$ in $\mathbb{F}_p^m$) and $H_1 + H_2 = U$. Define
   $$A_U := A_{H_1} \circ A_{H_2},$$
   which is $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR by Lemma 17(4). For any $h \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ with $\mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq O$, since $\mathrm{supp}(A_{H_2}(h)) \subseteq O$, applying **Lemma 1(3)** to $\phi(W) := h(\mathbf{X} + p^{e - 1} W, \mathbf{Y})$ gives
   $$A_U(h)(X) = \frac{1}{|U|^s} \sum_{W \in \mathrm{Mat}_{m \times s}(U)} h\big(\mathbf{X} + p^{e - 1} W,\; \mathbf{Y}\big).$$

3. *Radon inversion:* Define the operator $K \colon \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)} \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ by
   $$K := \sum_{c = 0}^s (-1)^{s - c} p^{\binom{s - c + 1}{2}} \binom{m - 1 - c}{s - c}_p \sum_{\substack{U \le \mathbb{F}_p^m \\ \mathrm{codim}(U) = c}} A_U.$$
   Because every outer coefficient is an integer (hence in $S$), $K$ is $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR by Lemma 17(3). For every $h \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$ with $\mathrm{supp}(h) \subseteq O$ and every $X = (\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \in A^m$, **Lemma 1(2)** applied to $\phi(W) := h(\mathbf{X} + p^{e - 1} W, \mathbf{Y})$ yields
   $$K(h)(X) = \phi(0) = h(X).$$

### Step 4: Assembly, All Arities, and Transfer to Arbitrary Unital Rings $\mathbf{R}$
Define
$$I := J_{\mathrm{prop}} + K - K \circ J_{\mathrm{prop}} \colon \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)} \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}.$$
By Lemma 17(3)–(4), $I$ is $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UR. For every $f \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(m)}$, Step 1 gives $\mathrm{supp}(f - J_{\mathrm{prop}}(f)) \subseteq O$, so Step 3 yields
$$I(f) = J_{\mathrm{prop}}(f) + K\big(f - J_{\mathrm{prop}}(f)\big) = J_{\mathrm{prop}}(f) + \big(f - J_{\mathrm{prop}}(f)\big) = f.$$
Thus $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}^{(k + 1)}$ is $(k, \mathbf{A}, S)$-UG. By **Theorem 18 (`theorem:unigen`)** of `arXiv:2602.04034v1` applied to the full clonoid $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}$ (trivially closed under composition with constants on $A$), $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, S}$ is uniformly generated by $k$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, S)$-minors.
*(Equivalently, the arity induction is immediate: $n \le k$ uses $\mathrm{Id}_n \in R_k^{n, n}(\mathbf{A})$; for $n \ge k + 1 \to n + 1$, applying the $n$-ary formula with $x_{n + 1}$ fixed writes $g(X, x_{n + 1}) = \sum_i \alpha_i g(M_i X, x_{n + 1})$ where $\mathrm{diag}(M_i, 1) \in R_{k + 1}^{n + 1, n + 1}(\mathbf{A})$ factors through $A^{k + 1}$, and applying the $(k + 1)$-ary rank-$\le k$ formula on the middle factor reduces every matrix to $R_k^{n + 1, n + 1}(\mathbf{A})$.)*

This completes the strong induction on $N = \log_p |\mathbf{A}|$ over $S = \mathbb{Z}[1/p]$.

Finally, let $\mathbf{R}$ be any unital ring in which $p \cdot 1_R$ is invertible, with canonical central unital ring homomorphism $\iota \colon S \to Z(\mathbf{R}) \subseteq \mathbf{R}$. For each $n \in \mathbb{N}$, our $(k(\mathbf{A}), \mathbf{A}, S)$-UG formula $f(X) = \sum_{i = 1}^Q \alpha_i f(M_i X)$ ($\alpha_i \in S$, $M_i \in R_{k(\mathbf{A})}^{n, n}(\mathbf{A})$) evaluated on the indicator functions $\delta_Y \colon A^n \to S$ ($Y \in A^n$) gives the exact scalar identities
$$\sum_{\substack{1 \le i \le Q \\ M_i X = Y}} \alpha_i = \delta_{X, Y} \in S \qquad (\forall X, Y \in A^n).$$
Applying $\iota$ yields $\sum_{M_i X = Y} \iota(\alpha_i) = \delta_{X, Y} \cdot 1_R$ in $\mathbf{R}$. Hence for every $g \colon A^n \to R$ and every $X \in A^n$,
$$\sum_{i = 1}^Q \iota(\alpha_i) \, g(M_i X) = \sum_{Y \in A^n} \left( \sum_{\substack{1 \le i \le Q \\ M_i X = Y}} \iota(\alpha_i) \right) g(Y) = 1_R \cdot g(X) = g(X).$$
Because $(z_1, \dots, z_Q) \mapsto \sum_{i = 1}^Q \iota(\alpha_i) z_i$ is a term operation of the regular left $\mathbf{R}$-module $\mathbf{R}$, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{R}}$ is uniformly generated by $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{R})$-minors. This proves **Theorem 1** and settles **Conjecture 59 (`conjecture:strong`)**. $\square$

## 4. Proof of Theorem 2 (General Coprime Modules and Conjecture 1 / `conjecture:main`)

### Proof of Part 1
Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a finite module over a unital ring, let $\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}} := (A, +, 0, -)$ be its underlying finite abelian group, and let $\mathbf{R}$ be a unital ring in which $|A| \cdot 1_R$ is invertible.

- If $|A| = 1$, then $k(\mathbf{A}) = 1$, and for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and every left $\mathbf{R}$-module $\mathbf{B}$, $g(X) = g(0_{n \times n} X)$ with $0_{n \times n} \in R_1^{n, n}(\mathbf{A})$ (as $0 = x - x \in \mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A})$), so $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}}$ is $(1, \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-UG, and every $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-clonoid is generated by its unary part by Corollary 20 (`corollary:unigen`).
- Suppose $|A| > 1$, with primary decomposition $\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbf{A}_{p_1} \times \dots \times \mathbf{A}_{p_r}$ into nontrivial Sylow $p_j$-subgroups for distinct primes $p_1, \dots, p_r$ ($r \ge 1$).
  1. *(Prime invertibility.)* For each $j \in \{1, \dots, r\}$, writing $|A| = p_j q_j$ ($q_j \in \mathbb{N}$), the element $(q_j \cdot 1_R)(|A| \cdot 1_R)^{-1}$ is a two-sided inverse of $p_j \cdot 1_R$ in $\mathbf{R}$ (since $q_j \cdot 1_R, p_j \cdot 1_R \in Z(\mathbf{R})$). Thus $p_j \cdot 1_R \in R^\times$ for every $j$.
  2. *(Uniform generation of Sylow factors.)* By **Theorem 1**, each $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}_{p_j}, \mathbf{R}}$ is uniformly generated by $(\log_{p_j}|\mathbf{A}_{p_j}|)$-ary minors, hence by $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary minors (Lemma 17(2), as $\log_{p_j}|\mathbf{A}_{p_j}| \le k(\mathbf{A})$).
  3. *(Direct product of clones via CRT.)* Let $p_j^{e_j} = \exp(\mathbf{A}_{p_j})$. Because the integers $p_1^{e_1}, \dots, p_r^{e_r}$ are pairwise coprime, the Chinese Remainder Theorem yields $\mathbb{Z}/(\prod_{j=1}^r p_j^{e_j})\mathbb{Z} \cong \prod_{j=1}^r \mathbb{Z}/p_j^{e_j}\mathbb{Z}$. For any $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and any tuple of $n$-ary term operations $(t_1, \dots, t_r) \in \prod_{j=1}^r \mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A}_{p_j})^{(n)}$, lifting each coordinate coefficient tuple $(c_{1, i}, \dots, c_{r, i}) \in \prod_j \mathbb{Z}/p_j^{e_j}\mathbb{Z}$ via CRT to a single integer $c_i \in \mathbb{Z}$ gives a single term operation $t(x_1, \dots, x_n) = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i x_i \in \mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}})^{(n)}$ acting coordinatewise as $(t_1, \dots, t_r)$. Conversely, every term operation in $\mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}})$ acts coordinatewise. Hence
     $$\mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}}) = \mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A}_{p_1}) \times \dots \times \mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A}_{p_r})$$
     as a direct product of clones.
  4. *(Product, reduct, and target module transfer.)* By induction on $r$ using **Proposition 21 (`proposition:ugproduct`)** of `arXiv:2602.04034v1`, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}}, \mathbf{R}}$ is uniformly generated by $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary $(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}}, \mathbf{R})$-minors. Since $\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ is a reduct of the module $\mathbf{A}$, we have $\mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}}) \subseteq \mathrm{Clo}(\mathbf{A})$ and hence $R_{k(\mathbf{A})}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{ab}}) \subseteq R_{k(\mathbf{A})}(\mathbf{A})$; therefore the exact same witnessing formulas show $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{R}}$ is $(k(\mathbf{A}), \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{R})$-UG. By **Corollary 35 (`corollary:orbitsupport`)** of `arXiv:2602.04034v1`, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}}$ is uniformly generated by $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-minors for every left $\mathbf{R}$-module $\mathbf{B}$. Finally, by **Corollary 20 (`corollary:unigen`)**, every $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-clonoid $\mathcal{C}$ satisfies $\mathcal{C} = \langle \mathcal{C}^{(k(\mathbf{A}))} \rangle_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}}$.

### Proof of Part 2 (Conjecture 1 / `conjecture:main`)
Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a finite left $\mathbf{R}_A$-module and $\mathbf{B}$ be a finite left $\mathbf{R}_B$-module.

- **("If" direction: $\gcd(|A|, |B|) = 1$.)** Let $I_B := \{r \in R_B \mid \forall b \in B,\; r b = 0\}$ be the annihilator of $\mathbf{B}$, and set $\mathbf{R} := \mathbf{R}_B / I_B$, so $\mathbf{B}$ is term-equivalent to the faithful left $\mathbf{R}$-module $\mathbf{B}' = (B, +, 0, -, (r + I_B)_{r \in R_B})$. Let $n_B := \exp(B, +) \ge 1$ be the exponent of the finite abelian group $(B, +)$. Since $(n_B \cdot 1_R) b = n_B b = 0$ for all $b \in B$ and $\mathbf{B}'$ is faithful, we have $n_B \cdot 1_R = 0_R$ in $\mathbf{R}$. By Lagrange's theorem, every prime dividing $n_B$ divides $|B|$; hence $\gcd(|A|, |B|) = 1$ implies $\gcd(|A|, n_B) = 1$. By Bézout's identity there exist $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $u |A| + v n_B = 1$, and projecting to $\mathbf{R}$ gives
  $$(u \cdot 1_R)(|A| \cdot 1_R) = (|A| \cdot 1_R)(u \cdot 1_R) = 1_R - (v \cdot 1_R)(n_B \cdot 1_R) = 1_R.$$
  Thus $|A| \cdot 1_R$ is invertible in $\mathbf{R}$. By Part 1, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}'}$ — and by term-equivalence, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}}$ — is uniformly generated by $k(\mathbf{A})$-ary $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-minors, and every $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-clonoid $\mathcal{C}$ satisfies $\mathcal{C} = \langle \mathcal{C}^{(k(\mathbf{A}))} \rangle_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}}$. Because $A$ and $B$ are finite, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}}^{(k(\mathbf{A}))} = B^{A^{k(\mathbf{A})}}$ is finite, so there are at most $2^{|B|^{|A|^{k(\mathbf{A})}}} < \infty$ distinct $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-clonoids. Moreover, by **Corollary 23 (`corollary:fingen`)** of `arXiv:2602.04034v1`, every $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-clonoid is the polymorphism minion of relational structures of maximal arity at most $|A|^{k(\mathbf{A})}$.

- **("Only if" direction: $\gcd(|A|, |B|) > 1$.)** By **Theorem 1.3** (`thm:notcoprime`) of Mayr–Wynne, *Clonoids between modules*, Internat. J. Algebra Comput. **34**(4) (2024), 543–570 (`arXiv:2307.00076v3`), whenever two finite modules $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ do not have coprime order, there exists an infinite ascending chain of clonoids from $\mathbf{A}$ to $\mathbf{B}$; in particular, there are infinitely many $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$-clonoids.

This completes the proof of **Theorem 2** and settles **Conjecture 1 (`conjecture:main`)**. $\square$
