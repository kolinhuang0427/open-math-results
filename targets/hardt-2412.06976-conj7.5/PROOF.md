# Proof of Hardt–Wallach Conjecture 8.5 (`arXiv:2412.06976v2`; Conjecture 7.5 in `v1`)

**Conjecture 8.5 (`arXiv:2412.06976v2`, Section 8, lines 1603–1607; folder slug `conj7.5`):**
For any composition $\alpha = (\alpha_i)_{i \in \mathbb{Z}}$ of finite support with $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, let $\eta(\alpha) = \{i \in \mathbb{Z} \mid \nexists j < i,\, \alpha_j = \alpha_i\}$ and let $\delta_i$ be the composition with $1$ at index $i$ and $0$ elsewhere. Then in the ring $\overline{R}$ of back-symmetric functions,
$$\xi(\overline{\kappa}_\alpha) = \sum_{i \in \eta(\alpha)} \overline{\kappa}_{\alpha - \delta_i}.$$

*(Note on terminology: Since $\alpha$ has finite support with nonnegative integer entries $\alpha_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, $\alpha_j = 0$ for all $j \ll 0$, so every $i$ with $\alpha_i = 0$ satisfies $\alpha_j = 0$ for $j \ll 0$, whence $i \notin \eta(\alpha)$. Hence $\alpha_i \ge 1$ for every $i \in \eta(\alpha)$, and $\alpha - \delta_i$ is again a valid composition.)*

We prove this unconditionally for all compositions $\alpha$.

---

## 1. Shift Reduction

Let $\gamma : \overline{R} \to \overline{R}$ be the unit shift automorphism ($x_j \mapsto x_{j+1}$, $\gamma(w)(j) = w(j-1)+1$, $\gamma(\alpha)_{j+1} = \alpha_j$). Since nilplactic equivalence $\overset{\bullet}{\sim}$ (`Section 8`, Eq. (34), lines 1531–1537) is translation-invariant under $a \mapsto a + 1$, Definition 8.1 gives $\gamma(\overline{\kappa}_\alpha) = \overline{\kappa}_{\gamma(\alpha)}$. Because $\gamma(s_k w) = s_{k+1}\gamma(w)$ preserves Coxeter length drops ($\ell(s_k w) < \ell(w) \iff \ell(s_{k+1}\gamma(w)) < \ell(\gamma(w))$), Eq. `eq:nabla-xi-olR-action` yields $\xi(\gamma(f)) = \gamma(\xi(f))$ for all $f \in \overline{R}$. Furthermore $\eta(\gamma(\alpha)) = \{i+1 : i \in \eta(\alpha)\}$ and $\gamma(\alpha) - \delta_{i+1} = \gamma(\alpha - \delta_i)$. For the zero composition $\alpha = 0$, $\overline{\kappa}_0 = 1 = \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathrm{id}}$, $\eta(0) = \emptyset$, and $\xi(1) = 0 = \sum_{i \in \emptyset} \overline{\kappa}_{-\delta_i}$. Since $\gamma$ is an automorphism of $\overline{R}$, it suffices to prove the conjecture for positive compositions $\alpha$ (nonzero compositions supported on $\{1, 2, \dots, m\}$ with $\alpha_i = 0$ for $i \le 0$ and $i > m$).

## 2. Commutation of $\xi$ with Demazure Operators $\pi_i$ on $\overline{R}$

For each $i \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $s_i$ swap $x_i$ and $x_{i+1}$, and define the divided-difference and isobaric Demazure operators on $\overline{R}$ by
$$\partial_i(f) := \frac{f - s_i f}{x_i - x_{i+1}}, \qquad \pi_i(f) := \partial_i(x_i f).$$
Every $f \in \overline{R}$ is symmetric in $\{x_j : j \le a\}$ for some $a < i$, so $f$ is a polynomial in $x_i, x_{i+1}$ with coefficients symmetric in $x_{\le a}$; hence $\partial_i(f) \in \overline{R}$. Moreover, for any $w \in S_{\mathbb{Z}}$, taking $k \gg 0$ so that $i + k \ge 1$ and $\gamma^k(w) \in S_{\mathbb{Z}_+}$, specializing $x_{\le 0} = 0$ commutes with $\partial_{i+k}$ and sends $\gamma^k(\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w) \mapsto \mathfrak{S}_{\gamma^k(w)}$, where $\partial_{i+k}\mathfrak{S}_{\gamma^k(w)} = \mathfrak{S}_{\gamma^k(w)s_{i+k}}$ if $w(i) > w(i+1)$ and $0$ otherwise. Since $\{\mathfrak{S}_u : u \in S_{\mathbb{Z}_+}\}$ is linearly independent and $\{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_u : u \in S_{\mathbb{Z}}\}$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $\overline{R}$, it follows that in $\overline{R}$:
$$\partial_i(\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w) = \begin{cases} \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{w s_i} & \text{if } w(i) > w(i+1), \\ 0 & \text{if } w(i) < w(i+1). \end{cases}$$
Also, by Definition 3.13 (together with Definition 3.4), $\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_i} = \sum_{b \le i} x_b$, so $x_i = \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_i} - \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_{i-1}} \in \overline{R}$, and therefore $x_i f \in \overline{R}$ and $\pi_i(f) \in \overline{R}$ for all $f \in \overline{R}$.

**Lemma 1.** *For every $i \in \mathbb{Z}$ and every $f \in \overline{R}$, we have $\xi(\partial_i f) = \partial_i(\xi f)$ and $\xi(\pi_i f) = \pi_i(\xi f)$.*

*Proof.* First, we verify $\xi(\partial_i \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w) = \partial_i(\xi \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w)$ on the basis $\{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w : w \in S_{\mathbb{Z}}\}$.
- Suppose $w(i) < w(i+1)$, so $\xi(\partial_i \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w) = 0$. For any $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $s_k w < w$, $k+1$ appears before $k$ in the one-line notation of $w$, so we cannot have $(w(i), w(i+1)) = (k, k+1)$. Since $s_k$ swaps only the values $k$ and $k+1$, it preserves the inequality $w(i) < w(i+1)$, so $(s_k w)(i) < (s_k w)(i+1)$ and $\partial_i(\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_k w}) = 0$. Thus $\partial_i(\xi \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w) = 0$.
- Suppose $w(i) > w(i+1)$. Then
  $$\xi(\partial_i \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w) = \sum_{\substack{k \in \mathbb{Z} \\ s_k(w s_i) < w s_i}} \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_k w s_i}, \qquad \partial_i(\xi \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w) = \sum_{\substack{k \in \mathbb{Z} \\ s_k w < w,\; (s_k w)(i) > (s_k w)(i+1)}} \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_k w s_i}.$$
  For $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $\{w(i), w(i+1)\} \neq \{k, k+1\}$, swapping positions $i, i+1$ does not alter the relative order of values $k, k+1$, and swapping values $k, k+1$ does not alter the inequality between positions $i, i+1$; hence $s_k(w s_i) < w s_i \iff s_k w < w$ and $(s_k w)(i) > (s_k w)(i+1)$. In the sole remaining case $\{w(i), w(i+1)\} = \{k, k+1\}$, since $w(i) > w(i+1)$ we have $w(i) = k+1$ and $w(i+1) = k$, so $(w s_i)(i) = k < k+1 = (w s_i)(i+1)$ (giving $s_k(w s_i) > w s_i$) and $(s_k w)(i) = k < k+1 = (s_k w)(i+1)$ (excluding $k$ from both sums). Thus $\xi \partial_i = \partial_i \xi$ on $\overline{R}$.

Second, by Eq. `eq:nabla-xi-olR-action` and Nenashev's Leibniz rule on $\overline{R}$ (`Section 4.3`, Proposition 4.21),
$$\xi(x_i) = \xi(\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_i}) - \xi(\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_{i-1}}) = \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathrm{id}} - \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\mathrm{id}} = 1 - 1 = 0.$$
Hence $\xi(x_i f) = \xi(x_i) f + x_i \xi(f) = x_i \xi(f)$, and therefore
$$\xi(\pi_i f) = \xi(\partial_i(x_i f)) = \partial_i(\xi(x_i f)) = \partial_i(x_i \xi(f)) = \pi_i(\xi f). \quad \square$$

**Lemma 2.** *For any composition $\alpha$ and any $i \in \mathbb{Z}$,*
$$\pi_i(\overline{\kappa}_\alpha) = \begin{cases} \overline{\kappa}_{s_i \alpha} & \text{if } \alpha_i > \alpha_{i+1}, \\ \overline{\kappa}_\alpha & \text{if } \alpha_i \le \alpha_{i+1}. \end{cases}$$

*Proof.* Set $\tilde{\alpha} = s_i \alpha$ if $\alpha_i > \alpha_{i+1}$ and $\tilde{\alpha} = \alpha$ if $\alpha_i \le \alpha_{i+1}$. Both $\pi_i(\overline{\kappa}_\alpha)$ and $\overline{\kappa}_{\tilde{\alpha}}$ lie in $\overline{R}$ (by Proposition 8.2). For any sufficiently large $k \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ such that $i + k \ge 1$, $\gamma^k(\alpha)$ and $\gamma^k(\tilde{\alpha})$ are positive compositions, and all $\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w$ appearing in the finite basis expansion of $\pi_i(\overline{\kappa}_\alpha) - \overline{\kappa}_{\tilde{\alpha}}$ satisfy $\gamma^k(w) \in S_{\mathbb{Z}_+}$, Eq. `eq:key-stabilization` gives
$$\gamma^k(\pi_i(\overline{\kappa}_\alpha))|_{x_{\le 0}=0} = \pi_{i+k}\!\left(\gamma^k(\overline{\kappa}_\alpha)|_{x_{\le 0}=0}\right) = \pi_{i+k}(\kappa_{\gamma^k(\alpha)}) = \kappa_{\gamma^k(\tilde{\alpha})} = \gamma^k(\overline{\kappa}_{\tilde{\alpha}})|_{x_{\le 0}=0},$$
where $\pi_j(\kappa_\beta) = \kappa_{s_j\beta}$ for $\beta_j > \beta_{j+1}$ and $\pi_j(\kappa_\beta) = \kappa_\beta$ for $\beta_j \le \beta_{j+1}$ (with $j = i + k$) follows from $\kappa_\beta = \pi_{u(\beta)} x^\lambda$, where $\lambda = \mathrm{sort}(\beta)$ is weakly decreasing and $u(\beta) \in S_{\mathbb{Z}_+}$ is the minimal-length permutation satisfying $(u(\beta)\lambda)_a = \lambda_{u(\beta)^{-1}(a)} = \beta_a$: setting $r = u(\beta)^{-1}(j)$ and $r' = u(\beta)^{-1}(j+1)$, if $\beta_j > \beta_{j+1}$ then $\lambda_r > \lambda_{r'}$ forces $r < r'$, so $u(s_j\beta) = s_j u(\beta)$ with length $\ell(u(\beta))+1$ and $\pi_j \kappa_\beta = \kappa_{s_j\beta}$; if $\beta_j < \beta_{j+1}$ then $\lambda_r < \lambda_{r'}$ forces $r > r'$, so $s_j$ is a left descent of $u(\beta)$ and $\pi_j^2 = \pi_j$ gives $\pi_j \kappa_\beta = \kappa_\beta$; if $\beta_j = \beta_{j+1}$ then $\lambda_r = \lambda_{r'}$, and since $u(\beta)$ is minimal in $u(\beta)W_\lambda$ (hence order-preserving on the contiguous block of indices with $\lambda$-value $\beta_j$), $r' = r+1$ with $\lambda_r = \lambda_{r+1}$ and $s_j u(\beta) = u(\beta) s_r$ of length $\ell(u(\beta))+1$, so $\pi_j \pi_{u(\beta)} x^\lambda = \pi_{u(\beta)} \pi_r x^\lambda = \pi_{u(\beta)} x^\lambda = \kappa_\beta$ because $x^\lambda$ is symmetric in $x_r, x_{r+1}$ (giving $\pi_r(x^\lambda) = x^\lambda$). Since $\{\mathfrak{S}_u : u \in S_{\mathbb{Z}_+}\}$ is linearly independent and $\{\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w : w \in S_{\mathbb{Z}}\}$ is a basis of $\overline{R}$, $\pi_i(\overline{\kappa}_\alpha) = \overline{\kappa}_{\tilde{\alpha}}$ in $\overline{R}$. $\square$

## 3. Base Case: Decreasing Partitions via Dominant Permutations

Let $\lambda = (\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_m > 0)$ be a decreasing positive partition (with $\lambda_j = 0$ for $j \le 0$ and $j > m$), and let $w(\lambda) \in S_{\mathbb{Z}_+}$ be the unique permutation whose Lehmer code (`Definition 2.2`) is $\mathrm{code}(w(\lambda)) = \lambda$.

**Lemma 3.** *For every partition $\lambda = (\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_m > 0)$:*
1. *$w(\lambda)$ is $132$-avoiding (dominant), and $\overline{\kappa}_\lambda = \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{w(\lambda)}$.*
2. *The left descents $\{k \in \mathbb{Z} : s_k w(\lambda) < w(\lambda)\}$ are in bijection with $\eta(\lambda)$ via $i \in \eta(\lambda) \mapsto k = \lambda_i$.*
3. *For every $i \in \eta(\lambda)$ with $k = \lambda_i$, we have $\mathfrak{S}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = \kappa_{\lambda - \delta_i}$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x_1, x_2, \dots]$, and hence $\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = \overline{\kappa}_{\lambda - \delta_i}$ in $\overline{R}$.*
4. *Consequently, $\xi(\overline{\kappa}_\lambda) = \sum_{i \in \eta(\lambda)} \overline{\kappa}_{\lambda - \delta_i}$.*

*Proof.* Let $w := w(\lambda)$ and $c_r := \mathrm{code}(w)_r = \lambda_r$.

**(1)** Suppose $w$ contained a $132$-pattern $1 \le a < b < c$ with $w(a) < w(c) < w(b)$, chosen to minimize $b - a$. Then no $y \in (a, b)$ has $w(y) < w(a)$ (otherwise $(y, b, c)$ would be a $132$-pattern with smaller $b - y$). Thus every $z > a$ with $w(z) < w(a)$ satisfies $z > b$ (as $w(b) > w(a)$) and $w(z) < w(b)$, while $z = c > b$ also satisfies $w(a) < w(c) < w(b)$. Hence $c_b \ge c_a + 1$, contradicting $c_a \ge c_b$. So $w$ is $132$-avoiding.

Moreover, for every $r \ge 1$, the set $\{w(z) : z > r,\, w(z) < w(r)\}$ has no gaps: if $x < y < w(r)$ with $y$ appearing after $r$ and $x$ before $r$, then $(x, w(r), y)$ would be a $132$-pattern. Since this set has size $\lambda_r$, we have
$$\{w(z) : z > r,\, w(z) < w(r)\} = \{1, 2, \dots, \lambda_r\} \qquad \text{for every } r \ge 1. \tag{$*$}$$
We verify $\mathfrak{S}_{w(\lambda)} = x^\lambda$ by induction on $|\lambda|$ via the Lascoux–Schützenberger transition formula (empty for $\lambda = \emptyset$). For $m \ge 1$, $r = m$ is the last descent of $w$ (as $\lambda_m > 0 = \lambda_{m+1} = \dots$ gives $w(m) > w(m+1) < w(m+2) < \dots$). Let $s = \max\{j > m : w(j) < w(m)\} = m + \lambda_m$ (by ($*$), the $\lambda_m$ values $1, \dots, \lambda_m$ lie in increasing order at $m+1, \dots, m+\lambda_m$), and set $u' = w t_{m,s}$. Then $\mathrm{code}(u') = \lambda - \delta_m$ (still weakly decreasing). No $a < m$ has $w(a) < w(s)$ (otherwise $(a, m, s)$ is a $132$-pattern since $w(s) < w(m)$), so transition gives $\mathfrak{S}_{w(\lambda)} = x_m \mathfrak{S}_{w(\lambda - \delta_m)} = x^\lambda = \kappa_\lambda$. By Remark 8.4 (`Section 8`, line 1599: $\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_w = \sum_\alpha \overline{\kappa}_\alpha$ has the same expansion as $\mathfrak{S}_w = \sum_\alpha \kappa_\alpha$) and Proposition 8.3, it follows that $\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{w(\lambda)} = \overline{\kappa}_\lambda$.

**(2)** Since $w \in S_{\mathbb{Z}_+}$, every left descent $s_k w < w$ has $k \ge 1$ ($k+1$ precedes $k$ in $w$).
- Suppose $k \notin \{\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_m\}$, and let $i \ge 1$ be minimal with $\lambda_i < k$ ($\lambda_0 := \infty$). For every $a < i$, $\lambda_a \ge k+1$, so by ($*$) $k+1$ appears after $a$. If $k+1$ appeared at some $a \ge i$ before $k$, then $k \in \{w(z) : z > a,\, w(z) < w(a) = k+1\} = \{1, \dots, \lambda_a\}$ by ($*$), contradicting $\lambda_a \le k-1$. Hence $k$ precedes $k+1$, and $s_k w > w$.
- Conversely, suppose $i \in \eta(\lambda)$, so $\lambda_{i-1} > \lambda_i = \dots = \lambda_t = k > \lambda_{t+1}$ for $t := \max\{j : \lambda_j = k\} \ge i$. By ($*$), $k+1$ appears after $i-1$ (as $\lambda_{i-1} \ge k+1$) while $\{1, \dots, k\}$ all appear after $t$ (as $\lambda_t = k$). In particular $w(i) \ge k+1$; if $w(i) > k+1$, then $k+1$ would lie in $\{w(z) : z > i,\, w(z) < w(i)\} = \{1, \dots, k\}$, impossible. Thus $w(i) = k+1$, while $k = w(q)$ for some $q > t \ge i$. Hence $k+1$ precedes $k$, so $s_k w < w$.

**(3)** Fix $i \in \eta(\lambda)$ with $k = \lambda_i \ge 1$ and $t = \max\{j : \lambda_j = k\} \ge i$. Writing $\mu := \lambda - \delta_t$, the shortest permutation sorting $\mu$ to $\lambda - \delta_i$ is $s_i s_{i+1} \cdots s_{t-1}$, so
$$\kappa_{\lambda - \delta_i} = \pi_i \pi_{i+1} \cdots \pi_{t-1}\!\left(x_1^{\lambda_1}\cdots x_{i-1}^{\lambda_{i-1}} (x_i \cdots x_t)^{k-1} (x_i \cdots x_{t-1}) x_{t+1}^{\lambda_{t+1}} \cdots x_m^{\lambda_m}\right) = x_1^{\lambda_1}\cdots x_{i-1}^{\lambda_{i-1}} (x_i \cdots x_t)^{k-1} e_{t-i}(x_i,\dots,x_t)\, x_{t+1}^{\lambda_{t+1}} \cdots x_m^{\lambda_m},$$
since $(x_i \cdots x_t)^{k-1}$ is symmetric in $x_i, \dots, x_t$ and $\pi_i \cdots \pi_{t-1}(x_i \cdots x_{t-1}) = e_{t-i}(x_i, \dots, x_t)$ (by induction from $j = t - 1$ down to $j = i$: setting $S = \{x_{j+2}, \dots, x_t\}$ and $r = t - 1 - j$ with $e_r(x_{j+1}, S) = x_{j+1} e_{r-1}(S) + e_r(S)$, we have $\pi_j(x_j e_r(x_{j+1}, S)) = x_j x_{j+1} e_{r-1}(S) + (x_j + x_{j+1}) e_r(S) = e_{r+1}(x_j, x_{j+1}, S)$).

We now prove $\mathfrak{S}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = x_1^{\lambda_1}\cdots x_{i-1}^{\lambda_{i-1}} (x_i \cdots x_t)^{k-1} e_{t-i}(x_i,\dots,x_t)\, x_{t+1}^{\lambda_{t+1}} \cdots x_m^{\lambda_m}$ by induction on $|\lambda| \ge 1$ using the transition formula for $u := s_k w(\lambda)$ (which swaps $w(i) = k+1$ with $w(q) = k$, $q > t$).
- **Case $i = t = m$:** Since $\lambda_m = k$ and $\lambda_{m+1} = 0$, ($*$) gives $w(m+1..m+k) = (1, 2, \dots, k)$, so $q = m+k$. Swapping $w(m) = k+1$ with $w(m+k) = k$ replaces $k+1$ at $m$ by $k$ and changes $1, \dots, k-1, k$ at $m+1..m+k$ to $1, \dots, k-1, k+1$, giving $\mathrm{code}(u) = \lambda - \delta_m$. As $\lambda - \delta_m$ is weakly decreasing, part (1) gives $u = w(\lambda - \delta_m)$ and $\mathfrak{S}_u = x^{\lambda - \delta_m} = \kappa_{\lambda - \delta_m}$.
- **Otherwise ($t < m$ or $i < t = m$):** In $w$, $w(m+1) < w(m+2) < \dots$ and $w(m) > w(m+1)$. Let $s_w = m + \lambda_m = \max\{j > m : w(j) < w(m)\}$, so $w(\lambda) t_{m,s_w} = w(\lambda - \delta_m)$ by (1), and set $s_u := \max\{j > m : u(j) < u(m)\}$.
  - If $q \le m$, the tail $w(m+1) < w(m+2) < \dots$ is unchanged in $u$, and either $q < m$ (so $u(m) = w(m) > u(m+1)$) or $q = m$ (so $w(m) = k$ and $u(m) = k+1 > w(m) > u(m+1)$, with no $j > m$ taking value $k$ or $k+1$). Thus $r = m$ is still the last descent of $u$ and $s_u = s_w$.
  - If $i < t = m$, then $\lambda_m = k$, $w(m+1..m+k) = (1, \dots, k)$, and $q = m+k = s_w$; since $w(i) = k+1$ with $i < m$, we have $w(m) \ge k+2$ and $w(m+k+1) \ge k+2$. Replacing $w(m+k) = k$ by $k+1$ gives $u(m+1..m+k) = (1, \dots, k-1, k+1)$ with $u(m+k) = k+1 < w(m+k+1) = u(m+k+1)$, preserving $u(m+1) < u(m+2) < \dots$, while $u(m) = w(m) \ge k+2 > k+1 = u(m+k) \ge u(m+1)$ gives $r = m$ as the last descent of $u$ and $s_u = m+k = s_w$.
  - If $t < m$ and $q > m$, then $\lambda_m < k$, so ($*$) for $r = m$ forces $w(m) < k$ (otherwise $k \in \{1, \dots, \lambda_m\}$), giving $w(m+1) < w(m) < k = w(q)$ and hence $q \ge m+2$; moreover $w(q+1) \ge k+2$ (since $w(i) = k+1$ with $i \le t < m < q$). Replacing $k$ by $k+1$ at $q \ge m+2$ thus preserves $u(m+1) < u(m+2) < \dots$ and $u(m) = w(m) > u(m+1)$ (so $r = m$ is the last descent of $u$), and $s_u = s_w$ since $u(q) = k+1 > w(m) = u(m)$.

  In all subcases, $r = m$ is the last descent of $u$ and $s_u = s_w$, so $u' := u t_{m,s_u} = s_k(w(\lambda) t_{m,s_w}) = s_k w(\lambda - \delta_m)$.
  - If $t = m$ (and $i < m$): Here $w(m) \ge k+2$, $q = m+k = s_u$, $u'(m) = k+1$, $u'(i) = k$, and for all $a \in \{1,\dots,m-1\} \setminus \{i\}$, $\lambda_a \ge k \implies u'(a) = w(a) > k+1$ (since $k+1 = w(i)$ was at $i$ and $1,\dots,k$ lie after $m$ by ($*$)). Thus $a = i$ is the unique index $< m$ with $u' t_{a,m} \gtrdot u'$, and $u' t_{i,m}$ puts $k+1$ back at $i$, $k$ at $m$, and $w(m)$ at $m+k$, which equals $w(\lambda - \delta_m)$. Transition and the induction hypothesis give
    $$\mathfrak{S}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = x_m \mathfrak{S}_{s_k w(\lambda - \delta_m)} + \mathfrak{S}_{w(\lambda - \delta_m)} = x_1^{\lambda_1}\cdots x_{i-1}^{\lambda_{i-1}}(x_i\cdots x_m)^{k-1}\bigl(x_m e_{m-1-i}(x_i,\dots,x_{m-1}) + e_{m-i}(x_i,\dots,x_{m-1})\bigr) = \kappa_{\lambda - \delta_i}.$$
  - If $t < m$: Here $\lambda_m < k$. Writing $w' := w(\lambda - \delta_m)$, since $w$ avoids $132$, we have $w'(a) > w'(m)$ for all $a < m$ (as $w'(m) = w(s_w) < w(m)$, and $w(a) < w(s_w) < w(m)$ would be a $132$-pattern). Since $u' = s_k w'$ only swaps values $k$ and $k+1$, an inequality $u'(a) < u'(m)$ for $a < m$ could only arise if $w'(a) = k+1$ and $w'(m) = k$, i.e., $w(s_w) = k$ and $q = s_w > m$. But as shown above, $q > m$ and $\lambda_m < k$ force $w(s_w) < w(m) < k$, contradiction! Hence $u'(a) > u'(m)$ for all $a < m$, so transition gives zero extra terms:
    $$\mathfrak{S}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = x_m \mathfrak{S}_{s_k w(\lambda - \delta_m)} = \kappa_{\lambda - \delta_i}$$
    by the induction hypothesis (with $\lambda_m$ decremented by $1$; when $\lambda_m = 1$, $s_k w(\lambda - \delta_m)$ retains the same left descent $s_k$).

Finally, since $\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = \sum_\beta \overline{\kappa}_\beta$ has the exact same expansion as $\mathfrak{S}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = \sum_\beta \kappa_\beta$ (Remark 8.4), $\mathfrak{S}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = \kappa_{\lambda - \delta_i}$ implies $\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = \overline{\kappa}_{\lambda - \delta_i}$ in $\overline{R}$. Note also that if $\lambda_i = 1$ with $i = t = m$, $s_1 w(\lambda) = w(\lambda - \delta_m)$ directly, and Remark 8.4 still yields $\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_1 w(\lambda)} = \overline{\kappa}_{\lambda - \delta_m}$.

**(4)** Applying Eq. `eq:nabla-xi-olR-action` to $\overline{\kappa}_\lambda = \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{w(\lambda)}$ and substituting (2) and (3) gives
$$\xi(\overline{\kappa}_\lambda) = \sum_{k : s_k w(\lambda) < w(\lambda)} \overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{s_k w(\lambda)} = \sum_{i \in \eta(\lambda)} \overline{\kappa}_{\lambda - \delta_i}. \quad \square$$

## 4. Induction to Arbitrary Positive Compositions

Let $\alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m, 0, \dots)$ be any positive composition, and let $\lambda$ be the decreasing partition obtained by sorting $(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m)$. Any positive composition $\alpha$ is reached from $\lambda$ by a sequence of adjacent transpositions $s_j$ ($1 \le j \le m - 1$) satisfying $\beta_j > \beta_{j+1}$, so by Lemma 3(4) and Lemma 2 it suffices to prove: *if the conjecture holds for $\beta$ and $\beta_j > \beta_{j+1}$, then it holds for $\beta' := s_j \beta$.*

By Lemmas 1 and 2 and the induction hypothesis for $\beta$:
$$\xi(\overline{\kappa}_{s_j \beta}) = \xi(\pi_j \overline{\kappa}_\beta) = \pi_j(\xi(\overline{\kappa}_\beta)) = \sum_{\ell \in \eta(\beta)} \pi_j(\overline{\kappa}_{\beta - \delta_\ell}).$$
Set $A := \beta_j$, $B := \beta_{j+1}$ ($A > B \ge 0$), and $S_{<j} := \{0\} \cup \{\beta_1, \dots, \beta_{j-1}\}$. Compare $\ell \in \eta(\beta)$ with $\ell \in \eta(\beta')$ (note $\beta'_j = B$, $\beta'_{j+1} = A$, and $\beta'_r = \beta_r$ for $r \notin \{j, j+1\}$):
1. **$\ell < j$:** $\ell \in \eta(\beta) \iff \ell \in \eta(\beta')$, and $(\beta - \delta_\ell)_j = A > B = (\beta - \delta_\ell)_{j+1}$, so Lemma 2 gives $\pi_j(\overline{\kappa}_{\beta - \delta_\ell}) = \overline{\kappa}_{\beta' - \delta_\ell}$.
2. **$\ell > j + 1$:** The set of values at indices $< \ell$ is identical in $\beta$ and $\beta'$, so $\ell \in \eta(\beta) \iff \ell \in \eta(\beta')$, and again $(\beta - \delta_\ell)_j = A > B = (\beta - \delta_\ell)_{j+1}$, giving $\pi_j(\overline{\kappa}_{\beta - \delta_\ell}) = \overline{\kappa}_{\beta' - \delta_\ell}$.
3. **$\ell \in \{j, j + 1\}$:** Checking all four possibilities for membership of $A, B$ in $S_{<j}$:
   - *$A \notin S_{<j},\, B \notin S_{<j}$:* Both $j, j+1$ lie in $\eta(\beta)$ and in $\eta(\beta')$. For $\ell = j + 1$, $(\beta - \delta_{j+1})_j = A > B - 1 = (\beta - \delta_{j+1})_{j+1}$, so Lemma 2 gives $\pi_j(\overline{\kappa}_{\beta - \delta_{j+1}}) = \overline{\kappa}_{\beta' - \delta_j}$. For $\ell = j$, $(\beta - \delta_j)_j = A - 1 \ge B = (\beta - \delta_j)_{j+1}$; whether $A - 1 > B$ or $A - 1 = B$, Lemma 2 gives $\pi_j(\overline{\kappa}_{\beta - \delta_j}) = \overline{\kappa}_{\beta' - \delta_{j+1}}$.
   - *$A \in S_{<j},\, B \in S_{<j}$:* Neither $j$ nor $j+1$ lies in $\eta(\beta)$ or $\eta(\beta')$; both contributions are $0$.
   - *$A \notin S_{<j},\, B \in S_{<j}$:* $\eta(\beta) \cap \{j, j+1\} = \{j\}$ while $\eta(\beta') \cap \{j, j+1\} = \{j+1\}$. Since $(\beta - \delta_j)_j = A - 1 \ge B = (\beta - \delta_j)_{j+1}$, Lemma 2 gives $\pi_j(\overline{\kappa}_{\beta - \delta_j}) = \overline{\kappa}_{\beta' - \delta_{j+1}}$.
   - *$A \in S_{<j},\, B \notin S_{<j}$:* $\eta(\beta) \cap \{j, j+1\} = \{j+1\}$ while $\eta(\beta') \cap \{j, j+1\} = \{j\}$. Since $(\beta - \delta_{j+1})_j = A > B - 1 = (\beta - \delta_{j+1})_{j+1}$, Lemma 2 gives $\pi_j(\overline{\kappa}_{\beta - \delta_{j+1}}) = \overline{\kappa}_{\beta' - \delta_j}$.

In all cases, $\sum_{\ell \in \eta(\beta)} \pi_j(\overline{\kappa}_{\beta - \delta_\ell}) = \sum_{\ell \in \eta(\beta')} \overline{\kappa}_{\beta' - \delta_\ell}$, completing the induction and the proof of Conjecture 8.5 (`v2`; Conjecture 7.5 in `v1`). $\blacksquare$
